The Langlands program

A vast network of conjectures predicting that every arithmetic-geometric L-function — those built from Galois representations, from elliptic curves, from étale cohomology of arithmetic varieties — agrees with an analytic L-function built from automorphic representations of $\GL_n$. Class field theory is the abelian case ($n = 1$); modularity of elliptic curves is the proven non-abelian case for $n = 2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$; Sato–Tate is its symmetric-power consequence. The general statement remains conjectural and is the organising principle of half of modern number theory. Robert Langlands sketched the program in a 1967 letter to André Weil; the predicted reciprocity laws would unify class field theory, Artin reciprocity, modularity, and the Riemann hypothesis under a single Tannakian framework.

1. The philosophy: arithmetic on one side, analytic on the other

The Langlands program organises itself around two columns of objects, each with its own L-function machinery, and the conjecture that every L-function on the left equals an L-function on the right.

What "matches" means. Every $\rho$ on the left has an L-function — for each unramified prime $p$, the local factor is

$$L_p(\rho, s) = \det\!\bigl(I_n - \rho(\Frob_p) p^{-s}\bigr)^{-1},$$

and we form the global Euler product $L(\rho, s) = \prod_p L_p(\rho, s)$. Every $\pi$ on the right also has an L-function (Godement-Jacquet), built from the local Hecke eigenvalues at each unramified place. The Langlands correspondence asserts: for every $\rho$ there is a unique $\pi$ with $L(\rho, s) = L(\pi, s)$ at every place, and conversely — a bijection between (irreducible $n$-dim) Galois reps and (cuspidal automorphic) representations of $\GL_n$, mediated by L-functions.

The arithmetic side is rigid (algebraic, with prime-by-prime data). The analytic side is flexible (built from harmonic analysis on adelic groups, with a functional equation by general principles). The Langlands philosophy is that arithmetic rigidity = analytic flexibility; they are two views of the same underlying object, and L-functions are the testable shadow.

2. L-functions: the bridge

L-functions sit in the centre of the Langlands picture because they are the only invariants that both sides produce in the same form. From a Galois representation $\rho$ over $\mathbb{Q}$:

$$L(\rho, s) = \prod_p \det\!\bigl(I_n - \rho(\Frob_p)\, p^{-s} \big|\, V_\ell^{I_p}\bigr)^{-1}, \qquad \Re(s) \gg 0.$$

(Take inertia-invariants $V_\ell^{I_p}$ at ramified primes; characteristic polynomial of geometric Frobenius elsewhere.) From an automorphic representation $\pi = \otimes_v \pi_v$ of $\GL_n(\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q})$:

$$L(\pi, s) = \prod_p L(\pi_p, s),$$

where each $L(\pi_p, s)$ is built from the Satake parameters of $\pi_p$ — eigenvalues of a spherical Hecke operator at $p$. Two qualitative facts make L-functions the right invariant for the matching:

  • Functional equation. Both sides predict $L(s)$ to extend to a meromorphic function on $\mathbb{C}$ and satisfy $\Lambda(s) = \varepsilon \cdot \Lambda(1-s)$ for a completed L-function $\Lambda$ that includes archimedean Gamma factors. On the automorphic side this is automatic from the transformation law of $\pi$ (Tate's thesis pattern); on the Galois side it is conjectural until you can match $\rho$ to some $\pi$.
  • Strong multiplicity one. An automorphic representation of $\GL_n$ is determined by its L-function — knowing the Euler factors at almost all places pins $\pi$ uniquely (Jacquet-Shalika). So the L-function is a complete invariant of an automorphic rep, and equality of L-functions on both sides really does identify Galois reps with automorphic reps.

3. Local Langlands for $\GL_n$

The program splits prime-by-prime. Local Langlands at a finite place $v$ of $\mathbb{Q}$ predicts a bijection between

  • $n$-dimensional Frobenius-semisimple Weil-Deligne representations of $W_{\mathbb{Q}_v}$ (the Weil group of the local field $\mathbb{Q}_v$, with a monodromy operator $N$ encoding ramification), and
  • irreducible smooth admissible representations of $\GL_n(\mathbb{Q}_v)$.

Concretely: a $W$-D representation packages $(\rho, N)$ with $\rho\colon W_{\mathbb{Q}_v} \to \GL_n(\mathbb{C})$ semisimple and $N$ a nilpotent matrix satisfying $\rho(\sigma) N \rho(\sigma)^{-1} = q^{|\sigma|} N$. On the automorphic side, smooth admissible reps of $\GL_n(\mathbb{Q}_v)$ are infinite-dimensional vector spaces with a continuous $\GL_n$-action; the irreducible ones are classified (Bernstein-Zelevinsky) into supercuspidal, principal, and Steinberg pieces.

Status. Local Langlands for $\GL_n$ over a $p$-adic field is proven (Harris-Taylor, Henniart, 2001). Over an archimedean local field ($\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$), it is the Langlands classification of admissible representations of real reductive groups (Langlands, 1973). For groups other than $\GL_n$ — orthogonal, symplectic, exceptional — local Langlands is partially proven and substantially harder.

4. Global Langlands for $\GL_n$

The global statement glues local Langlands across all places. Global Langlands for $\GL_n$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ predicts a bijection between

  • continuous, irreducible, $n$-dimensional Galois representations $\rho\colon \Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}) \to \GL_n(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ that are unramified outside a finite set of primes, almost everywhere de Rham, and "geometric" (cuts of cohomology of varieties), and
  • cuspidal automorphic representations $\pi$ of $\GL_n(\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q})$ that are "algebraic" (whose archimedean component has integral Hodge-Tate weights).

The bijection sends $\rho \mapsto \pi$ such that the local Langlands correspondence at each finite place $v$ takes $\rho|_{W_{\mathbb{Q}_v}}$ to $\pi_v$. Equivalently: $L(\rho, s) = L(\pi, s)$ for all $s$.

Known cases.

  • $n = 1$: class field theory (proven). Galois reps = continuous characters $\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}) \to \mathbb{C}^\times$; automorphic reps of $\GL_1(\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q}) = \mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q}^\times$ = Hecke characters.
  • $n = 2$: modularity of elliptic curves (Wiles, Taylor-Wiles, BCDT — proven for elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}$); plus modularity of most 2-dimensional Galois representations of regular weight (Khare-Wintenberger for Serre's conjecture). The general $n=2$ case (potentially modular for all suitable Galois reps) remains active research.
  • $n \geq 3$: only fragmentary cases. Symmetric-power liftings $\Sym^k$ of $\GL_2$-reps to $\GL_{k+1}$-reps are known up to $k = 4$ (Kim-Shahidi); Sato-Tate (Taylor et al.) is the corresponding consequence.

5. Class field theory as Langlands for $\GL_1$

The abelian case is fully proven and worth seeing as the prototype. Class field theory says: continuous characters of the Galois group $\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})^{\mathrm{ab}}$ correspond bijectively to continuous characters of the idèle class group $\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q}^\times / \mathbb{Q}^\times$.

From the Langlands viewpoint:

  • $\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is the group of $\mathbb{C}^\times$-valued (i.e. $1$-dim) Galois reps. So Galois reps for $n = 1$ are abelianised characters.
  • $\GL_1(\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q}) = \mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q}^\times$. Cuspidal automorphic representations of this group are continuous characters $\chi\colon \mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q}^\times / \mathbb{Q}^\times \to \mathbb{C}^\times$ — i.e. Hecke characters.
  • The Langlands correspondence between them is exactly Artin's reciprocity isomorphism: $\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})^{\mathrm{ab}} \cong \widehat{\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q}^\times / \mathbb{Q}^\times}$ (where $\widehat{}$ is the Pontryagin dual, identifying the discrete group with its character group).

And the L-function matching: an Artin L-function $L(\chi, s)$ for an abelian Galois character $\chi$ equals the Hecke L-function $L(\chi^{\mathrm{Hecke}}, s)$ for the corresponding Hecke character. This is what Artin proved in the 1920s — long before the term "Langlands program" existed.

Slogan. Class field theory is Langlands for $\GL_1$. Modularity is Langlands for $\GL_2$ (over $\mathbb{Q}$, semistable case). The general program is Langlands for $\GL_n$ — and beyond, with the Langlands group $L_F$ as the predicted universal symmetry.

6. Modularity: the proven $\GL_2$ case

The Modularity Theorem says: every elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{Q}$ is associated to a weight-$2$ cusp form $f \in S_2(\Gamma_0(N))$ where $N$ is the conductor of $E$, with

$$L(E, s) = L(f, s).$$

Wiles (1995, semistable case), Taylor-Wiles (the patching that closed the 1993 gap), Breuil-Conrad-Diamond-Taylor (general case, 2001) proved the theorem.

From the Langlands viewpoint, this is the case $n = 2$ of global Langlands over $\mathbb{Q}$ for the specific class of $2$-dimensional Galois representations $\rho_{E,\ell}\colon \Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}) \to \GL_2(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ coming from $\ell$-adic Tate modules of elliptic curves. The corresponding automorphic representation is the cusp form $f$ — more precisely, the cuspidal automorphic representation $\pi_f$ of $\GL_2(\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q})$ that $f$ generates via the classical-to-adelic dictionary.

Beyond elliptic curves. Khare-Wintenberger (2009) proved Serre's conjecture: every odd, irreducible, mod-$\ell$ representation $\bar\rho\colon \Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}) \to \GL_2(\mathbb{F}_\ell)$ is modular — comes from a modular form. This is the mod-$\ell$ analogue of Langlands for $\GL_2$ and a crucial ingredient in modern proofs of modularity beyond the elliptic-curve case.

The deformation-theoretic technology (Mazur deformation rings, Taylor-Wiles patching, the $R = T$ identity between universal deformation rings and Hecke algebras) is the engine that powers the proven cases. Each new "potential modularity" or "automorphy lifting" theorem is an increment along the same line.

7. Functoriality and the Langlands group

Beyond bijections, Langlands predicted a vast network of functorial liftings: given a continuous homomorphism $\rho\colon {}^L H \to {}^L G$ between Langlands dual groups, every automorphic representation $\pi$ of $H(\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q})$ should lift to one of $G(\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q})$ in a way compatible with L-functions.

Concrete instances:

  • Symmetric power lifting. For $\pi$ on $\GL_2(\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q})$, its $k$-th symmetric power $\Sym^k \pi$ should be an automorphic representation of $\GL_{k+1}(\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q})$ with $L(\Sym^k \pi, s) = L(\Sym^k \rho_\pi, s)$. Known unconditionally for $k \le 4$ (Kim-Shahidi 2002, Kim 2003); extended in the modular case (Newton-Thorne 2020 for all $k$).
  • Base change. An automorphic rep on $\GL_n(\mathbb{A}_F)$ for a number field $F$ lifts to one on $\GL_n(\mathbb{A}_E)$ for cyclic extensions $E/F$. Established in many cases (Arthur-Clozel for $\GL_n$).
  • Endoscopic transfer. Liftings between $G$ and a related group $H$ ("endoscopic group"). Required for the trace-formula approach to functoriality. Largely proven for classical groups (Arthur, 2013).

The conjectural Langlands group $L_F$. Langlands proposed that for each global field $F$ there should be a topological group $L_F$ — the "Langlands group" — whose continuous $n$-dimensional representations are exactly the cuspidal automorphic representations of $\GL_n(\mathbb{A}_F)$. The group $L_F$ is conjectural — it is not known to exist as a literal topological group — but its representations would unify everything: $L_F^{\mathrm{ab}}$ = idèle class group; the étale-realisation quotient of the motivic Galois group sits inside $L_F$; reciprocity between $L_F$-reps and Galois reps is the global Langlands correspondence.

Tannakian frame. The Langlands group $L_F$ is to automorphic representations what the motivic Galois group is to motives: a Tannakian fundamental group whose existence and structure encode the symmetries of the entire automorphic / motivic apparatus. The compatibility between the two — the conjectural map $G_{\mathrm{mot}}(F) \to L_F^{\mathrm{geom}}$ — is the deepest layer of the Langlands philosophy.

8. Universal reciprocity — the philosophy realised

The Langlands program is conjecturally the unique unification of:

  • Class field theory — the abelian case ($n = 1$), proven by Artin in the 1920s.
  • Modularity of elliptic curves — the $n = 2$ case for one specific class of Galois reps, proven by Wiles + Taylor-Wiles + BCDT (1995-2001).
  • The Sato-Tate conjecture — the corresponding statement for $\Sym^k \rho_E$, proven for elliptic curves over totally real fields (Taylor et al., 2008+).
  • The Hodge / Tate / motivic conjectures — the predicted compatibility with the motivic Galois group.
  • The Riemann hypothesis (and its generalisations) — through the Galois-rep side: GRH for L-functions of automorphic forms is part of the Langlands package.

The framework is also what extends the analogy between number theory and geometry. Geometric Langlands (Drinfeld, Beilinson, Mukai) replaces the field $\mathbb{Q}$ with the function field $\mathbb{F}_q(C)$ of a curve $C$, and the resulting correspondence sends $\ell$-adic local systems on $C$ to D-modules on $\mathrm{Bun}_G(C)$. This geometric mirror is fully proven for $\GL_2$ (Drinfeld) and substantially proven for general groups — the geometric side is in some ways the most accessible window into the program.

Status (number-theoretic side). Most of the program remains conjectural. Local Langlands for $\GL_n$ over $p$-adic fields is proven (Harris-Taylor, Henniart 2001). Global Langlands is proven for $n = 1$ (CFT), substantially for $n = 2$ (modular elliptic curves + Serre's conjecture), and only fragmentarily for $n \ge 3$. The Langlands group $L_F$ does not provably exist; the predicted compatibilities with the motivic Galois group are open. This is the largest open program in modern number theory, and most of what can be proven about specific arithmetic objects (modularity, Sato-Tate, L-function meromorphic continuation) is a partial increment of the full picture.

And yet the framework already organises an enormous chunk of arithmetic. Every L-function of arithmetic significance — Riemann zeta, Dirichlet, Hecke, Artin, Hasse-Weil, Sato-Tate symmetric powers — fits inside the picture, and the matching with automorphic L-functions is the testable evidence at every step.

Connections

The Langlands program touches every active frontier of modern arithmetic. The reciprocity column is the symmetry side of the motivic Galois group; the L-function bridge is what concretely couples Galois data to harmonic analysis on adelic groups; functoriality predicts a network of liftings that is the engine of every non-trivial automorphy result.

  • Galois representations — the arithmetic side of the correspondence; supplies the candidate Galois reps for every Langlands-style theorem.
  • Automorphic forms (adelic) — the analytic side; the bridge from classical modular forms to automorphic representations of $\GL_n(\mathbb{A})$.
  • L-functions — the matching invariant; both sides produce L-functions and the program asserts they agree.
  • Modularity & FLT — the proven $n=2$ case over $\mathbb{Q}$; deformation-theoretic technology underwriting it.
  • Motives — the conjectural cousin; $G_{\mathrm{mot}}(\mathbb{Q})$ sits beside $L_\mathbb{Q}$ in the picture, predicted to be compatible via realization functors.
  • Sato-Tate — the equidistribution consequence of symmetric-power Langlands for elliptic curves.
  • Class field theory — the abelian Langlands case; the historical prototype.
