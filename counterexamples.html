Counterexamples gallery

A plausible implication, and the object that kills it. Each card states a tempting claim, names the single hypothesis that has to break, and links to the page where the notebook builds the counterexample. In the spirit of Counterexamples in Analysis / Topology — the fastest way to learn what a theorem really needs is to see what happens when you drop one assumption. A green check marks counterexamples whose home concept you've mastered.

Some objects recur across sections — the line with two origins is the topologists' "not-Hausdorff" example and the geometers' "not-separated" scheme; the Cantor set fails three different theorems on three different pages. That's the point: one pathology refutes many tidy statements.

Statements are deliberately informal — follow the links for the precise hypotheses.
