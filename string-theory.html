String theory

A relativistic string sweeps out a 2D world-sheet; quantizing it forces a critical spacetime dimension, and curling six of those dimensions into a Calabi–Yau 3-fold connects 10D physics to 4D effective theory. Dualities — T-duality, mirror symmetry, AdS/CFT — have produced mathematics that survives independently of whether nature is stringy.

Honest framing. String theory is currently unverified as a description of nature: no experiment has detected a stringy signature, and no preferred 4D vacuum has been singled out from the landscape. What this page tracks is the mathematical structure — actions, quantizations, dualities, theorems — and the substantial body of pure math the framework has fed back into geometry, number theory, and representation theory. Treat the physics claims here as conditional; treat the math output (mirror symmetry, moonshine, Langlands) as established.

1. The world-sheet action

A point particle traces out a 1D world-line; its action is proportional to the proper length of that line. Replace the particle by a 1-dimensional string and you get a 2D world-sheet $\Sigma$ embedded in $D$-dimensional spacetime via $X^\mu(\tau,\sigma)$, $\mu = 0,\dots,D-1$. The natural geometric action is the Nambu–Goto area:

$S_{NG} = -T\int_\Sigma \sqrt{-\det h_{ab}}\,d^2\sigma,\qquad h_{ab} = \partial_a X^\mu\, \partial_b X_\mu$

where $T = 1/(2\pi\alpha')$ is the string tension and $h_{ab}$ is the induced (pullback) metric on $\Sigma$. The square root makes quantization awkward. Polyakov's trick: introduce an independent world-sheet metric $h_{ab}(\tau,\sigma)$ as an auxiliary field and write

$S_P = -\dfrac{T}{2}\int_\Sigma \sqrt{-h}\, h^{ab}\, \partial_a X^\mu\, \partial_b X_\mu\, d^2\sigma.$

Varying $h^{ab}$ algebraically and substituting back recovers $S_{NG}$, so the two actions are classically equivalent. But $S_P$ is polynomial in the $X^\mu$ — the gauge-fixed theory is a free 2D field theory. The action has three local symmetries:

Three local parameters can fix the three components of $h_{ab}$. The standard choice is conformal gauge $h_{ab} = \eta_{ab} = \mathrm{diag}(-1,+1)$. In that gauge the equations of motion are simply $\partial_a\partial^a X^\mu = 0$ — free 2D wave equations — supplemented by the constraints $T_{ab} = 0$ that survive as residual gauge fixing.

2. Conformal field theory on the world-sheet

After fixing conformal gauge $h_{ab} = \eta_{ab}$, the Polyakov action becomes a free 2D field theory of $D$ scalar fields $X^\mu(\tau,\sigma)$. But the gauge fixing leaves a residual symmetry: any conformal transformation of the world-sheet (any holomorphic + antiholomorphic reparametrization of $z = \sigma^1 + i\sigma^2$) preserves the gauge condition up to a Weyl rescaling. The world-sheet theory is thus a conformal field theory.

The key data: the holomorphic stress tensor $T(z)$ generates conformal transformations. Mode-expand $T(z) = \sum_n L_n\, z^{-n-2}$. The modes $L_n$ satisfy the Virasoro algebra:

$[L_m, L_n] = (m-n)\,L_{m+n} + \dfrac{c}{12}\,(m^3 - m)\,\delta_{m+n,0}.$

The number $c$ is the central charge — the conformal anomaly, measuring how the classical Weyl symmetry fails to survive quantization. Each free boson $X^\mu$ contributes $c = 1$. The reparametrization ghosts (from gauge-fixing $h_{ab}$) contribute $c_{gh} = -26$. Anomaly cancellation $D + (-26) = 0$ forces $D = 26$ for the bosonic string — a derivation we revisit in §3.

Primary fields and OPE. An operator $\phi(z)$ of conformal weight $h$ has the OPE $T(z)\phi(w) \sim \frac{h\,\phi(w)}{(z-w)^2} + \frac{\partial\phi(w)}{z-w}$ — the leading singular pieces of the product as $z\to w$. The state $|\phi\rangle = \phi(0)|0\rangle$ is then an $L_0$ eigenstate with eigenvalue $h$. State-operator correspondence is the bridge between operator language (CFT on the plane) and Hilbert-space language (CFT on the cylinder).

The subalgebra spanned by $L_{-1}, L_0, L_1$ closes without central extension and integrates to the global Möbius group $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ acting on the boundary of the upper half-plane. This is the part of the conformal symmetry that is non-anomalous regardless of $c$.

String theory · Bosonic strings and the critical dimension

3. Bosonic strings and the critical dimension $D=26$

Now quantize. In light-cone gauge one fixes the residual conformal symmetry by setting $X^+(\tau,\sigma) = x^+ + p^+\tau$. The constraints $T_{ab} = 0$ then determine the longitudinal $X^-$ in terms of the $D-2$ transverse oscillators $X^i$ ($i = 1,\dots,D-2$). The mass spectrum of the open string becomes

$\alpha'\, M^2 = N - a,\qquad N = \sum_{n\ge 1}\sum_i \alpha^i_{-n}\alpha^i_n,\qquad a = \dfrac{D-2}{24}.$

The constant $a$ is the normal-ordering shift: the zero-point energy of the $D-2$ transverse oscillator towers, formally $\sum_{n\ge 1} n = -1/12$ per direction (by zeta regularization), summed over $D-2$ transverse directions and divided by $2$. Lorentz invariance — visible in light-cone gauge through closure of the $J^{i-}$ commutators — forces $a = 1$, hence:

$\dfrac{D-2}{24} = 1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D = 26.$

This is one of the most striking results in physics: an apparently free choice (spacetime dimension) is fixed by the consistency of the quantum theory. The same number reappears via BRST anomaly cancellation: $c_{\mathrm{matter}} + c_{\mathrm{ghost}} = D + (-26) = 0$.

Tachyon. At $N = 0$ (the ground state) we get $\alpha' M^2 = -1 < 0$ — the bosonic string ground state has imaginary mass. Tachyons signal that we have expanded around an unstable vacuum. The first excited level $N = 1$ gives $M^2 = 0$: massless states (a photon for the open string, the graviton for the closed string). The bosonic string is mathematically the cleanest version of the theory but physically untenable.

String theory · Superstrings and supersymmetry

4. Superstrings and the critical dimension $D=10$

Adding world-sheet fermions $\psi^\mu(\tau,\sigma)$ partnered to each $X^\mu$, plus a local supersymmetry on the world-sheet, produces the RNS superstring. The boson-fermion match upgrades the matter central charge to $c_{\mathrm{matter}} = D + D/2 = 3D/2$; super-conformal ghosts contribute $c_{\mathrm{ghost}} = -15$. Cancellation gives:

$\dfrac{3D}{2} - 15 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad D = 10.$

The fermions $\psi^\mu$ admit two boundary conditions on the closed string: Ramond (R, periodic, gives spacetime fermions) and Neveu–Schwarz (NS, antiperiodic, gives spacetime bosons). The NS sector still has a tachyonic ground state — but the GSO projection $(-1)^F = +1$ keeps only states with even fermion number, removing it. After GSO the lightest NS state is the massless gauge boson / graviton.

5. Compactification and Calabi–Yau manifolds

The superstring lives in 10 spacetime dimensions; we observe four. The Kaluza–Klein resolution: assume spacetime is $\mathbb{R}^{1,3} \times X$, where $X$ is a small compact 6-manifold. Six is exactly $10 - 4$. The geometry of $X$ then determines the 4D effective theory: its isometries become 4D gauge symmetries, its harmonic forms become 4D scalar moduli, and its size sets a Kaluza–Klein scale.

To preserve $\mathcal{N}=1$ supersymmetry in 4D — the minimal amount that makes the theory both physically interesting and mathematically tractable — count covariantly constant spinors on $X$. A 10D Majorana–Weyl supercharge restricted to $\mathbb{R}^{1,3}\times X$ decomposes as a sum over the holonomy representations of $X$. Generic $\mathrm{SO}(6)$ holonomy preserves no spinor; $\mathrm{SU}(3) \subset \mathrm{SO}(6)$ holonomy preserves exactly one. By Berger's classification + Yau's theorem (the proof of the Calabi conjecture), $\mathrm{SU}(3)$-holonomy compact Kähler 6-manifolds are exactly Calabi–Yau 3-folds: compact Kähler with vanishing first Chern class $c_1(X) = 0$, equivalently with trivial canonical bundle, equivalently admitting a global non-vanishing holomorphic 3-form $\Omega$.

The full Hodge diamond of a CY 3-fold: $$\begin{array}{ccccccc} & & & 1 & & & \\ & & 0 & & 0 & & \\ & 0 & & h^{1,1} & & 0 & \\ 1 & & h^{2,1} & & h^{2,1} & & 1 \\ & 0 & & h^{1,1} & & 0 & \\ & & 0 & & 0 & & \\ & & & 1 & & & \end{array}$$ The two non-trivial entries are $h^{1,1}(X)$ (Kähler / size moduli) and $h^{2,1}(X)$ (complex-structure / shape moduli). For the quintic 3-fold $\{f_5(x_0,\dots,x_4) = 0\}\subset \mathbb{P}^4$, $h^{1,1} = 1$ and $h^{2,1} = 101$.

The 4D effective theory inherits massless scalar fields — one per harmonic form — called moduli. Their unfixed values are the famous moduli problem: bare CY compactification has a continuous family of vacua. Adding fluxes through 3-cycles, branes, and non-perturbative effects can lift these, leading to the landscape. Heuristic counts of flux vacua produce the often-quoted figure $\sim 10^{500}$, which is not a theorem but a back-of-envelope dimensional estimate.

6. Dualities: T-duality and mirror symmetry

String theory enjoys a remarkable web of dualities: equivalences between theories at different parameter values, often relating a strong-coupling regime of one theory to a weak-coupling regime of another. The cleanest example is T-duality on a circle.

T-duality on a circle

Compactify one direction $X^{25}$ on a circle of radius $R$. The closed string spectrum receives two integer quantum numbers: momentum $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ (from periodicity) and winding $m \in \mathbb{Z}$ (the string can wrap the circle $m$ times). The mass formula is

$M^2(n,m,R) = \left(\dfrac{n}{R}\right)^2 + \left(\dfrac{mR}{\alpha'}\right)^2 + \dfrac{2}{\alpha'}(N + \tilde N - 2)$

and is invariant under $(R, n, m) \mapsto (\alpha'/R, m, n)$: shrinking the circle to $\alpha'/R$ is physically the same as expanding to $R$, while exchanging momentum and winding. The fixed point $R = \sqrt{\alpha'}$ — the self-dual radius — is special: at that point an enhanced $\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)$ gauge symmetry appears.

T-duality on type IIA exchanges it with type IIB. Apply T-duality once and you have swapped the two type-II theories; this also swaps even-dimensional and odd-dimensional D-branes (§7).

Mirror symmetry as fibrewise T-duality

Strominger–Yau–Zaslow (1996) proposed that the geometric mechanism behind mirror symmetry is T-duality on a special-Lagrangian $T^3$-fibration. A Calabi–Yau 3-fold $X$ should admit a fibration $X \to B$ over a real 3-base $B$ whose generic fibres are $T^3$ tori carrying special-Lagrangian structure. The mirror $Y \to B$ is the dual fibration: replace each torus fibre $T^3_b$ by its Pontryagin dual $\check T^3_b = \mathrm{Hom}(T^3_b, U(1))$. Fibrewise T-duality swaps

  • the Kähler moduli of $X$ ($h^{1,1}$ in number) with the complex-structure moduli of $Y$ ($h^{2,1}$ in number);
  • the A-model on $X$ (symplectic; counts pseudo-holomorphic curves; Gromov–Witten invariants) with the B-model on $Y$ (complex; period integrals of the holomorphic 3-form);
  • type IIA on $X$ with type IIB on $Y$.

Concretely, Candelas–de la Ossa–Green–Parkes (1991) computed the Picard–Fuchs equation governing periods on the mirror of the quintic and read off — under the mirror map — Gromov–Witten counts of rational curves on the quintic: $2{,}875$ lines, $609{,}250$ conics, $317{,}206{,}375$ twisted cubics, $\dots$ Givental and Lian–Liu–Yau later proved these to all orders.

7. Branes and the math output of string theory

Type-II superstring theories contain non-perturbative D-branes: $(p+1)$-dimensional submanifolds where open strings are allowed to end (Dirichlet boundary conditions in $9-p$ transverse directions). For a long time D-branes were just a useful boundary condition. Polchinski (1995) showed they are physical, dynamical objects carrying Ramond–Ramond charge — exactly the charges that closed-string theory had predicted but no perturbative state had supplied. Suddenly non-perturbative string theory had its missing solitons.

A stack of $N$ coincident D-branes supports a $U(N)$ gauge theory in its world-volume: open strings stretching from brane $i$ to brane $j$ provide $N^2$ massless gauge bosons (the adjoint of $U(N)$). This is brane engineering: 4D (or other-dimension) gauge theories built from geometry. The AdS/CFT correspondence (Maldacena 1997) takes this to its limit: a stack of $N$ D3-branes at low energy is dual to type-IIB string theory on $\mathrm{AdS}_5 \times S^5$, identifying $\mathcal{N} = 4$ super-Yang–Mills with a gravitational theory in one higher dimension.

The physics of string theory remains experimentally untested. Its mathematical output, however, is established and substantial. Below is a curated gallery of duality-driven theorems that have entered pure mathematics on their own terms.

8. Connections

String theory is the largest single bridge between graduate-level pure mathematics and theoretical physics. Its physical status is debated; its mathematical output is not. The mathematics entered the literature through six routes that are largely independent of the physics: Calabi–Yau geometry as the natural target for $\mathrm{SU}(3)$-holonomy, mirror symmetry as a categorical equivalence and as enumerative-geometry organizer, Kac–Moody and vertex operator algebras as moonshine machinery, derived/Fukaya categories as homological mirror symmetry, the Hitchin moduli space and geometric Langlands as $\mathcal{N}=4$ SYM $S$-duality, and AdS/CFT-derived integrable structures inside conformal-bootstrap data. Open frontiers: a non-perturbative formulation of M-theory; a derivation of the Standard Model from a specific geometric compactification; a rigorous mathematical foundation for the Fukaya category in full generality; and the moduli-stabilization problem of producing a controlled 4D vacuum without unphysical light scalars.

  • Calabi–Yau manifolds — string compactifications, DT, SYZ — the geometric input that makes §5 work.
  • Mirror symmetry — homological mirror symmetry (Kontsevich) — the derived-categorical formulation of §6.
  • Monstrous moonshine — the Monster vertex algebra — chiral 26D bosonic CFT explains the McKay–Thompson series.
  • Vertex operator algebras — the formal axioms — the rigorous algebraic framing of a chiral 2D CFT, born from string theory.
  • General relativity — Einstein's equations — the IR limit that the closed-string graviton is supposed to reproduce.
  • Gauge theory — the 4D effective theories living on D-brane stacks; AdS/CFT runs in the opposite direction.
  • Symplectic manifolds — A-model targets; the Fukaya category sits over them.
