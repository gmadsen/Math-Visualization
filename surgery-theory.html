Surgery theory & high-dimensional manifolds

Surgery is the geometric move that swaps an embedded $S^{k-1}\times D^{n-k}$ for a $D^k\times S^{n-k-1}$. From this single operation Smale, Browder, Novikov, Sullivan, and Wall built a classification machine for closed manifolds in dimension $\ge 5$ — and the dimensions where the machine breaks (3, 4) are where the deepest geometry lives.

Reader's note. This page assumes comfort with smooth manifolds, characteristic classes, and the handle-decomposition language of Morse theory §2. The first three sections build the geometric machinery (handles, $h$-cobordism, $s$-cobordism); the next two do the algebraic side ($L$-theory, surgery exact sequence); the last harvests the consequences (exotic spheres, the dim-3-and-4 frontier).

1. Handle decompositions and surgery

Let $M$ be a smooth closed $n$-manifold. A $k$-handle is a copy of $D^k\times D^{n-k}$ attached to a manifold-with-boundary $W$ along an embedded copy of $S^{k-1}\times D^{n-k}\subset\partial W$. The number $k$ is the index; $D^k\times\{0\}$ is the core; $\{0\}\times D^{n-k}$ is the cocore. From the Morse-theoretic side: a Morse function $f\colon W\to\mathbb{R}$ adds exactly one handle of index $k$ each time $f$ crosses a critical value of index $k$. Handles are how the topology changes; cells are their CW-shadow.

The same operation, viewed from the boundary, is surgery. Given an embedding $\varphi\colon S^{k-1}\times D^{n-k}\hookrightarrow M^n$, the surgery $$M \;\rightsquigarrow\; M' \;=\; \bigl(M\setminus\varphi(S^{k-1}\times\mathring{D}^{n-k})\bigr)\;\cup_{S^{k-1}\times S^{n-k-1}}\;\bigl(D^k\times S^{n-k-1}\bigr)$$ removes the open tube and glues in its dual. The two pieces share boundary $S^{k-1}\times S^{n-k-1}$, and the resulting $M'$ is again a closed $n$-manifold. The trace of the surgery is the cobordism $W^{n+1} = M\times[0,1]\cup_{\varphi}(D^k\times D^{n-k})$, with $\partial W = M\sqcup M'$. So surgery preserves cobordism class while changing the manifold — this is the lever that converts manifold classification into a search through cobordism representatives.

Trivial-attaching examples in low dimensions

Surgery on a trivially embedded $S^0\hookrightarrow M^n$ (so $k=1$) on a connected $M$ disconnects two points and joins them by a 1-handle — equivalently, takes the connected sum with $S^1\times S^{n-1}$ along a small ball. Surgery on an unknotted $S^1\hookrightarrow S^3$ with $0$-framing turns $S^3$ into $S^2\times S^1$ — the simplest non-trivial example, and the gateway to Kirby calculus on 3-manifolds.

2. The $h$-cobordism theorem (Smale)

An $h$-cobordism is a compact cobordism $W^{n+1}$ with both inclusions $M_0\hookrightarrow W\hookleftarrow M_1$ homotopy equivalences. Smale's $h$-cobordism theorem (1962) is the apex statement of high-dimensional manifold theory:

Theorem (Smale 1962). If $W^{n+1}$ is a compact $h$-cobordism between simply-connected closed manifolds $M_0,M_1$ with $\dim M_i = n\ge 5$, then $W$ is diffeomorphic to $M_0\times[0,1]$ — in particular $M_0\cong M_1$.

The generalized Poincaré conjecture in dimensions $\ge 5$ is an immediate corollary: if $\Sigma^n$ is a homotopy $n$-sphere, then $\Sigma\setminus\{\text{pt}\}$ is contractible, so removing two open disks from $\Sigma$ produces an $h$-cobordism between two copies of $S^{n-1}$; Smale's theorem makes this trivial, hence $\Sigma$ is a union of two disks — homeomorphic to $S^n$.

Proof strategy: cancel handles in pairs

Pick a Morse function $f\colon W\to[0,1]$ with $M_0=f^{-1}(0)$, $M_1=f^{-1}(1)$, no boundary critical points. The relative homology $H_*(W,M_0)$ vanishes (homotopy equivalence). The Morse-complex chains in degree $k$ are free $\mathbb{Z}$-modules on the index-$k$ critical points; vanishing relative homology forces matching algebraic counts of index-$k$ and index-$(k+1)$ handles. A miracle of dimension $\ge 5$ — the Whitney trick — converts each algebraic intersection number of $\pm 1$ into a clean geometric one, after which Smale's handle cancellation lemma erases the matched pair. Iterating in turn empties the Morse function of all critical points, leaving $W=M_0\times[0,1]$.

Why dimension $\ge 5$. The Whitney trick uses an embedded 2-disk $D^2\hookrightarrow W$ to slide one intersection point onto another. In ambient dimension $\ge 5$ a generic 2-disk has self-intersections of expected dimension $4 - N \le -1$ (negative), so generic position eliminates them. In dimension 4 the disk's interior may self-intersect and there is no room to push the crossings off — Casson handles and Freedman's machinery recover topological-but-not-smooth versions of the trick.

3. The $s$-cobordism theorem and the Whitehead group

Drop simple connectivity. Now $H_*(W,M_0)=0$ no longer reduces the chain complex of handles to a free, perfectly-matched form: the Whitehead torsion $\tau(W)\in\mathrm{Wh}(\pi_1 M)$ is the residual obstruction. Define $$\mathrm{Wh}(\pi) \;:=\; K_1(\mathbb{Z}\pi)/\bigl\langle \pm g \,\big|\, g\in\pi\bigr\rangle.$$ Concretely: an element of $K_1(\mathbb{Z}\pi)$ is the class of an invertible matrix over $\mathbb{Z}\pi$; Whitehead torsion mods out the obvious $1\times 1$ units $\pm g$. Writing the chain complex of $\widetilde W$ over $\mathbb{Z}\pi$ as $C_*$, the matrices that cancel adjacent pairs of handles assemble — after acyclicity — into a single class $\tau(W)\in\mathrm{Wh}(\pi)$.

Theorem ($s$-cobordism, Mazur–Stallings–Barden). An $h$-cobordism $W^{n+1}$ with $n\ge 5$ between manifolds with $\pi_1=\pi$ is trivial (i.e. $W\cong M_0\times[0,1]$) iff $\tau(W)=0$ in $\mathrm{Wh}(\pi)$.

Whitehead group of small finite cyclic groups

The Whitehead group is computable but rarely easy. Higman: $\mathrm{Wh}(\mathbb{Z}/n)=0$ for $n=1,2,3,4,6$. Bass: $\mathrm{Wh}(\mathbb{Z}/5)\cong\mathbb{Z}$, generated by the cyclotomic unit $1-t+t^2$ in $\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/5]$. For free abelian $\pi=\mathbb{Z}^k$ the Bass–Heller–Swan formula gives $\mathrm{Wh}(\mathbb{Z}^k)=0$ — the (geometric) reason every $h$-cobordism on a torus is trivial.

4. The surgery exact sequence

The classification of closed manifolds in a fixed homotopy type — Browder–Novikov–Sullivan–Wall — is captured by a long exact sequence of pointed sets / abelian groups. For a closed manifold $X^n$ with $n\ge 5$ and fundamental group $\pi=\pi_1 X$: $$\dots \;\longrightarrow\; L_{n+1}(\mathbb{Z}\pi) \;\longrightarrow\; \mathcal{S}(X) \;\longrightarrow\; \mathcal{N}(X) \;\xrightarrow{\;\sigma\;}\; L_n(\mathbb{Z}\pi).$$ Each term has its own meaning:

  • $\mathcal{S}(X)$, the structure set — pairs $(N,h)$ where $N$ is a closed manifold and $h\colon N\to X$ is a homotopy equivalence, modulo $h$-cobordism. The thing we want to compute.
  • $\mathcal{N}(X)$, normal invariants — degree-1 normal maps $f\colon M\to X$ modulo normal cobordism. Sullivan: $\mathcal{N}(X)\cong[X,G/O]$ (smooth) or $[X,G/\mathrm{Top}]$ (topological).
  • $L_n(\mathbb{Z}\pi)$, Wall's $L$-groups — algebraic surgery obstructions. Quadratic forms (in even $n$) or formations (odd $n$) on stably free $\mathbb{Z}\pi$-modules, modulo hyperbolic ones.
  • $\sigma$, the surgery obstruction map — turns a normal invariant into its $L$-theoretic shadow. The element of $\mathcal{N}(X)$ lifts to $\mathcal{S}(X)$ exactly when $\sigma$ vanishes.

The action of $L_{n+1}(\mathbb{Z}\pi)$ on $\mathcal{S}(X)$ is by plumbing: take a quadratic form's worth of new manifold pieces, glue them onto $X\times[0,1]$, and read off a new homotopy structure. Two homotopy structures land in the same $\mathcal{N}(X)$-class iff they differ by such a plumbing.

5. $L$-theory and the Wall obstruction

Wall's $L$-groups are the algebraic engine. In each dimension, $L_n(\mathbb{Z}\pi)$ classifies a different kind of geometric data:

0non-degenerate symmetric form, mod hyperbolic$\mathbb{Z}$ (signature$/8$)
1automorphism of hyperbolic form, mod boundary$0$
2non-degenerate quadratic form over $\mathbb{Z}/2$, mod hyperbolic$\mathbb{Z}/2$ (Arf invariant)

The values for trivial $\pi$ are 4-periodic: $L_n(\mathbb{Z}) = L_{n+4}(\mathbb{Z})$, a reflection of the periodicity in the underlying signature/Arf machinery. For $\pi=\mathbb{Z}/2$, Wall computed $L_*(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/2])$ in his 1970 book; the answer involves an extra factor of $\mathbb{Z}/2$ in dimension 2, capturing the Browder–Livesay obstruction.

Browder's surgery theorem

The hard direction of the surgery exact sequence. A normal map $f\colon M\to X$ has an algebraic surgery obstruction $\sigma(f)\in L_n(\mathbb{Z}\pi_1 X)$, obtained by reading the intersection form on the kernel of $f_*\colon H_n(\widetilde M)\to H_n(\widetilde X)$ (mid-dimensional) modulo a quadratic refinement.

Browder's surgery theorem. If $\dim X\ge 5$, then $f$ is normally cobordant to a homotopy equivalence iff $\sigma(f) = 0$ in $L_n(\mathbb{Z}\pi)$.

The smooth analogue at the prime 2 is Rokhlin's theorem: a closed smooth simply-connected oriented $4$-manifold with even intersection form (i.e. $w_2=0$, equivalently spin) has signature divisible by 16. The $E_8$ lattice has signature 8 but is not realised smoothly; Freedman built a topological 4-manifold with intersection form $E_8$, and Donaldson proved no smooth representative exists.

6. Applications: exotic spheres, dimensions 3 and 4

Let $\Theta_n$ denote the abelian group (under connected sum) of $h$-cobordism classes of homotopy $n$-spheres. Kervaire and Milnor (1963) computed $\Theta_n$ via surgery, fitting it into an exact sequence connecting the stable homotopy of spheres, the cokernel of the $J$-homomorphism, and a piece $\mathrm{bP}_{n+1}\subset\Theta_n$ of plumbing-boundary spheres. The headline is that $\Theta_n$ is finite for every $n\ge 5$. Sample values:

6$0$smooth Poincaré is true
7$\mathbb{Z}/28$Milnor's exotic 7-spheres
8$\mathbb{Z}/2$Kervaire's element of order 2
9$(\mathbb{Z}/2)^3$image-of-$J$ + Kervaire

The orders of $\mathrm{bP}_{4k}$ are essentially Bernoulli denominators — a startling appearance of number theory inside differential topology. Concretely $|\mathrm{bP}_{4k}| = 2^{2k-2}(2^{2k-1}-1)\,\mathrm{numerator}(4B_{2k}/k)$ (Kervaire–Milnor); spot-check $k=2$ gives $4\cdot 7\cdot 1=28$ and $k=3$ gives $16\cdot 31\cdot 2 = 992$.

The dimensions that escape

Dimension 3. Smale's theorem fails: the Whitney trick needs $n\ge 5$. The Poincaré conjecture in dim 3 was open for a century after its 1904 statement; Perelman (2003) finally proved it via Hamilton's Ricci flow with surgery. Freedman's topological proof in dim 4 is unrelated; Perelman's argument is purely Riemannian-geometric and surgery-free in the high-dim sense.

Dimension 4. Freedman (1982, Fields Medal 1986): every closed simply-connected topological 4-manifold is determined by its intersection form on $H^2$ and the Kirby–Siebenmann invariant $\mathrm{ks}\in\mathbb{Z}/2$. So topological surgery succeeds — for good fundamental groups, including $\pi_1=1$. Smoothly, the story diverges sharply: Donaldson (1983) showed the negative-definite forms $E_8$, $E_8\oplus E_8$, … are not smoothly realised; Donaldson and Seiberg–Witten produce gauge-theoretic invariants invisible to surgery. The smooth 4-dimensional Poincaré conjecture — is every homotopy 4-sphere diffeomorphic to $S^4$? — remains open as of 2026; no exotic smooth $S^4$ has been built, but no obstruction is known.

Connections

Surgery is one of three places where the smooth/topological/PL splitting of high-dimensional topology became visible — the other two being characteristic-class theory and gauge theory. The frontier today is dimension 4: smooth Poincaré is open, the Kirby calculus / handle-slide picture stops yielding diffeomorphism types, and Heegaard Floer and Seiberg–Witten replace algebraic surgery as the lever. Higher categorical surgery (algebraic $L$-theory in the Ranicki sense, surgery spectra) connects the classical exact sequence to the $L$-spectrum of $\mathbb{Z}\pi$ and ultimately to assembly maps in the Farrell–Jones conjecture.

  • Cobordism — surgery and $h$-cobordism — the bordism-ring perspective: surgery is the move that connects bordism representatives.
  • Morse theory — Cerf theory and wall-crossing — the diff-topological foundation of handle slides, used implicitly throughout the $h$-cobordism proof.
  • Mapping class groups & 4-manifolds — Freedman's topological classification and the Donaldson preview that obstructs smooth surgery in dim 4.
  • Algebraic K-theory foundations — where the Whitehead group $\mathrm{Wh}(\pi)$ lives; the $s$-cobordism theorem is the geometric face of $K_1(\mathbb{Z}\pi)$.
  • Atiyah–Singer index theorem — the $L$-genus appearing in $L$-theory and the signature theorem are the same characteristic class; surgery and index theory shake hands at dimension $4k$.
