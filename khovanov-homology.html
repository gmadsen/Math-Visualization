Khovanov homology

Lift the Jones polynomial from a Laurent polynomial to a bigraded chain complex. Each crossing becomes a hypercube edge, each resolution becomes a tensor power of the Frobenius algebra $\mathbb{Z}[X]/(X^2)$, and the graded Euler characteristic puts Jones back where you found it. The homology, however, sees more: cobordism functoriality, the Rasmussen invariant $s(K)$, slice-genus bounds, and a categorical answer to "what is the Jones polynomial counting?"

Reader's note. This page assumes the Jones polynomial from the bracket / state-sum side and a working command of chain complexes and bigradings. We will categorify each piece of the bracket — the smoothings, the multiplications, the writhe correction — into a piece of homological algebra, then read off the Jones polynomial as $\sum (-1)^i q^j \dim Kh^{i,j}$.

1. The cube of resolutions

Fix an oriented diagram $D$ of a link $L$ with $n$ crossings. Each crossing has two local smoothings:

  • The $0$-resolution, in which the strand going from upper-left to lower-right is cut and reconnected horizontally.
  • The $1$-resolution, in which the strand going from upper-right to lower-left is cut and reconnected vertically.

Choosing one resolution at each crossing gives a planar tangle with no crossings — a disjoint collection of embedded circles in the plane. The set of all such choices is indexed by the cube $\{0,1\}^n$: each vertex $\alpha\in\{0,1\}^n$ labels a complete resolution $D_\alpha$, and each edge of the cube flips a single resolution $0\to 1$ at one chosen crossing.

Two adjacent vertices $\alpha\to\alpha'$ differ by changing one circle into two (a split) or merging two circles into one (a merge). These are exactly the elementary saddle cobordisms — locally, you push the smoothing across a half-twist. The cube is therefore not just a combinatorial object but a diagram in the category $\mathbf{Cob}^2$ of $1$-manifolds and $2$-cobordisms between them.

2. The Khovanov chain complex

Now layer linear algebra over the cube. Let $A=\mathbb{Z}[X]/(X^2)$, the rank-$2$ free abelian group on basis $\{1,X\}$, with grading $\deg(1)=1$, $\deg(X)=-1$ — this is the unique nontrivial graded commutative Frobenius algebra of rank two over $\mathbb{Z}$. Its Frobenius structure consists of:

  • multiplication $m\colon A\otimes A\to A$ with $m(1\otimes a)=a$, $m(X\otimes X)=0$,
  • comultiplication $\Delta\colon A\to A\otimes A$, $\Delta(1)=1\otimes X+X\otimes 1$, $\Delta(X)=X\otimes X$,
  • unit $\eta\colon\mathbb{Z}\to A$, $1\mapsto 1$, and counit $\varepsilon\colon A\to\mathbb{Z}$, $\varepsilon(X)=1$, $\varepsilon(1)=0$.

To each cube vertex $D_\alpha$ with $k_\alpha$ circles, assign the graded module $V_\alpha = A^{\otimes k_\alpha}\{|\alpha|\}$, where $|\alpha|=\sum\alpha_i$ is the height in the cube and $\{m\}$ is a quantum-grading shift by $m$. To each edge $\alpha\to\alpha'$, assign the linear map $d_e\colon V_\alpha\to V_{\alpha'}$ given by $m$ on the merging edge or $\Delta\otimes\mathrm{id}$ on the splitting edge, signed by $(-1)^{\sigma(e)}$ for a coherent edge-orientation $\sigma$ that makes squares anticommute.

Sum the per-edge maps over each height level to obtain $d^i\colon\bigoplus_{|\alpha|=i}V_\alpha\to\bigoplus_{|\alpha|=i+1}V_\alpha$. Anticommuting squares force $d^{i+1}\circ d^i = 0$. After applying the global shifts

$$C^{i,j}(D) \;=\; \bigoplus_{|\alpha|=i+n_-} (V_\alpha)_j\,\{n_+ - 2n_-\},$$

where $n_\pm$ is the number of positive / negative crossings, the bigraded complex $(C^{i,j}(D),d)$ is the Khovanov chain complex. Its cohomology $Kh^{i,j}(L) := H^i(C^{*,j}(D))$ is what we shall verify is a link invariant.

Why the shifts? The shifts $\{n_+ - 2n_-\}$ in $j$ and by $-n_-$ in $i$ are exactly what makes $Kh$ invariant under R1 (the move that changes $n_\pm$ but not the underlying link). They are the categorified avatar of the $(-A)^{-3w(D)}$ writhe-correction in the Kauffman bracket.

3. Categorifying the Jones polynomial

The whole construction is engineered to make the graded Euler characteristic recover Jones. For each $i$, set the graded dimension

$\dim_q Kh^i(L) := \sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}} q^j \dim_{\mathbb{Q}} Kh^{i,j}(L)\otimes\mathbb{Q}.$

Then the alternating sum recovers the unnormalised Jones polynomial:

$$\widehat V_L(q) \;=\; \sum_{i,j}(-1)^i q^j \dim Kh^{i,j}(L) \;=\; (q+q^{-1})\,V_L(q^2),$$

where $V_L$ is the usual Jones polynomial normalised so that $V_{\text{unknot}}(q)=1$. The factor $q+q^{-1}$ is the graded dimension of $A$ itself — the homology of the unknot, $Kh(\text{unknot})\cong A$, with classes in bidegrees $(0,1)$ and $(0,-1)$.

Reidemeister invariance. One checks that the chain homotopy type of $C^{*,*}(D)$ is unchanged by R1, R2, R3. The proof is local: for each move, exhibit an explicit chain homotopy equivalence between the two chain complexes attached to the two sides of the move. R2 and R3 are easy bookkeeping; R1 uses the Frobenius structure crucially (the deletion / contraction of an unknotted circle absorbs into multiplication by $1\otimes X$). Once R1, R2, R3 are checked, $Kh^{i,j}$ depends only on the link.

Functoriality under cobordisms. A cobordism $\Sigma\colon L_0\to L_1$ in $\mathbb{R}^3\times[0,1]$ induces a chain map $\phi_\Sigma\colon Kh(L_0)\to Kh(L_1)$, well-defined up to overall sign by Bar-Natan's local moves. This is the categorified analogue of the Jones polynomial's invariance: in the polynomial world, a cobordism merely sends $V_{L_0}$ and $V_{L_1}$ to the same number when one collapses; in homology, the cobordism is itself an arrow between two objects.

4. The Lee deformation and the Rasmussen invariant

Khovanov homology has a one-parameter deformation due to Lee. Replace the Frobenius algebra $A=\mathbb{Z}[X]/(X^2)$ by

$A_{Lee} \;=\; \mathbb{Q}[X]/(X^2-1).$

The multiplications and comultiplications carry over, but $X^2=1$ now, and the algebra splits as $A_{Lee}\cong\mathbb{Q}\oplus\mathbb{Q}$ via $X\mapsto\pm 1$. The Bar-Natan deformation is the universal one over $\mathbb{Z}$: $A_{BN}=\mathbb{Z}[X,h]/(X^2-hX)$, recovering Khovanov at $h=0$ and Lee at $h\to\text{(generic)}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

Lee's theorem: for a knot $K$, $Kh_{Lee}(K)\cong\mathbb{Q}^2$, regardless of $K$. The two generators have an explicit description: orient $K$ and $\overline K$ (the orientation reversal) and apply the Seifert algorithm; the resulting all-orientation-respecting smoothing produces a canonical class $\mathfrak{s}_o$ for each orientation $o\in\{K,\overline K\}$. These two classes span $Kh_{Lee}(K)$.

The classes $\mathfrak{s}_o$ are not homogeneous in the original $q$-grading — Lee's differential is filtered, not graded. But it makes sense to ask for the minimum and maximum $q$-grading of $\mathfrak{s}_K+\mathfrak{s}_{\overline K}$ and $\mathfrak{s}_K-\mathfrak{s}_{\overline K}$. They are equal up to $\pm 2$, and the average

$$s(K) \;:=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\big(q_{\min}(\mathfrak{s}_K+\mathfrak{s}_{\overline K}) + q_{\max}(\mathfrak{s}_K+\mathfrak{s}_{\overline K})\big) \;\in\; 2\mathbb{Z}$$

is the Rasmussen invariant. It is additive under connected sum, $s(K_1\#K_2)=s(K_1)+s(K_2)$, and satisfies the slice-genus bound

$|s(K)| \;\le\; 2g_4(K),$

where $g_4(K)$ is the smooth four-genus — the minimum genus of an embedded oriented surface in $B^4$ with boundary $K$.

5. Foams and $sl_n$ link homology

Khovanov homology categorifies the Jones polynomial — equivalently, the Reshetikhin–Turaev invariant for $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ on the standard $2$-dimensional representation. The natural question is: what categorifies the $\mathfrak{sl}_n$ Reshetikhin–Turaev invariant for arbitrary $n\ge 2$?

Khovanov and Rozansky's answer replaces the Jones state model by one where the local picture at each strand is an $n$-coloured trivalent web. The basic building blocks are foams: branched surfaces with marked seams, glued from sheets that locally satisfy Kuperberg-style spider relations. To each such foam one assigns a polynomial in dotted generators modulo a calculus of relations. Stacking foams over the height direction gives a chain complex whose homology is $sl_n$ Khovanov–Rozansky homology $KR_n(L)$.

An equivalent — sometimes more tractable — model uses matrix factorisations of the potential $W(x)=x^{n+1}$. Each crossing becomes a Koszul-style $2$-periodic complex over $\mathbb{Q}[x_1,\dots,x_k]$, and the link invariant is the homology of the total tensor product. The $n=2$ case recovers Khovanov; $n\ge 3$ produces genuinely new invariants whose graded Euler characteristic is the $\mathfrak{sl}_n$ HOMFLY specialisation.

The triply-graded refinement, HOMFLY-PT homology, packages all $sl_n$ towers into one homology theory $\mathcal{H}(L)$ with three gradings $(i,j,k)$. Its graded Euler characteristic is the HOMFLY-PT polynomial; specialising the third grading recovers $KR_n$ for each $n$. The conjectural existence of colored HOMFLY-PT homology, indexed by Young diagrams, ties the picture to the Hilbert scheme of points on $\mathbb{C}^2$ (Gorsky–Negut–Rasmussen).

6. Applications and open problems

The single most striking application is Rasmussen's combinatorial proof of the Milnor conjecture: for the $(p,q)$ torus knot $T_{p,q}$,

$g_4(T_{p,q}) \;=\; \dfrac{(p-1)(q-1)}{2}.$

Kronheimer and Mrowka proved this in the 1990s using gauge-theoretic Donaldson invariants; Rasmussen's $2004$ proof uses only Khovanov homology and the elementary inequality $|s(K)|\le 2g_4(K)$, plus the fact that $s(T_{p,q})=(p-1)(q-1)$. It was the first major topology theorem proved by combinatorial knot homology.

Detection results. Khovanov homology detects the unknot: $Kh(K)$ has rank $2$ (over $\mathbb{Q}$) iff $K$ is the unknot (Kronheimer–Mrowka). The proof goes through a spectral sequence from Khovanov homology to instanton homology of the double branched cover; the unknot collapses the spectral sequence trivially. Subsequently, Hedden–Ni and Baldwin–Sivek have shown $Kh$ detects the trefoils, the figure-eight, and the Hopf link.

Slice-disk obstructions. Beyond Rasmussen's inequality, one uses $Kh$ at the level of cobordism maps to obstruct specific surfaces. Sundberg–Swann and others have developed concrete tests for whether a given knot bounds a specific genus-$g$ surface in $B^4$.

Open frontiers. Whether $Kh$ distinguishes all knots is open. The relationship to Heegaard Floer's link Floer homology is conjecturally a spectral sequence (Manolescu's pillar), and to Seidel–Smith's symplectic Khovanov homology is conjecturally an isomorphism (Abouzaid–Smith). An integer lift of $s$ refining the rational invariant remains elusive, and the categorification of the colored HOMFLY-PT polynomial is still being assembled.

Connections

Khovanov homology sits at a four-way crossing: gauge theory (where the unknot detection theorem really lives), symplectic topology (Seidel–Smith and the wrapped Fukaya category of an $A_n$-resolution), categorified representation theory (Khovanov–Lauda–Rouquier algebras categorify quantum group reps), and combinatorial knot theory (Rasmussen's combinatorial proofs of slice-genus bounds). Open frontiers cluster around comparing distinct link-homology theories — Khovanov, instanton, Heegaard Floer, symplectic Khovanov — all of which categorify some flavour of the Jones polynomial but disagree pointwise.

  • Knot polynomials — Jones polynomial — the polynomial recovered as the graded Euler characteristic; everything in §3 is the categorification of the bracket calculation.
  • Knot polynomials — quantum invariants — the modular tensor category of $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$-modules; foams categorify the $sl_n$ Reshetikhin–Turaev invariants.
  • Homological algebra — chain complexes — the cube of resolutions is the basic example of a multicomplex; the bigrading and edge-sign convention are textbook homological algebra dressed up.
  • Cobordism — TQFT — Khovanov homology is functorial under link cobordisms (Bar-Natan's $(1+1)$-dim TQFT extended to $4$-dim cobordisms in $\mathbb{R}^3\times[0,1]$); the foam calculus is a $(2+1)$-dim TQFT skeleton.
  • Gauge theory — Kronheimer–Mrowka's spectral sequence from $Kh$ to instanton homology proves the unknot-detection theorem and connects categorified topology to Donaldson–Floer theory.
  • Quantum groups — Khovanov–Lauda–Rouquier algebras categorify $U_q^-(\mathfrak{g})$, and link homologies arise via diagrammatic categorified reps; the quantum group is no longer just a Hopf algebra but a $2$-category.
