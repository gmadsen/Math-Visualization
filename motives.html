Motives

Algebraic geometry has many cohomology theories — Betti, de Rham, $\ell$-adic étale, crystalline — that compute different invariants of the same variety yet share an uncannily parallel formal structure. Motives are Grothendieck's conjectural answer to why: a universal cohomology that all the rest are realizations of, with a Galois group acting on it that subsumes the classical $\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$. Half conjecture, half organising principle, the framework underwrites the Hodge conjecture, the Tate conjecture, and large stretches of the Langlands program.

1. The cohomology zoo and the realization problem

Pick a smooth projective variety $X$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. Several apparently-unrelated machines compute "cohomology of $X$":

  • Betti: $H^*_B(X(\mathbb{C}); \mathbb{Q})$ — singular cohomology of the underlying topological space (after base change to $\mathbb{C}$). Pure transcendental invariant; depends on the embedding $\mathbb{Q} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{C}$.
  • Algebraic de Rham: $H^*_{\dR}(X/\mathbb{Q})$ — derived from the algebraic Kähler-differentials complex $\Omega^\bullet_{X/\mathbb{Q}}$ (specifically, the hypercohomology $\mathbb{H}^*$, which is sheaf cohomology of a complex rather than a single sheaf — see algebraic de Rham). Defined purely algebraically; carries a Hodge filtration $F^\bullet$.
  • $\ell$-adic étale: $H^*_{\et}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}; \mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ — derived functors on the étale site. Carries a continuous action of $\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$ for each prime $\ell$.
  • Crystalline: $H^*_{\mathrm{cris}}(X_{\mathbb{F}_p}/W(\mathbb{F}_p))$ — for the reduction of $X$ mod $p$, in characteristic-$p$ Witt vectors. Carries a Frobenius $\varphi$ and a connection.

These take values in genuinely different categories — $\mathbb{Q}$-vector spaces, filtered $\mathbb{Q}$-vector spaces, $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$-vector spaces with Galois action, $W(\mathbb{F}_p)$-modules with Frobenius — yet for any fixed $X$ they produce numerically the same Betti numbers, satisfy parallel Künneth and Poincaré-duality formulas, both have cycle-class maps from $\mathrm{CH}^*(X)$, and all admit a Lefschetz fixed-point theorem.

Slogan. Each cohomology theory is a different orchestration of the same underlying motive. Just as a musical motif appears in strings, brass, and woodwinds with different timbre but the same melodic skeleton, a variety has one motive that the four realization functors play on different instruments.

Grothendieck's vision: there should exist a $\mathbb{Q}$-linear category $\mathsf{Mot}_k$ of motives over $k$ together with a universal cohomology functor $h\colon \mathsf{SmProj}_k \to \mathsf{Mot}_k$, such that every "reasonable" cohomology theory factors uniquely as $h$ followed by a realization functor:

The dashed arrows are realization functors; the goal of the next several sections is to make the middle box rigorous. The unifying observation is that every cohomology theory people care about (ordinary, $K$-theory, cobordism, étale, motivic) fits this template, with the Eilenberg–MacLane spectrum replaced by some other ring spectrum. Motives are the universal target.

2. Correspondences as morphisms

The first hard idea is that morphisms in $\mathsf{Mot}_k$ are not the regular morphisms of varieties — they are algebraic correspondences: formal $\mathbb{Q}$-linear combinations of subvarieties of products.

For smooth projective varieties $X$ and $Y$ over $k$ of dimensions $d_X$ and $d_Y$, an algebraic correspondence of degree $r$ from $X$ to $Y$ is an element of

$$\mathrm{Corr}^r(X, Y) := \mathrm{CH}^{d_X + r}(X \times Y) \otimes \mathbb{Q},$$

where $\mathrm{CH}^*$ is the Chow group — algebraic cycles modulo rational equivalence. Composition is a pushforward / pullback formula on triple products: for $\alpha \in \mathrm{Corr}(X, Y)$ and $\beta \in \mathrm{Corr}(Y, Z)$,

$$\beta \circ \alpha := (p_{XZ})_*\bigl((p_{XY})^*\alpha \cdot (p_{YZ})^*\beta\bigr) \in \mathrm{Corr}(X, Z),$$

where the $p_{ij}$ are the projections from $X \times Y \times Z$ and "$\cdot$" is intersection product on $\mathrm{CH}^*$.

Two correspondences worth seeing concretely:

  • Graphs of morphisms. Any regular morphism $f\colon X \to Y$ has a graph $\Gamma_f \subset X \times Y$, which is a degree-$0$ correspondence. Composition of graphs is the graph of the composition: $\Gamma_g \circ \Gamma_f = \Gamma_{g \circ f}$. So the category of varieties embeds into the category of correspondences.
  • The transposed identity. The diagonal $\Delta_X \subset X \times X$ is the identity correspondence on $X$. Its transpose $\Delta_X^t$ — viewed as a map $X \to X$ — is also $\Delta_X$, but viewed in $\mathrm{Corr}^*(X, X)$ in the opposite direction. The transpose operation is what makes $\mathsf{Mot}_k$ a category with duals.

The crucial generalisation over morphisms: every cohomology theory turns a correspondence $\alpha \in \mathrm{Corr}^r(X, Y)$ into a linear map $\alpha_*\colon H^*(X) \to H^{*+2r}(Y)$ by $\alpha_*(\xi) = (p_Y)_*(\mathrm{cl}(\alpha) \cdot p_X^*\xi)$ where $\mathrm{cl}$ is the cycle-class map. That is what motives need: a class of "morphisms" each cohomology theory can interpret consistently.

3. Pure Chow motives

With correspondences as morphisms, the category of pure Chow motives $\mathsf{Mot}_k^{\mathrm{Chow}}$ is built in three steps.

Step 1. Take the category whose objects are smooth projective varieties over $k$ and whose morphisms are degree-$0$ correspondences modulo rational equivalence. This is already a $\mathbb{Q}$-linear additive category.

Step 2 — pseudo-abelian envelope. Add the data of idempotent splittings: an object is now a pair $(X, p)$ where $p \in \Corr^0(X, X)$ satisfies $p^2 = p$, with morphisms $(X, p) \to (Y, q)$ being correspondences $\alpha$ such that $q \alpha p = \alpha$. A motive is now a "piece" of a variety cut out by a projector. Example: the diagonal of $\mathbb{P}^1$ decomposes as $\Delta = e_0 + e_2$ where $e_0 = \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathrm{pt}$ (the projector onto the $H^0$ piece) and $e_2 = \mathrm{pt} \times \mathbb{P}^1$ (onto the $H^2$ piece) — orthogonal idempotents whose sum is $\Delta$. This splits $h(\mathbb{P}^1) = (\mathbb{P}^1, e_0) \oplus (\mathbb{P}^1, e_2) = \mathbf{1} \oplus \mathbb{L}$.

Step 3 — Tate twists. Formally invert tensoring with the Lefschetz motive $\mathbb{L}$. Objects become triples $(X, p, n)$ with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$; the integer $n$ shifts cohomological degree by $2n$. (We meet $\mathbb{L}$ properly in the next section.)

The choice of equivalence relation on cycles produces different categories:

EquivalenceCategoryStatus
Rational$\mathsf{Mot}_k^{\mathrm{Chow}}$ (Chow motives)Largest; conjecturally Tannakian
Homological$\mathsf{Mot}_k^{\mathrm{hom}}$ (homological motives)Depends on choice of $H^*$; conjecturally indep.
Numerical$\mathsf{Mot}_k^{\mathrm{num}}$ (numerical motives)Smallest; semisimple (Jannsen)

Whether these three categories actually coincide is the content of the standard conjectures; we return to this below.

4. The Tate twist $\mathbb{Q}(n)$

The simplest non-trivial motive after $\mathbf{1}$ (the motive of a point) is the Lefschetz motive $\mathbb{L} = h^2(\mathbb{P}^1)$ — the "top piece" of the projective line. Its inverse is the Tate motive $\mathbb{Q}(1) := \mathbb{L}^{-1}$. Iterating, we have $\mathbb{Q}(n) := \mathbb{Q}(1)^{\otimes n}$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

Tate motives are the building blocks of all "Tate-type" structure in cohomology. Their realizations are:

Realization$\mathbb{Q}(1)$ becomes
Betti$2\pi i \cdot \mathbb{Q} \subset \mathbb{C}$ — a rank-1 $\mathbb{Q}$-Hodge structure of weight $-2$, type $(-1, -1)$
de Rham$\mathbb{Q}$, with Hodge filtration $F^{-1} = $ everything, $F^0 = 0$
$\ell$-adic$\mathbb{Q}_\ell(1) = \varprojlim \mu_{\ell^n}$, the cyclotomic character $\chi_\ell\colon G_\mathbb{Q} \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times$
Crystalline$\mathbb{Q}_p \cdot \tfrac{1}{p}$, with Frobenius $\varphi$ acting by $\tfrac{1}{p}$

That last column hides the punchline: every cohomology theory has its own incarnation of "$2\pi i$" and they all agree as motives, even though their realizations are scattered across vector spaces, Galois reps, and Frobenius modules. The number $2\pi i$ is the period integral $\int_{S^1} dz/z$ that pairs the Betti and de Rham realizations of $\mathbb{Q}(1)$ — and it is transcendental over $\mathbb{Q}$ (Hermite–Lindemann).

Periods of $\mathbb{Q}(n)$. The period matrix of $\mathbb{Q}(n)$ in the Betti-vs-de-Rham comparison is the scalar $(2\pi i)^n$. Multiple zeta values $\zeta(n_1, \ldots, n_r)$ are periods of mixed Tate motives over $\mathbb{Z}$ (Goncharov, Brown) — and the conjectural motivic Galois group of that subcategory acts on them, predicting every relation among MZVs from representation theory alone.

5. Tannakian categories — representations of an invisible group

The structural payoff of motives requires one more piece of category theory: the Tannakian formalism. A neutral Tannakian category over $k$ is a $k$-linear rigid abelian symmetric monoidal category $\mathcal{T}$ together with a faithful exact tensor functor $\omega\colon \mathcal{T} \to \mathsf{Vect}_k$ (the "fiber functor"), satisfying enough axioms that $\mathcal{T}$ is equivalent to the category of finite-dimensional representations of an affine group scheme:

$$\mathcal{T} \;\simeq\; \mathsf{Rep}_k\bigl(\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}^\otimes(\omega)\bigr).$$

The right-hand side names the group $G = \underline{\mathrm{Aut}}^\otimes(\omega)$ — the Tannakian fundamental group — as the automorphism scheme of the fiber functor: it remembers everything about $\mathcal{T}$ as the symmetries of one realization. Choose a different fiber functor and you get an isomorphic group; choose two fiber functors and you get a torsor between the corresponding groups.

Slogan (rephrased). A Tannakian category is "the category of representations of a group you haven't computed yet." Solving the category $\Leftrightarrow$ solving the group.

Examples:

  • $\mathsf{Vect}_k$ itself — Tannakian with $G = 1$.
  • Finite-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}$-Hodge structures — Tannakian, with $G$ the Mumford-Tate group.
  • Finite-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$-representations of $\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$ — Tannakian, with $G$ a quotient of $G_\mathbb{Q}$ itself (after $\ell$-adic completion).
  • Finite-dimensional $D$-modules with regular singularities on a complex variety — Tannakian, with $G$ a differential-Galois group.

6. The motivic Galois group

Now the climax. The category $\mathsf{Mot}_k^{\mathrm{num}}$ of numerical pure motives is, by Jannsen's theorem (1992), $\mathbb{Q}$-linear, abelian, and semisimple. Modulo the standard conjectures, it is also Tannakian; pick the Betti realization as a fiber functor (when $k \subset \mathbb{C}$) and the resulting Tannakian fundamental group is the motivic Galois group:

$$G_{\mathrm{mot}}(k) \;:=\; \underline{\mathrm{Aut}}^\otimes(\omega_B).$$

Concretely, $G_{\mathrm{mot}}(k)$ is a pro-reductive group scheme over $\mathbb{Q}$ (subject to the conjectures), and motives over $k$ are the same thing as finite-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}$-representations of $G_{\mathrm{mot}}(k)$.

The diagram is the motivic version of "every classical group acting on cohomology comes from a single bigger group acting on the universal motive." Concretely:

  • The étale realization $G_{\mathrm{mot}}(\mathbb{Q}) \to \Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$ sends a motivic representation to its $\ell$-adic Galois representation. This is how classical Galois theory is recovered.
  • The Hodge realization $G_{\mathrm{mot}}(\mathbb{Q}) \to \mathrm{MT}(X)$ recovers Mumford-Tate groups; the Hodge conjecture asks whether the Hodge realization is full.
  • The crystalline realization recovers the Newton-and-Hodge polygons of the variety mod $p$.

The motivic Galois group is to varieties what the Galois group $\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$ is to fields — but one categorical level up. Where classical Galois theory studied the symmetries of algebraic numbers, motivic Galois theory studies the symmetries of algebraic-geometric cohomology in all theories at once.

7. Realizations: how Betti, de Rham, ℓ-adic, crystalline all come from one motive

A realization functor on $\mathsf{Mot}_k$ is a tensor functor $R\colon \mathsf{Mot}_k \to \mathcal{C}$ for some target category $\mathcal{C}$ (depending on the realization). Each of the four cohomology theories from §1 is, conjecturally, such a functor:

RealizationTarget categoryExtra structure on $h(X)$
Betti $\omega_B$$\mathbb{Q}$-vector spaces$H^*_B(X(\mathbb{C}); \mathbb{Q})$ — a graded $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space
de Rham $\omega_{\mathrm{dR}}$filtered $k$-vector spaces$H^*_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/k)$ with Hodge filtration $F^\bullet$
$\ell$-adic $\omega_\ell$$\mathbb{Q}_\ell[\![G_k]\!]$-modulesGalois representation; Frobenius eigenvalues at unramified primes
Crystalline $\omega_{\mathrm{cris}}$filtered $\varphi$-modules over $W(\mathbb{F}_q)$$H^*_{\mathrm{cris}}(X_{\mathbb{F}_q}/W)$ with Frobenius and connection

The comparison isomorphisms tie them together. For $X / \mathbb{Q}$ with $X_\mathbb{C}$ smooth projective:

$$H^*_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/\mathbb{Q}) \otimes_\mathbb{Q} \mathbb{C} \;\cong\; H^*_B(X(\mathbb{C}); \mathbb{Q}) \otimes_\mathbb{Q} \mathbb{C} \quad(\text{Grothendieck's comparison theorem}),$$

and the $\ell$-adic Tate-comparison $H^*_B(X(\mathbb{C}); \mathbb{Q}) \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell \cong H^*_{\et}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}; \mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ (Artin), and Fontaine's $p$-adic comparison $B_{\mathrm{cris}} \otimes H^*_{\mathrm{cris}} \cong B_{\mathrm{cris}} \otimes H^*_{\et}(X; \mathbb{Q}_p)$ for varieties with good reduction at $p$.

Whatever your favorite cohomology theory is, it factors through the same motivic data; the realizations are the windows you observe it through.

8. The standard conjectures and the road forward

Many of the structural assertions used above are still conjectures. The standard conjectures on algebraic cycles (Grothendieck, 1968) are the technical specifications motives need; they are open in general and have been for sixty years.

The classical formulation is in three pieces:

  • Lefschetz standard conjecture (B). For $X$ smooth projective, the inverse of the Hard Lefschetz isomorphism $L^{n-i}\colon H^i \to H^{2n-i}$ is induced by an algebraic correspondence.
  • Künneth standard conjecture (C). The Künneth components of the diagonal in $H^*(X \times X)$ are algebraic.
  • Hodge standard conjecture (I). The bilinear form on the primitive part of $H^*(X; \mathbb{Q})$ is positive-definite — a "polarisation" property.

Modulo (B) + (C), homological and numerical equivalence agree, $\mathsf{Mot}_k^{\mathrm{hom}}$ becomes Tannakian, and the motivic Galois group exists in the form described in §6. Without (B) + (C), the picture survives as a robust framework but the formalism is conjectural.

Open since 1968. Lefschetz (B), Künneth (C), Hodge (I) are all known in characteristic 0 only for very specific classes — abelian varieties, surfaces, complete intersections of small degree. Even in characteristic $p$, the picture is fragmentary. Nobody has proven the standard conjectures in full generality, and "we don't know how to prove the standard conjectures" is the common one-line summary of why motives remain conjectural.

What's known unconditionally? A surprising amount.

  • Numerical motives are abelian semisimple (Jannsen, 1992) — without standard conjectures.
  • Mixed Tate motives over $\mathbb{Z}$ have a fully-realised motivic Galois group, computed by Deligne–Goncharov + Brown. Periods include $\zeta(n)$ for $n \geq 2$ and all multiple zeta values; the Galois action is explicit.
  • Mixed Hodge structures form an unconditionally Tannakian category over $\mathbb{Q}$ — Deligne (1971-74). Their motivic Galois group is the Mumford-Tate group of all-of-Hodge-theory, computed for many varieties.
  • Voevodsky's triangulated category $\mathsf{DM}(k)$ (early 2000s) gives a robust mixed-motives theory, sufficient to prove the Bloch–Kato conjecture (Fields Medal work).
  • $\mathbb{A}^1$-homotopy theory (Morel–Voevodsky) embeds motives into a homotopical setting; motivic spectra carry $K$-theory, motivic cobordism, motivic cohomology as objects of one $\infty$-category.

Connections

Motives sit at the intersection of nearly every modern arithmetic-geometric program. The standard conjectures remain open, but the framework already organizes the L-functions of arithmetic geometry, predicts every cohomology-theoretic identity that has ever been observed, and is conjecturally the correct setting for the Langlands philosophy and the Hodge / Tate conjectures.

  • Étale cohomology — the $\ell$-adic realization. The Weil conjectures are the first place where the motivic picture cashed out concretely.
  • Algebraic de Rham + Hodge filtration — the de Rham realization; the Hodge filtration is part of the data that pure motives carry.
  • Galois representations — the $\ell$-adic realization of motives gives the Galois reps of arithmetic geometry; the Langlands program pairs these with automorphic forms.
  • L-functions — every motive has an $L$-function $L(M, s) := \prod_p \det(1 - \mathrm{Frob}_p \cdot p^{-s} \mid M_\ell^{I_p})^{-1}$; the analytic-continuation + functional-equation properties are conjecturally automatic from the motivic data (Hasse-Weil).
  • Tannakian formalism — the foundational tool: a category equivalent to representations of an invisible group.
