Algebraic K-theory foundations

From $K_0$ as the Grothendieck group of projective modules, through $K_1$ and $K_2$ encoding stable invertible matrices and Steinberg relations, to Quillen's Q-construction and the localization long exact sequence — homotopy-theoretic invariants of rings, with applications to topology, arithmetic, and motives.

1. $K_0$ of a ring: the Grothendieck group of projective modules

Algebraic K-theory begins with a single question: what should the "stable" classification of projective modules look like? Given a ring $R$ and the abelian monoid $\mathrm{Proj}(R)$ of isomorphism classes of finitely generated projective $R$-modules under direct sum, $K_0(R)$ is its group completion — formally, the universal abelian group equipped with a homomorphism from $\mathrm{Proj}(R)$.

Concretely: $K_0(R)$ is the free abelian group on isomorphism classes $[P]$ modulo the relation $[P\oplus Q]=[P]+[Q]$. Every element has the form $[P]-[Q]$ (a formal difference), and $[P]=[Q]$ in $K_0(R)$ iff there exists $n$ with $P\oplus R^n\cong Q\oplus R^n$ — the "stable" identification, weaker than isomorphism.

Three landmark cases:

  • $R$ a field $k$: every $k$-module is free, classified by dimension, so $K_0(k)\cong\mathbb{Z}$ via $[V]\mapsto\dim_k V$.
  • $R$ a PID (e.g. $\mathbb{Z}$): every f.g. projective is free, so $K_0(R)\cong\mathbb{Z}$ via rank — same answer for the same reason.
  • $R$ a Dedekind domain: every f.g. projective is $R^{n-1}\oplus I$ for some fractional ideal $I$, with $I_1\oplus I_2\cong R\oplus(I_1 I_2)$. The class group $\mathrm{Cl}(R)$ records non-principal ideals, and $K_0(R)\cong\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathrm{Cl}(R)$. For $R=\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$ the class group is $\mathbb{Z}/2$, generated by the non-principal ideal $(2,1+\sqrt{-5})$.

The same construction recovers vector bundles on a topological space (giving topological $K^0$), locally free coherent sheaves on a scheme, and the class group in number theory. K-theory is the universal home for "objects you can add but not always cancel."

2. $K_1$ of a ring: stable invertible matrices

$K_1$ asks: how should one classify invertible matrices over $R$, stably and up to "elementary" moves? Inside $GL_n(R)$ sits the elementary subgroup $E_n(R)$, generated by transvections $I+r\,e_{ij}$ for $i\ne j$ and $r\in R$ — the row operations that don't change the determinant or the abstract structure.

Take the direct limit along the upper-left embeddings $GL_n(R)\hookrightarrow GL_{n+1}(R)$, $A\mapsto\mathrm{diag}(A,1)$. The result is $GL(R)=\varinjlim_n GL_n(R)$, the stable general linear group. Its elementary subgroup $E(R)=\varinjlim E_n(R)$ enjoys a remarkable property — Whitehead's lemma: $E(R)$ is precisely the commutator subgroup of $GL(R)$, and is itself perfect ($E(R)=[E(R),E(R)]$). Each elementary matrix is a commutator: $I+a\,e_{ij}=[I+a\,e_{ik},I+e_{kj}]$ for any third index $k$.

Define $K_1(R) = GL(R)^{\mathrm{ab}} = GL(R)/E(R)$.

For commutative $R$ the determinant gives a split surjection $\det\colon K_1(R)\to R^\times$, so $K_1(R) = R^\times \oplus SK_1(R)$. The kernel $SK_1(R)$ vanishes for fields (Whitehead determinant theorem) and Dedekind domains (Bass-Milnor-Serre), but not in general — $SK_1(\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}])\ne 0$ already. The Whitehead group $\mathrm{Wh}(G) = K_1(\mathbb{Z}[G])/(\pm G^{\mathrm{ab}})$ houses the s-cobordism obstruction in surgery theory.

3. $K_2$ of a ring: the Steinberg relations

The Whitehead lemma identifies $E(R)$ with $[GL(R),GL(R)]$ but says nothing about hidden relations among the elementary matrices themselves. The same elementary matrices $e_{ij}(r) = I + r\,e_{ij}\in E(R)\subset GL_n(R)$ from §2 — abstracted away from any particular embedding — are the Steinberg generators, written $x_{ij}(r)$. The Steinberg group $\mathrm{St}(R)$ is the abstract group on these generators subject only to the obvious commutator relations:

  • $x_{ij}(r)\cdot x_{ij}(s) = x_{ij}(r+s)$ (additivity in the entry);
  • $[x_{ij}(r), x_{kl}(s)] = 1$ when $j\ne k$ and $i\ne l$ (disjoint indices commute);
  • $[x_{ij}(r), x_{jk}(s)] = x_{ik}(rs)$ when $i\ne k$ (Witt-style commutator).

There is a canonical surjection $\pi\colon\mathrm{St}(R)\twoheadrightarrow E(R)$ killing nothing extra. Define

$$K_2(R) := \ker(\pi) = Z(\mathrm{St}(R)) \subset \mathrm{St}(R) \twoheadrightarrow E(R).$$

Equivalently, $K_2(R)$ is the kernel of the universal central extension of $E(R)$. It captures the relations that hold in $E(R)$ but are NOT consequences of the Steinberg relations alone.

Matsumoto's theorem: for a field $F$, $K_2(F)$ is the abelian group on symbols $\{a,b\}$, $a,b\in F^\times$, modulo bilinearity ($\{aa',b\}=\{a,b\}\{a',b\}$) and the Steinberg relation $\{a,1-a\}=1$. So a $K_2(F)$ class is a symbol $\{a,b\}$ — the universal "two-variable cocycle" on the units.

Concrete computations:

  • $K_2(\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}/2$, generated by $\{-1,-1\}$ (Tate, via the localization sequence).
  • For each prime $p$, the tame symbol (the residue map $\partial_p\colon K_2(\mathbb{Q})\to\mathbb{F}_p^\times$ in the localization sequence) extracts the residue $\overline{(-1)^{v_p(a)v_p(b)}\cdot a^{v_p(b)}/b^{v_p(a)}}\bmod p$.
  • The Hilbert symbol $(a,b)_p\in\{\pm 1\}$ — the local norm-residue symbol that is $+1$ iff $a$ is a norm from $\mathbb{Q}_p(\sqrt{b})$ — is a $K_2$-symbol, and quadratic reciprocity reduces to a single $K_2$-relation in $K_2(\mathbb{Q})$.

4. Quillen's Q-construction and higher K-theory

The bottom three K-groups have explicit algebraic descriptions: $K_0$ via projectives, $K_1$ via stable invertibles, $K_2$ via Steinberg symbols. But what is $K_3$? Or $K_n$ for general $n$? Quillen's Q-construction gives a uniform answer.

For an exact category $\mathcal{E}$ — an additive category with a notion of admissible monomorphisms ($\hookrightarrow$) and admissible epimorphisms ($\twoheadrightarrow$) such that "short exact sequences" are well-defined — define the category $Q\mathcal{E}$:

  • Objects: same as $\mathcal{E}$.
  • Morphisms $X\to Y$: equivalence classes of zigzags $X\twoheadleftarrow Z\hookrightarrow Y$ — first an admissible epi, then an admissible mono. Compose by pulling back.

The classifying space $BQ\mathcal{E}$ is then a topological space, and

$$K_n(\mathcal{E}) := \pi_{n+1}(BQ\mathcal{E}, *).$$

The shift by 1 is because $Q\mathcal{E}$ is non-trivial in low degree: $\pi_1(BQ\mathcal{E}) \cong K_0(\mathcal{E})$, so $K_0$ becomes the fundamental group, $K_1$ becomes $\pi_2$, etc.

For a ring $R$, applying Quillen's construction to the exact category $\mathcal{P}(R)$ of finitely generated projective $R$-modules gives the algebraic K-theory of $R$:

$$K_n(R) := K_n(\mathcal{P}(R)) = \pi_{n+1}(BQ\mathcal{P}(R)).$$

An equivalent definition uses the plus-construction: $K_n(R) = \pi_n(BGL(R)^+)$ for $n\ge 1$. Quillen's plus-equals-Q theorem identifies the two: both compute the same homotopy invariants. Waldhausen later extended the framework to categories with cofibrations and weak equivalences (the $S_\bullet$-construction), allowing K-theory of derived/triangulated categories and chain complexes.

5. The localization long exact sequence

Higher K-theory becomes computable through a single core mechanism: Quillen's localization theorem. Given a ring $R$ and a multiplicative subset $S\subset R$, there is a long exact sequence

$$\cdots \to K_{n+1}(S^{-1}R) \to K_n(\mathcal{H}_S(R)) \to K_n(R) \to K_n(S^{-1}R) \to K_{n-1}(\mathcal{H}_S(R)) \to \cdots$$

where $\mathcal{H}_S(R)$ is the exact category of finitely generated $S$-torsion $R$-modules of finite Tor-dimension. The connecting maps are residue maps: they detect "boundary behavior" of K-theory under localization.

Applied to the canonical localization $\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Q}$ (where $S=\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$, so $\mathcal{H}_S(\mathbb{Z})$ is the category of finite abelian groups, which by Quillen devissage is $\bigoplus_p\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{F}_p)$), the sequence reads:

$$\cdots \to K_n(\mathbb{Z}) \to K_n(\mathbb{Q}) \to \bigoplus_p K_{n-1}(\mathbb{F}_p) \to K_{n-1}(\mathbb{Z}) \to K_{n-1}(\mathbb{Q}) \to \cdots$$

Computing the bottom degrees:

  • $n=0$: $K_0(\mathbb{Z})\to K_0(\mathbb{Q})$ is $\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}$, both rank, an isomorphism. The residue map $K_1(\mathbb{Q})\to\bigoplus_p K_0(\mathbb{F}_p)\cong\bigoplus_p\mathbb{Z}$ is the $p$-adic valuation.
  • $n=1$: kernel of $K_1(\mathbb{Q})\to\bigoplus_p\mathbb{Z}$ is $\{a\in\mathbb{Q}^\times : v_p(a)=0\;\forall p\} = \mathbb{Z}^\times = \{\pm 1\} = K_1(\mathbb{Z})$.
  • $n=2$: $K_2(\mathbb{Q})\to\bigoplus_p K_1(\mathbb{F}_p) = \bigoplus_p\mathbb{F}_p^\times$ is Tate's tame symbol map; kernel is $K_2(\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}/2$.

For the higher $K_n(\mathbb{Z})$, Quillen computed $K_n(\mathbb{F}_q)$ explicitly for finite fields, and Borel showed

$$K_n(\mathcal{O}_F)\otimes\mathbb{Q} \cong \begin{cases} \mathbb{Q}^{r_1+r_2-1}&n=1\\ 0 & n\equiv 0\pmod 4, n\ge 2\\ \mathbb{Q}^{r_1+r_2} & n\equiv 1\pmod 4, n\ge 5\\ 0 & n\equiv 2\pmod 4\\ \mathbb{Q}^{r_2} & n\equiv 3\pmod 4\end{cases}$$

linking K-theory ranks of rings of integers to special values of Dedekind zeta functions $\zeta_F$.

6. Applications: Bass-Quillen, Wall finiteness, motivic K-theory

Algebraic K-theory's structural results turn into obstruction theories across topology, ring theory, and arithmetic. Three flagship applications:

Bass-Quillen: extension over polynomial rings

Serre conjectured (1955) that every finitely generated projective module over $k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ is free. Quillen and Suslin independently proved this in 1976. The Bass-Quillen extension is more general: for any regular Noetherian ring $R$, every f.g. projective $R[t]$-module is extended from $R$ — of the form $P\otimes_R R[t]$ for some f.g. projective $P$. Equivalently, $K_0(R[t])\cong K_0(R)$. The proof uses Quillen patching: a projective $R[t]$-module that becomes extended after each localization $R[t]_\mathfrak{m}$ ($\mathfrak{m}$ a maximal ideal of $R$) is already extended.

Wall finiteness obstruction

A topological space $X$ is finitely dominated if it's a retract of a finite CW complex up to homotopy. Wall's theorem (1965): $X$ is homotopy equivalent to a finite CW complex iff a class

$$w(X) \in \widetilde{K}_0(\mathbb{Z}[\pi_1 X])$$

vanishes. Here $\widetilde{K}_0$ is the reduced (kernel-of-augmentation) version. The obstruction $w(X)$ is the Euler class of the chain complex of the universal cover. For $\pi_1=\{e\}$, $\widetilde{K}_0(\mathbb{Z})=0$, so simply-connected finitely-dominated spaces are finite — a coherent topological consequence. For $\pi_1=C_p$ a cyclic group of large enough prime order, $\widetilde{K}_0(\mathbb{Z}[C_p])$ contains the cyclotomic class group and the obstruction can be nonzero.

Motivic K-theory and Beilinson-Lichtenbaum

Voevodsky's resolution (2011) of the Bloch-Kato conjecture proved the Beilinson-Lichtenbaum conjecture relating algebraic K-theory of a smooth variety $X$ to motivic cohomology:

$$K_n(X)_{(\ell)} \cong \bigoplus_p H^{2p-n}_M(X,\mathbb{Z}_{(\ell)}(p))$$

after $\ell$-localization, where $H^*_M$ is motivic cohomology and $\mathbb{Z}(p)$ is Voevodsky's motivic coefficient sheaf. The bridge from $K^M_n(F)$ (Milnor K-theory of a field) to étale cohomology $H^n_{\text{ét}}(F,\mu_\ell^{\otimes n})$ realizes a longstanding dream: K-theory and arithmetic geometry speak the same language at the chromatic-prime level.

7. Connections

Algebraic K-theory is the homotopy-theoretic universal target for "objects you can add but only stably classify." Open frontiers include the K-theoretic Beilinson conjectures (special values of $L$-functions vs. K-theory ranks), the trace methods program (Hesselholt-Madsen's TC/THH approximating $K(R)$ via cyclotomic spectra), the chromatic perspective via $K(n)$-local stable homotopy (which sees algebraic K-theory of finite fields as a periodic shadow), and Lurie's $\infty$-categorical reformulation that merges Quillen's Q with Waldhausen's $S_\bullet$ into a single universal property.

  • Homological algebra — projective modules — the input category $\mathcal{P}(R)$ whose Grothendieck group is $K_0(R)$.
  • Derived categories — $D(\mathcal{A})$ — natural setting for Waldhausen's $S_\bullet$, and where K-theory of complexes lives.
  • Group cohomology — Galois cohomology & the Brauer group — $H^2(k,\bar{k}^\times)=\mathrm{Br}(k)$ relates to $K_2$ via the Merkurjev-Suslin theorem.
  • K-theory — Algebraic K-theory $K_n(R)$ — the geometric/topological cousin where the Bott periodicity lives.
  • Quadratic reciprocity — Hilbert symbols — $K_2$-symbols give a streamlined derivation of reciprocity laws.
  • Elliptic curves — Mordell-Weil — heights and L-function ranks connect to $K$-theoretic Beilinson conjectures.
