Homotopy theory

Where algebraic topology stops at $\pi_1$ and homology, homotopy theory keeps going: $\pi_n(X)=[(S^n,*),(X,*)]$ for every $n$, the long exact sequence of a fibration that lets you compute them, the Eilenberg–MacLane spaces $K(G,n)$ that represent ordinary cohomology, and the stable category of spectra in which $\pi_*$ becomes a graded ring. Modern incarnations live in model categories and $\infty$-categories — but the moves you make are still the moves Hopf, Hurewicz, Whitehead, Serre, and Sullivan invented.

Reader's note. This page assumes comfort with the fundamental group and singular homology from Algebraic topology, with cup products from Cohomology and duality, and at least a glance at the simplicial-set perspective from Simplicial sets & the nerve. We focus on the homotopy groups $\pi_n$ — what makes them abelian, how to compute a few, what theorems pin them down, and what stable category they live in.

1. Higher homotopy groups

For a pointed space $(X,x_0)$ and $n\ge 1$, the $n$-th homotopy group is

$\pi_n(X,x_0) \;=\; [(S^n,*),\,(X,x_0)] \;=\; \{f\colon S^n\to X\mid f(*)=x_0\}/\!\simeq_{\!*}$

— based homotopy classes of based maps from the $n$-sphere. For $n=1$ this is the fundamental group; for $n\ge 2$ it is a richer invariant that detects "$n$-dimensional holes" in a way homology cannot always see.

The group operation and abelianness

Two based maps $f,g\colon S^n\to X$ are concatenated by composing with the pinch map $S^n\to S^n\vee S^n$ (collapse an equator). For $n=1$ this is loop concatenation; for $n\ge 2$ the same construction works in any of the $n$ available "axes" — and they all give the same group operation.

Eckmann–Hilton. If a set carries two unital binary operations $\bullet,\circ$ that interchange — $(a\circ b)\bullet(c\circ d)=(a\bullet c)\circ(b\bullet d)$ — and share the same unit, then $\bullet=\circ$ and the operation is commutative. For $n\ge 2$, the $n$-fold loop space $\Omega^n X$ has $n$ distinct compositions (one per coordinate), all unital and pairwise interchanging. So $\pi_n(X)=\pi_0(\Omega^n X)$ is abelian.

Two patterns the table makes visible:

  • $\pi_n(S^n)=\mathbb{Z}$, generated by the identity. The integer attached to a self-map $f\colon S^n\to S^n$ is its Brouwer degree, computable as $\int_{S^n}f^*\omega$ for any normalised volume form (or as the signed count of preimages of a regular value).
  • $\pi_3(S^2)=\mathbb{Z}$ is not small. The generator is the Hopf map $\eta\colon S^3\to S^2$, $(z_1,z_2)\mapsto[z_1:z_2]\in\mathbb{CP}^1=S^2$. Two distinct points of $S^2$ have linked circles as preimages — the linking number is the Hopf invariant.

The long exact sequence of a fibration

A Serre fibration $p\colon E\to B$ is a map with the homotopy lifting property for cubes $I^n$. Pointed Serre fibrations $F\hookrightarrow E\xrightarrow{p}B$ produce the long exact sequence

$\cdots\to\pi_n(F)\to\pi_n(E)\xrightarrow{p_*}\pi_n(B)\xrightarrow{\partial}\pi_{n-1}(F)\to\cdots\to\pi_0(F)\to\pi_0(E)\to\pi_0(B).$

The connecting map $\partial$ takes a map $S^n\to B$, lifts a homotopy with chosen endpoint, and reads off the failure-to-be-fixed at the endpoint as a class in $\pi_{n-1}(F)$. This is the workhorse for computing homotopy groups by induction.

Worked computation: $\pi_n(S^1)$. The exponential $\mathbb{R}\to S^1$, $t\mapsto e^{2\pi i t}$, is a fibration with fibre $\mathbb{Z}$. The LES gives $\pi_n(\mathbb{R})\to\pi_n(S^1)\to\pi_{n-1}(\mathbb{Z})$. For $n\ge 2$ both ends are $0$ (contractible $\mathbb{R}$, discrete $\mathbb{Z}$), so $\pi_n(S^1)=0$. For $n=1$ the connecting map gives the iso $\pi_1(S^1)\cong\pi_0(\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}$.

2. Hurewicz and Whitehead theorems

Homotopy groups are hard to compute directly; homology groups are merciful. The Hurewicz and Whitehead theorems tell us when, and to what extent, $\pi_*$ and $H_*$ determine each other.

Hurewicz: the first non-trivial group is the same on both sides

The Hurewicz map $h\colon\pi_n(X,x_0)\to H_n(X;\mathbb{Z})$ sends a class $[f\colon S^n\to X]$ to $f_*[S^n]$, the image of the fundamental class.

Hurewicz theorem. If $X$ is path-connected and $\pi_i(X)=0$ for every $i

Worked example. $\pi_2(S^2)$: $S^2$ is simply connected, so $\pi_1=0$ and Hurewicz applies in degree $2$. We get $\pi_2(S^2)\cong H_2(S^2;\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}$, generated by the identity. (Note: above degree $n$, Hurewicz says nothing — $\pi_3(S^2)=\mathbb{Z}$ but $H_3(S^2)=0$.)

Whitehead: a map is an equivalence iff it's an iso on all $\pi_n$

Whitehead theorem. Let $f\colon X\to Y$ be a continuous map between connected CW complexes. If $f_*\colon\pi_n(X,x_0)\to\pi_n(Y,f(x_0))$ is an isomorphism for every $n\ge 0$, then $f$ is a homotopy equivalence.

The proof is induction up the cell tower of $Y$, lifting cells one at a time using the homotopy-lifting property of CW pairs.

The Whitehead trap. The CW hypothesis is essential. The Warsaw circle — a closed curve drawn from a topologist's sine curve — has the same trivial homotopy groups as a point ($\pi_n=0$ for all $n$), yet is not contractible. It is not a CW complex, and the local pathology (no neighbourhood basis of nice sets at one accumulation point) blocks the cell-by-cell argument. For arbitrary topological spaces, the right notion is weak equivalence (iso on $\pi_*$); the upgrade to homotopy equivalence requires CW.

The standard fix when $X$ is not a CW complex: apply CW approximation (singular complex composed with geometric realization, or directly the iterated cell-attachment) to replace $X$ by a weakly equivalent CW complex. This is one motivation for working in the Quillen model structure on simplicial sets: every object is cofibrant, and weak equivalences are detected by $|\cdot|$.

Combined: simply connected + acyclic ⟹ contractible

If a CW complex $X$ is simply connected and $\widetilde H_n(X;\mathbb{Z})=0$ for every $n>0$, then by Hurewicz (induction up the connectivity) every $\pi_n(X)=0$. By Whitehead, the unique map $X\to\mathrm{pt}$ is then a homotopy equivalence — $X$ is contractible. This is how you prove things like "a simply-connected manifold with the same homology as a point is a disk" in dimensions where smoothing is automatic.

Hurewicz: π_n vs H_n in low degrees

Hurewicz says $\pi_n=H_n$ at the first non-trivial degree (with $\pi_1\to H_1$ being abelianisation in degree $1$). Above that degree, the two are wildly different: $\pi_3(S^2)=\mathbb{Z}$ but $H_3(S^2)=0$ — the Hopf map is invisible to homology.

3. Eilenberg–MacLane spaces and ordinary cohomology

The Hurewicz / Whitehead apparatus suggests building spaces with prescribed homotopy. The simplest are spaces with homotopy concentrated in a single degree — the Eilenberg–MacLane spaces.

The defining property

For a group $G$ (abelian if $n\ge 2$) and integer $n\ge 1$, an Eilenberg–MacLane space $K(G,n)$ is a connected CW complex with

$\pi_n(K(G,n))=G,\qquad \pi_k(K(G,n))=0\text{ for every }k\ne n.$

Such a space exists for every $(G,n)$ (built by attaching cells to kill higher homotopy, then more cells to fix the lower ones — a Postnikov-style construction) and is unique up to homotopy equivalence (Whitehead).

Brown representability for ordinary cohomology

Theorem. For every CW complex $X$ and every $G,n$: $$H^n(X;G)\;\cong\;[X,K(G,n)]_*$$ naturally in $X$, where $[-,-]_*$ denotes based homotopy classes. The cohomology class $\iota_n\in H^n(K(G,n);G)$ corresponding to $\mathrm{id}\in[K(G,n),K(G,n)]$ is the fundamental class; pulling it back along $f\colon X\to K(G,n)$ recovers $f^*\iota_n\in H^n(X;G)$.

So singular cohomology is not just an algebraic gadget but a representable functor: it is what mapping into $K(G,n)$ measures. Two consequences:

  • Cohomology operations. Natural transformations $H^n(-;G)\to H^m(-;H)$ correspond to homotopy classes $K(G,n)\to K(H,m)$, equivalently to elements of $H^m(K(G,n);H)$. The Steenrod algebra $\mathcal{A}_2 = H^*(K(\mathbb{Z}/2,n);\mathbb{Z}/2)$-stable is the algebra of all mod-2 cohomology operations, and Serre / Cartan computed it explicitly via the Steenrod squares $\mathrm{Sq}^i$.
  • Postnikov tower. Every connected space $X$ admits a tower $\cdots\to X_n\to X_{n-1}\to\cdots\to X_1\to X_0=\mathrm{pt}$ with $X_n\to X$ inducing an iso on $\pi_i$ for $i\le n$ and $\pi_i(X_n)=0$ for $i>n$. Each map $X_n\to X_{n-1}$ is a principal $K(\pi_n X,n)$-fibration classified by a $k$-invariant $k_n\in H^{n+1}(X_{n-1};\pi_n X)$. The whole homotopy type of $X$ is encoded in the sequence $(\pi_n X, k_n)_{n\ge 1}$.

4. Stable homotopy and spectra

The homotopy groups $\pi_{n+k}(S^n)$ are notoriously erratic in $n$ — until you stabilise.

Freudenthal suspension

Freudenthal suspension theorem. The suspension homomorphism $\Sigma\colon\pi_k(S^n)\to\pi_{k+1}(S^{n+1})$ is an isomorphism when $k<2n-1$ and a surjection when $k=2n-1$. So for fixed $k$ the sequence $\pi_{k+n}(S^n)$ stabilises once $n>k+1$, defining the $k$-th stable homotopy group of spheres $$\pi_k^s \;=\; \mathrm{colim}_n\,\pi_{k+n}(S^n).$$

Examples: $\pi_0^s=\mathbb{Z}$ (degree), $\pi_1^s=\mathbb{Z}/2$ (the Hopf class $\eta$ stabilises), $\pi_2^s=\mathbb{Z}/2$ ($\eta^2$), $\pi_3^s=\mathbb{Z}/24$ (generated by the quaternionic Hopf class $\nu$), and so on. Computing $\pi_*^s$ is the central problem of stable homotopy theory; today's frontier reaches into the $90$s and beyond, fueled by the Adams and Adams–Novikov spectral sequences.

Spectra

A (sequential) spectrum $E$ is a sequence of pointed CW complexes $E_0,E_1,E_2,\ldots$ together with structure maps

$\sigma_n\colon\Sigma E_n\to E_{n+1}.$

Examples:

  • Sphere spectrum $\mathbb{S}$. $E_n=S^n$ with $\sigma_n\colon\Sigma S^n=S^{n+1}\xrightarrow{=}S^{n+1}$. Then $\pi_n(\mathbb{S})=\pi_n^s$.
  • Eilenberg–MacLane spectrum $H\mathbb{Z}$. $E_n=K(\mathbb{Z},n)$ with $\sigma_n$ the natural map $\Sigma K(\mathbb{Z},n)\to K(\mathbb{Z},n+1)$ adjoint to a generator of $H^{n+1}(\Sigma K(\mathbb{Z},n);\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}$. Represents singular cohomology: $H^n(X;\mathbb{Z})=H\mathbb{Z}^n(X)$.
  • Thom spectrum $MO$. $E_n=MO(n)$, the Thom space of the universal real $n$-bundle over $BO(n)$. By Thom's theorem, $\pi_*(MO)=\Omega_*^O$ — unoriented bordism.
  • Complex K-theory spectrum $KU$. $E_{2n}=BU\times\mathbb{Z}$, $E_{2n+1}=U$, with structure maps from Bott periodicity. Represents complex topological $K$-theory.

Generalised cohomology theories

For a spectrum $E$ and a CW complex $X$, set

$E^n(X) \;=\; \mathrm{colim}_k\,[\Sigma^k X,\,E_{n+k}]_*.$

The functor $E^*(-)$ satisfies the Eilenberg–Steenrod axioms minus dimension: it is homotopy-invariant, has Mayer–Vietoris, has long exact sequences for pairs, takes wedges to products. Brown representability: any contravariant functor from CW spaces to abelian groups satisfying these axioms is represented by a spectrum, unique up to weak equivalence. So:

generalised cohomology theories $\;\;\xleftrightarrow{\;\;\sim\;\;}\;\;$ spectra (up to weak equivalence).

Different spectra detect different invariants: $H\mathbb{Z}$ sees ordinary cohomology, $KU$ sees vector bundles up to stable equivalence, $MO$ sees bordism class, $tmf$ (topological modular forms) sees something deeper that surfaces in mathematical physics. Stable homotopy theory is the systematic study of this category.

For each fixed $k$, the sequence $\pi_{n+k}(S^n)$ becomes constant for $n>k+1$ (Freudenthal), reaching the stable group $\pi_k^s$. Stable values: $\pi_0^s=\mathbb{Z}$, $\pi_1^s=\mathbb{Z}/2$, $\pi_2^s=\mathbb{Z}/2$, $\pi_3^s=\mathbb{Z}/24$, $\pi_4^s=0$, $\pi_5^s=0$, $\pi_6^s=\mathbb{Z}/2$.

5. Hopf fibration and rational homotopy

The Hopf fibration is the smallest example of an "interesting" higher homotopy class — and Sullivan's theory shows that, after tensoring with $\mathbb{Q}$, all such classes are computable from a piece of commutative algebra.

The Hopf fibration

View $S^3\subset\mathbb{C}^2$ as the unit sphere of two complex variables, and $S^2=\mathbb{CP}^1$ as the projectivisation. The map

$\eta\colon S^3\to S^2,\qquad (z_1,z_2)\mapsto[z_1:z_2]$

is a smooth fibration with fibre the unit circle $\{(\lambda z_1,\lambda z_2):|\lambda|=1\}\cong S^1$. The long exact sequence of the fibration $S^1\to S^3\to S^2$ in degree $3$ reads

$\pi_3(S^1)\;\to\;\pi_3(S^3)\;\to\;\pi_3(S^2)\;\to\;\pi_2(S^1).$

Both ends are $0$, so $\pi_3(S^2)\cong\pi_3(S^3)=\mathbb{Z}$, and the iso sends $[\eta]$ to $1\in\mathbb{Z}$.

Why $\eta$ is non-trivial. The preimages $\eta^{-1}(p),\eta^{-1}(q)$ of two distinct points in $S^2$ are two disjoint great circles in $S^3$ — and they are linked, with linking number $\pm 1$. The Hopf invariant of a map $f\colon S^3\to S^2$ is exactly this linking number; it generates $\pi_3(S^2)=\mathbb{Z}$ and is the obstruction to $f$ being null-homotopic.

Serre's mod-$\mathcal{C}$ theory and rational homotopy of spheres

Computing $\pi_*(S^n)$ exactly is hopeless for now; computing $\pi_*(S^n)\otimes\mathbb{Q}$ is a finite calculation. Serre's theorem (1951):

$n$$\pi_*(S^n)\otimes\mathbb{Q}$
odd $n$$\mathbb{Q}$ in degree $n$, zero elsewhere
even $n$$\mathbb{Q}$ in degree $n$ and degree $2n-1$; zero elsewhere

So $\pi_*(S^3)\otimes\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Q}\cdot[\mathrm{id}]$ in degree $3$ alone; $\pi_*(S^2)\otimes\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Q}\cdot[\mathrm{id}]\oplus\mathbb{Q}\cdot[\eta]$ in degrees $2$ and $3$. The Hopf class is the only non-trivial rational homotopy class of $S^2$ above degree $2$.

Serre's proof uses the path-loop fibration $\Omega S^n\to PS^n\to S^n$ (with $PS^n$ contractible) and the Serre spectral sequence — see Cohomology and duality §5. Iterating gives Postnikov-tower-style computations of $\pi_*(S^n)\otimes\mathbb{Q}$ degree by degree.

Sullivan's rational homotopy theory

Sullivan (1977) made this slogan precise: for a simply-connected space $X$ of finite $\mathbb{Q}$-type, the rational homotopy type of $X$ is encoded in a piece of commutative algebra.

The bridge is the commutative dga of polynomial differential forms $\Omega^*_{\mathrm{PL}}(X;\mathbb{Q})$: a rational variant of de Rham forms on the singular complex of $X$. The cohomology of this dga is $H^*(X;\mathbb{Q})$, and its quasi-isomorphism class as a cdga (commutative differential graded algebra) determines the rational homotopy type.

A minimal Sullivan model of $X$ is a free graded-commutative cdga $(\Lambda V,d)$ with decomposable differential ($d V\subset \Lambda^{\ge 2} V$) together with a quasi-isomorphism $(\Lambda V,d)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Omega^*_{\mathrm{PL}}(X;\mathbb{Q})$. The generators recover homotopy:

$V^n \;\cong\; \mathrm{Hom}(\pi_n(X),\mathbb{Q})\quad(\text{as graded vector spaces}).$

Worked example: minimal model of $S^{2k}$. The cohomology $H^*(S^{2k};\mathbb{Q})=\mathbb{Q}[x]/(x^2)$ with $|x|=2k$ would suggest $V$ has a single generator $x$ in degree $2k$ — but then $d(x^2)=2x\cdot dx=0$ would force $x^2$ to live, contradicting $x^2=0$ in cohomology. The fix is to introduce a second generator $y$ of degree $4k-1$ with $dy=x^2$. Then $(\Lambda(x,y),d)$ has cohomology $\mathbb{Q}[x]/(x^2)$ — exactly $H^*(S^{2k})$. The two generators read off rational homotopy: $\pi_{2k}(S^{2k})\otimes\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Q}$ and $\pi_{4k-1}(S^{2k})\otimes\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Q}$, recovering Serre's calculation.

6. Loop spaces and Bott periodicity

The based loop space $\Omega X = \mathrm{Map}_*(S^1,X)$ is the homotopy-theoretic analogue of "shifting down one degree." Its homotopy is

$\pi_n(\Omega X)\;=\;\pi_{n+1}(X),$

since a map $S^n\to\Omega X$ adjoints to a map $S^n\wedge S^1=S^{n+1}\to X$. Iterating gives $\Omega^k X$ with $\pi_n(\Omega^k X)=\pi_{n+k}(X)$.

Iterated loop spaces are $E_\infty$

$\Omega X$ is an $H$-space (concatenation of loops, associative up to homotopy). $\Omega^2 X$ has two compatible compositions and is therefore homotopy-commutative (Eckmann–Hilton again). In general $\Omega^n X$ has the structure of an $E_n$-algebra, encoded by the little $n$-cubes operad $\mathcal{C}_n$. As $n\to\infty$ the operadic structure approaches $E_\infty$ — the homotopy-coherent commutative monoid structure that infinite loop spaces and the corresponding spectra carry.

Conversely, for a spectrum $E$ the infinite loop space $\Omega^\infty E = \mathrm{colim}_n\,\Omega^n E_n$ is an $E_\infty$-space, and the $\Omega^\infty\dashv\Sigma^\infty$ adjunction connects spaces and spectra. This perspective puts spectra at the heart of stable homotopy and structured ring theory.

Bott periodicity

The most striking phenomenon in classical loop-space theory: looping the stable orthogonal or unitary group eight or two times respectively recovers the same space.

Bott periodicity (1959). $$\Omega^2\,\mathrm{U}\;\simeq\;\mathrm{U}\times\mathbb{Z},\qquad \Omega^8\,\mathrm{O}\;\simeq\;\mathrm{O}\times\mathbb{Z}.$$ Equivalently, $\pi_n\mathrm{U}$ is $2$-periodic and $\pi_n\mathrm{O}$ is $8$-periodic in $n$.

James's splitting

For a connected space $X$, James's theorem identifies the homotopy type of the suspension of a loop space:

$\Sigma\Omega\Sigma X\;\simeq\;\bigvee_{n\ge 1}\Sigma X^{\wedge n},$

a wedge of suspensions of smash powers of $X$. So $\Omega\Sigma X$, the James construction, is built combinatorially out of $X$. This is the prototype for splitting results that compute the stable homotopy of classifying spaces and configuration spaces.

Connections

Homotopy theory is the engine room of modern topology. Beyond what is on this page, the open frontiers are: the chromatic filtration of stable homotopy by formal-group height (Morava $K$-theories, $tmf$, the $K(n)$-local sphere); structured ring spectra and their $\infty$-categorical foundations (Lurie's Higher Algebra); equivariant homotopy theory and its interplay with algebraic $K$-theory and motivic cohomology; and the slow accumulation of computations of $\pi_*^s$, where each new stem opens new Adams-spectral-sequence questions.

  • Algebraic topology — singular homology — the comparison map $\pi_n(X)\to H_n(X)$ is the Hurewicz map of §2; homology is the linearisation of homotopy.
  • Cohomology and duality — Serre spectral sequence — the workhorse for computing $\pi_*$ from a fibration; Serre used it for his calculations of $\pi_*(S^n)\otimes\mathbb{Q}$.
  • Model categories — Quillen structures on spaces and simplicial sets — the formal apparatus that lets you do homotopy theory in any category: weak equivalences, fibrations, cofibrations, plus Quillen-equivalent presentations of "the same" homotopy theory.
  • $\infty$-categories — the modern foundation: instead of model-categorical replacements, work directly with quasi-categories. Fibrations, limits, adjunctions all upgrade.
  • Cobordism — Thom spectrum $MO$ and K-theory — Bott periodicity — two of the canonical spectra of §4 living their own lives as generalised cohomology theories.
