Heegaard Floer & knot Floer homology

Every closed 3-manifold cuts along a surface into two handlebodies; lift those handlebodies into a symplectic stage built from the symmetric product of the splitting surface, count pseudoholomorphic disks between two Lagrangian tori, and out drops a powerful family of invariants $\widehat{HF},\;HF^+,\;HF^-,\;HF^\infty$. Restrict to a knot complement and you categorify the Alexander polynomial. The $d$-invariant obstructs rational-homology cobordism; the $\tau$ invariant gives a smooth slice-genus bound that no classical knot polynomial can see.

Reader's note. This page leans on three different threads of background. The 3-manifold side requires handle decompositions and the language of surgery. The Floer side requires Lagrangian submanifolds and the moduli of pseudoholomorphic curves; we treat the latter as a black-box counting machine. The knot side picks up where Alexander and Khovanov homology left off — Heegaard Floer is to Alexander what Khovanov is to Jones.

1. Heegaard splittings and diagrams

A genus-$g$ handlebody $U_g$ is a regular neighbourhood of a bouquet of $g$ circles in $\mathbb{R}^3$ — equivalently, $D^3$ with $g$ 1-handles attached. Its boundary $\partial U_g = \Sigma_g$ is the closed orientable surface of genus $g$.

Theorem (Moise 1952 / classical). Every closed orientable 3-manifold $Y$ admits a Heegaard splitting $$Y \;=\; U_g \;\cup_{\Sigma_g}\; U_g',$$ the union of two handlebodies of the same genus $g$, glued by an orientation-reversing diffeomorphism of their common boundary $\Sigma_g$.

The integer $g$ depends on the splitting: $S^3$ has genus-0 (and genus-$g$ for every $g\ge 0$) splittings; the Heegaard genus is the minimum. The 3-torus $T^3$ has Heegaard genus $3$. Lens spaces $L(p,q)$ have Heegaard genus $1$ — two solid tori glued by $\begin{pmatrix}p&\bullet\\q&\bullet\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$.

Heegaard diagrams

Rather than draw the gluing map explicitly we encode it on the splitting surface. A Heegaard diagram for $Y$ is a triple $(\Sigma_g,\boldsymbol\alpha,\boldsymbol\beta)$:

  • $\Sigma_g$ — the splitting surface;
  • $\boldsymbol\alpha = \{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_g\}$ — pairwise disjoint simple closed curves on $\Sigma_g$ bounding disks in $U_g$ (an $\alpha$-system);
  • $\boldsymbol\beta = \{\beta_1,\dots,\beta_g\}$ — pairwise disjoint simple closed curves bounding disks in $U_g'$.

Either system alone determines its handlebody up to diffeomorphism; the pair together determines $Y$. Stabilisation $(\Sigma_g,\boldsymbol\alpha,\boldsymbol\beta)\rightsquigarrow(\Sigma_{g+1},\boldsymbol\alpha\cup\{\alpha_{g+1}\},\boldsymbol\beta\cup\{\beta_{g+1}\})$ — adding a trivial handle and a canceling pair of curves — and curve isotopies/handleslides are the elementary equivalences; Reidemeister-Singer says any two diagrams of $Y$ are related by a sequence of these.

2. Lagrangian Floer homology

Heegaard Floer homology is the application of Lagrangian Floer homology — Floer's chain complex generated by intersection points of two Lagrangian submanifolds — to a very specific pair of tori inside a very specific symplectic manifold. Before the special case, the general construction.

Let $(X^{2n},\omega)$ be a symplectic manifold and $L_0,L_1\subset X$ two transversely intersecting compact Lagrangian submanifolds (so $\dim L_i = n$ and $\omega|_{L_i}=0$). The Floer chain complex $CF(L_0,L_1)$ is the free $\mathbb{Z}/2$-vector space on the finite intersection set $L_0\cap L_1$. The differential $$\partial\colon CF(L_0,L_1) \;\longrightarrow\; CF(L_0,L_1)$$ counts pseudoholomorphic Whitney disks: maps $u\colon (\mathbb{D},\partial^-\mathbb{D},\partial^+\mathbb{D})\to (X,L_0,L_1)$ with boundary arcs running on $L_0,L_1$ and $\partial_s u + J(u)\partial_t u = 0$ for a chosen $\omega$-compatible almost complex structure $J$. The coefficient $\langle\partial x,y\rangle$ is the mod-2 count of finite-energy, index-1 such disks from $x$ to $y$, modulo the $\mathbb{R}$-translation of the strip.

The miracle. Under suitable monotonicity / orientability hypotheses on $(X,L_0,L_1)$, $\partial^2 = 0$ and the resulting homology $HF(L_0,L_1)$ is invariant under Hamiltonian isotopy of either Lagrangian. The dimension of $HF(L_0,L_1)$ bounds the geometric intersection number of any pair Hamiltonian-isotopic to $(L_0,L_1)$ — the celebrated Arnold conjecture.

Heuristically: $\partial^2 = 0$ because the boundary of a 1-parameter family of holomorphic strips is itself a count of broken strips (= compositions $\partial\circ\partial$), which appear in canceling pairs from a Gromov-compactness argument. The technical heart is the analysis — transversality, compactness, gluing — formalised in Floer's 1988 papers.

3. Heegaard Floer homology and its variants

Apply Lagrangian Floer homology to a Heegaard diagram. The symplectic stage is the symmetric product $$\mathrm{Sym}^g(\Sigma_g) \;=\; \Sigma_g^{\times g} \,/\, S_g,$$ a $2g$-real-dimensional space whose smooth structure comes from regarding $\Sigma_g$ as a Riemann surface and unordered $g$-tuples of points as effective degree-$g$ divisors. The two Lagrangians are $$\mathbb{T}_\alpha \;=\; \alpha_1\times\cdots\times\alpha_g \,/\, S_g, \qquad \mathbb{T}_\beta \;=\; \beta_1\times\cdots\times\beta_g \,/\, S_g.$$ Each is a $g$-dimensional torus inside $\mathrm{Sym}^g(\Sigma_g)$. A generator of the chain complex is a $g$-tuple of intersection points, one $\alpha_i\cap\beta_{\sigma(i)}$ for some permutation $\sigma$. Define the chain complex over a polynomial ring $\mathbb{F}_2[U]$ — each holomorphic disk's $U$-power is its multiplicity at a marked basepoint $z\in\Sigma_g\setminus(\alpha\cup\beta)$.

Four flavors arise from how one localises in $U$:

flavorcoefficient ringwhat it sees
$\widehat{HF}(Y)$$\mathbb{F}_2$ (set $U=0$)Most computable; rank lower bound on the splitting's intersection
$HF^-(Y)$$\mathbb{F}_2[U]$The "minus" tower; carries torsion that decorates the geometry
$HF^+(Y)$$\mathbb{F}_2[U,U^{-1}]/U\mathbb{F}_2[U]$The "plus" tower; a $\mathbb{Z}[U^{-1}]$-module with the $d$-invariant at its bottom
$HF^\infty(Y)$$\mathbb{F}_2[U,U^{-1}]$"Infinity" version; for $\mathbb{Q}$-homology spheres always $\mathbb{F}_2[U,U^{-1}]^{2^{b_1}}$

They fit in a long exact sequence $$\cdots\to HF^-(Y)\xrightarrow{\cdot U}HF^-(Y)\to\widehat{HF}(Y)\to\cdots$$ plus a parallel sequence relating $HF^+$ and $HF^\infty$. Choice of $\mathrm{Spin}^c$-structure $\mathfrak{s}\in\mathrm{Spin}^c(Y)$ decomposes each variant: a generator $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}_\alpha\cap\mathbb{T}_\beta$ determines a $\mathrm{Spin}^c$-structure, and the differential preserves it.

4. Knot Floer homology

Restrict to the special case $Y=S^3$ and add a knot $K\subset S^3$. A doubly-pointed Heegaard diagram $(\Sigma_g,\boldsymbol\alpha,\boldsymbol\beta,z,w)$ for $(S^3,K)$ has two basepoints positioned so that the arc on $\Sigma_g$ joining them (avoiding $\alpha,\beta$) traces $K$. Ozsváth–Szabó (and independently Rasmussen) defined: $$\widehat{HFK}(K) \;=\; H_*\bigl( CF(\mathbb{T}_\alpha,\mathbb{T}_\beta) \;\text{with the }(z,w)\text{ filtration}\bigr).$$ The result is bigraded: a homological grading $M$ (Maslov) and an Alexander grading $A$ recording the relative multiplicity of $z,w$ in disk counts.

Theorem (Ozsváth–Szabó). Knot Floer homology categorifies the Alexander polynomial: $$\Delta_K(t) \;=\; \sum_{(M,A)} (-1)^M\,\dim_{\mathbb{F}_2}\widehat{HFK}_{M}(K,A)\;t^A.$$

The Euler characteristic recovers Alexander, but the homology itself is strictly more powerful. Knot Floer detects fiberedness (a knot is fibered $\iff$ the top Alexander summand $\widehat{HFK}(K,g(K))$ has rank 1, where $g(K)$ is the Seifert genus — Ghiggini, Ni). It detects the Seifert genus exactly ($\max\{A:\widehat{HFK}(K,A)\ne 0\} = g(K)$). It detects the unknot ($\widehat{HFK}(K)\cong\mathbb{F}_2$ in bigrading $(0,0)$ iff $K$ is the unknot). None of these are visible from Alexander alone.

5. The $d$-invariant (correction term)

For a rational homology sphere $Y$ (a closed 3-manifold with $H_*(Y;\mathbb{Q})=H_*(S^3;\mathbb{Q})$) and a $\mathrm{Spin}^c$-structure $\mathfrak{s}\in\mathrm{Spin}^c(Y)$, the group $HF^+(Y,\mathfrak{s})$ is finitely generated over $\mathbb{F}_2[U]$ and has a "tower" $\mathcal{T}^+ = \mathbb{F}_2[U,U^{-1}]/U\mathbb{F}_2[U]\subset HF^+(Y,\mathfrak{s})$. The $d$-invariant (or correction term) is the grading of the bottom of this tower: $$d(Y,\mathfrak{s}) \;=\; \min\{\,\text{grading of a }U^{-k}\text{-element in the }\mathcal{T}^+\text{ summand}\,\}\;\in\;\mathbb{Q}.$$ This is a rational-valued invariant of $(Y,\mathfrak{s})$ defined by Ozsváth–Szabó (2003).

Cobordism formula. If $W$ is a negative-definite 4-manifold with $\partial W = Y$ and $\mathfrak{t}\in\mathrm{Spin}^c(W)$ restricts to $\mathfrak{s}$, then $$d(Y,\mathfrak{s}) \;\ge\; \frac{c_1(\mathfrak{t})^2 + b_2(W)}{4}.$$ Hence $d$ obstructs $Y$ from bounding a rational homology ball: if $Y$ does bound such a ball, $d(Y,\mathfrak{s})=0$ for some $\mathfrak{s}$.

6. Surgery exact triangle and cobordism maps

Given a knot $K\subset Y$ and a framing $n\in\mathbb{Z}$, the manifolds $Y$, $Y_n(K)$ (Dehn surgery with framing $n$), and $Y_{n+1}(K)$ fit into a surgery exact triangle in Heegaard Floer homology: $$\widehat{HF}(Y)\;\longrightarrow\;\widehat{HF}(Y_n(K))\;\longrightarrow\;\widehat{HF}(Y_{n+1}(K))\;\longrightarrow\;\widehat{HF}(Y)[-1].$$ The maps come from the cobordism functoriality of Heegaard Floer homology: a 4-dimensional cobordism $W\colon Y_0\rightsquigarrow Y_1$ induces a $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear map $$F_{W,\mathfrak{t}}\colon HF^\circ(Y_0,\mathfrak{s}_0)\longrightarrow HF^\circ(Y_1,\mathfrak{s}_1)$$ for each $\mathrm{Spin}^c$-structure $\mathfrak{t}$ on $W$ restricting appropriately. Composition of cobordisms gives composition of maps (up to homotopy).

The triangle is constructed by recognising that $Y$, $Y_n(K)$, $Y_{n+1}(K)$ are the three manifolds obtained by Dehn-filling the knot exterior along three slopes pairwise distance $1$ apart in $H_1(\partial)$. Holomorphic-triangle counts in the symmetric product of a triply-pointed Heegaard surface give the three edge maps, and a holomorphic-rectangle count verifies that the composition vanishes.

7. The $\tau$ invariant and slice-genus bounds

For a knot $K\subset S^3$, the 4-ball genus (or smooth slice genus) $g_4(K)$ is the minimum genus of a smooth oriented surface $\Sigma\subset B^4$ with $\partial\Sigma=K$. It satisfies $g_4(K)\le g(K)$ (Seifert genus) but the inequality is usually strict and $g_4$ is hard to compute. Knot Floer gives a sharp lower bound.

Define the $\tau$-invariant $\tau(K)\in\mathbb{Z}$ as the unique integer such that there is a non-trivial element in the Alexander grading-$\tau(K)$ part of the $U=0$ quotient of $HF^-(K)$ that survives all $U$-tower torsion — concretely: $$\tau(K) \;=\; -\max\{\,A\,:\,\exists\,\xi\in\widehat{HFK}(K,A)\text{ that survives the spectral sequence to }\widehat{HF}(S^3)=\mathbb{F}_2\,\}.$$

Theorem (Ozsváth–Szabó, 2003). The $\tau$-invariant gives a smooth slice-genus bound: $$|\tau(K)| \;\le\; g_4(K).$$ Furthermore $\tau$ is a homomorphism from the smooth knot concordance group $\mathcal{C}$ to $\mathbb{Z}$ and equals the Rasmussen $s$-invariant for many classes of knots — both vanish on slice knots.

For the torus knot $T(p,q)$ with $p,q\ge 2$ coprime: $\tau(T(p,q))=\frac{(p-1)(q-1)}{2}=g_4(T(p,q))$, recovering Kronheimer–Mrowka's resolution of the Milnor conjecture by purely combinatorial means. For an alternating knot $K$, $\tau(K)=-\sigma(K)/2$ (half the signature). Examples where $\tau$ outperforms classical bounds include topologically slice knots with $\tau\ne 0$, certifying that $g_4>0$ even though every $\Delta_K$, signature, and Casson–Gordon invariant vanishes.

4-manifold consequences cascade: combined with Donaldson's diagonalisation theorem, $d$-invariants and $\tau$ rule out smooth bounding manifolds, distinguish exotic smooth structures, and feed into the homology-cobordism group $\Theta^3_{\mathbb{Z}}$. Many of the simplest deep facts about smooth 4-manifolds — that there exist exotic $\mathbb{R}^4$'s, that the Casson invariant alone misses smooth structure subtleties — are detected by the Heegaard Floer correction terms.

Connections

Heegaard Floer homology is now one of the central tools of low-dimensional topology. Active frontiers include the relationship to gauge-theoretic invariants (the Kutluhan–Lee–Taubes isomorphism $HF^+\cong HM$ between Heegaard Floer and monopole Floer is among the deepest results), involutive variants $\underline{HF}$ that refine the homology-cobordism group, and the bordered Heegaard Floer theory that decomposes the invariants under cutting along surfaces. Computability is genuinely hard — automatic tools exist for knots up to ~16 crossings, but closed 3-manifolds remain stubbornly case-by-case.

  • Khovanov homology — the parallel categorification of Jones. Both theories obstruct smooth slice genus; their relationship is still partly conjectural.
  • Exotic 4-spheres & smooth structures — Heegaard Floer plus Donaldson's theorem combine to detect smooth exotica that topological invariants miss.
  • Floer homology preview — the symplectic-side template from which $HF$ is built.
  • 4-manifolds and the Donaldson preview — cobordism maps give the natural 4-manifold-side functoriality.
  • Gauge theory (Seiberg–Witten) — the Kutluhan–Lee–Taubes isomorphism makes the Heegaard side computationally accessible without solving SW equations.
