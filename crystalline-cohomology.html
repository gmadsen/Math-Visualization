Crystalline cohomology

The $p$-adic Weil cohomology that closes the $\ell=p$ gap. Witt-vector coefficients, divided-power thickenings, a $\sigma$-semilinear Frobenius, and the Newton-above-Hodge classification — everything de Rham knows, transported across characteristic.

1. Why crystalline?

Étale cohomology gives a Weil cohomology theory $H^i_{\text{ét}}(X_0,\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ for any prime $\ell$ different from the residue characteristic $p$. Pick $X_0$ smooth proper over $k=\overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$: as $\ell$ ranges over $\{2,3,5,7,\ldots\}\setminus\{p\}$, the Betti numbers, Frobenius eigenvalues, and zeta function all stabilize beautifully across $\ell$. Try $\ell=p$ and the picture collapses. The constant sheaf $\mathbb{Z}/p^n$ on the étale site of a characteristic-$p$ scheme sees Frobenius-kernel pathology: $H^1_{\text{ét}}(\mathbb{A}^1_k,\mathbb{Z}/p)$ is already infinite-dimensional, and middle-degree cohomology of a smooth proper variety can vanish entirely. The $p$-adic étale cohomology is real but it does not compute the Betti numbers.

The gap. $\ell$-adic étale cohomology with $\ell\ne p$ is the right $p$-adic Weil cohomology in every direction except $p$-adically. To compute eigenvalues of Frobenius modulo $p$, or to compare to characteristic-$0$ data via $p$-adic periods, one needs a different theory.

The plan, due to Berthelot building on Grothendieck's "crystals" idea: in characteristic $0$, algebraic de Rham cohomology $H^*_{dR}(X/W)$ of a smooth proper $X$ over the Witt vectors $W=W(k)$ already supplies a free $W$-module of the expected rank with a Frobenius action. So define crystalline cohomology of $X_0$ as the de Rham cohomology of any smooth lift, and prove this is independent of the lift. When no lift exists globally, glue from local lifts using the bookkeeping of divided-power thickenings — that bookkeeping is the crystalline site.

The end result is a cohomology theory $H^*_{\mathrm{cris}}(X_0/W)$ taking values in finitely generated $W$-modules with a $\sigma$-semilinear Frobenius. For smooth proper $X_0$ it is free of rank equal to the Betti number of any complex lift, completes the Weil-cohomology package at $\ell=p$, and refines de Rham with a Newton polygon invisible in characteristic $0$.

2. Divided powers and the crystalline site

The motivating picture for crystalline cohomology is infinitesimal — Grothendieck's idea that an algebraic class on $X_0$ should be a "crystal" extending uniquely along nilpotent thickenings, the way a crystal of salt grows along its facets. To make that picture algebraic in characteristic $p$ one cannot simply take a Taylor series: the denominators $n!$ in $\sum x^n/n!$ are zero modulo $p$ as soon as $n\ge p$. The fix is to axiomatize the operations $\gamma_n(x):=x^n/n!$ as new data, called a divided-power structure or PD-structure (puissances divisées).

PD-structure on an ideal $I\subset A$. A system of maps $\gamma_n\colon I\to A$ for $n\ge 0$ such that for all $x,y\in I$, $\lambda\in A$, and $m,n\ge 0$: $$\gamma_0(x)=1,\quad \gamma_1(x)=x,\quad \gamma_n(x)\in I\text{ for }n\ge 1,$$ $$\gamma_n(\lambda x)=\lambda^n\gamma_n(x),\qquad \gamma_m(x)\gamma_n(x)=\binom{m+n}{m}\gamma_{m+n}(x),$$ $$\gamma_n(x+y)=\sum_{i+j=n}\gamma_i(x)\gamma_j(y),\quad \gamma_n(\gamma_m(x))=\frac{(mn)!}{n!\,(m!)^n}\gamma_{mn}(x).$$ In characteristic 0 these all hold uniquely with $\gamma_n(x)=x^n/n!$; in characteristic $p$ the data $\{\gamma_n\}$ is genuinely extra.

For $W=W(k)$ the kernel of $W\twoheadrightarrow k$ is $pW$, and the standard PD-structure on $pW$ is $\gamma_n(p)=p^n/n!\in pW$ — divisible because of the explicit calculation $v_p(p^n/n!)=n-v_p(n!)\ge 1$ for $n\ge 1$. Crystalline thickenings carry PD-structures compatible with this canonical one.

Crystalline site. Fix $X_0\to\Spec\,k$ smooth, with $k$ a perfect field of characteristic $p$ and $W=W(k)$. The site $(X_0/W)_{\mathrm{cris}}$ has:

  • Objects. Triples $(U,T,\delta)$ where $U\subset X_0$ is Zariski-open, $T$ is a $W$-scheme, $U\hookrightarrow T$ is a closed immersion over $W$, and $\delta$ is a PD-structure on the kernel ideal $\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{O}_T$ compatible with the canonical PD on $pW$.
  • Morphisms. Pairs of compatible morphisms $U'\to U$, $T'\to T$ respecting the PD-structures.
  • Coverings. Zariski coverings of the $T$-piece.

The structure sheaf $\mathcal{O}_{X/W}$ assigns $\Gamma(T,\mathcal{O}_T)$ to the object $(U,T,\delta)$. Crystalline cohomology is then defined intrinsically:

$$H^i_{\mathrm{cris}}(X_0/W) \;:=\; H^i\big((X_0/W)_{\mathrm{cris}},\,\mathcal{O}_{X/W}\big).$$
A PD-thickening, schematically
$X_0$ as the special fibre, $T$ as a PD-thickening
$X_0\hookrightarrow T$, kernel $\mathcal{I}$ with PD γ; $\mathcal{O}_{X/W}(T)=\mathcal{O}_T$.

This packaging looks ornate; its payoff is that everything is intrinsic to $X_0$. Different choices of local lift become different objects of the same site, and the structure sheaf treats them uniformly. Sections 3 and 4 cash this out into a computable invariant.

3. Comparison with de Rham of a lift

Why pay the price of the crystalline site? Because once $X_0$ admits a smooth proper lift $X/W$, the answer reduces to something already understood:

Berthelot–Ogus comparison. If $X/W$ is smooth proper with special fibre $X_0$, then there is a canonical isomorphism $$H^i_{\mathrm{cris}}(X_0/W) \;\xrightarrow{\,\sim\,}\; H^i_{dR}(X/W) \;=\; \mathbb{H}^i\big(X,\,\Omega^\bullet_{X/W}\big),$$ and the right-hand side is independent of the choice of lift (any two smooth proper lifts give canonically isomorphic answers).

The proof sketch: a smooth lift $X$ provides a global PD-thickening of $X_0$ in the crystalline site, and the structure sheaf $\mathcal{O}_{X/W}$ on that thickening is the de Rham complex (up to a quasi-isomorphism that uses divided powers to make the Poincaré lemma work in characteristic $p$). Two different lifts get connected by a third, and the PD-structure on overlapping kernels makes the comparison canonical, not just up to isomorphism.

Non-liftable case. Smooth lifts to $W$ are not guaranteed for arbitrary $X_0/k$ — Hirokado (1999) and Schröer (2003) constructed smooth proper varieties in characteristic $2$ and $3$ that admit no smooth lift to characteristic zero. Many natural examples only admit a $W_2(k)=W/p^2$-lift (Deligne–Illusie use only this); elliptic curves always lift to $W$ via Serre–Tate, though for supersingular $E_0/k$ the lift is non-canonical. The crystalline site handles non-liftability gracefully: cover $X_0$ by Zariski opens that do lift smoothly (they always do, locally on any smooth scheme), compute de Rham of each, and let the PD-structure on the kernels of the local thickenings glue the pieces canonically. The cohomology one obtains is intrinsic to $X_0$ and independent of every choice. The Berthelot–Ogus theorem above is the global form of this gluing principle.

4. Frobenius action and the Katz–Messing theorem

Crystalline cohomology earns its place in the Weil-cohomology zoo by carrying a canonical Frobenius operator. The absolute Frobenius $F\colon X_0\to X_0$ — the $p$-power map on functions — is a morphism of $X_0$ but not a $W$-linear morphism: there is no $W$-lift $X\to X$ inducing $F$ on the special fibre. The way out is semilinearity.

Frobenius on crystalline cohomology. $F$ induces a map $\varphi\colon H^i_{\mathrm{cris}}(X_0/W)\to H^i_{\mathrm{cris}}(X_0/W)$ that is $\sigma$-semilinear: $$\varphi(a\cdot v) = \sigma(a)\cdot \varphi(v), \qquad a\in W,\;v\in H^i_{\mathrm{cris}},$$ where $\sigma\colon W\to W$ is the unique lift of the $p$-power Frobenius on the residue field $k$.

Concretely: $\sigma$ acts on Witt-vector $(a_0,a_1,\ldots)$ by $(a_0^p,a_1^p,\ldots)$ — so $\sigma$ is a ring automorphism of $W=W(\overline{\mathbb{F}_p})$ (and of $W(\mathbb{F}_q)$ once one passes to the relevant unramified extension), but is the identity only on $\mathbb{Z}_p$. Semilinearity is the algebraic shadow of Frobenius being a non-trivial Galois action on the coefficients.

Geometric Frobenius. For $X_0/\mathbb{F}_q$ with $q=p^f$, the relevant operator is the geometric Frobenius — the $f$-th iterate $\varphi^f$ — which is $W(\mathbb{F}_q)$-linear (because $\sigma^f=\mathrm{id}$ on $W(\mathbb{F}_q)$). Its eigenvalues are honest algebraic numbers, and they answer the same point-counting question as étale Frobenius:

Katz–Messing (1974). For $X_0$ smooth proper over $\mathbb{F}_q$, the characteristic polynomial of geometric Frobenius on $H^i_{\mathrm{cris}}(X_0/W)\otimes K$ has integer coefficients and equals the characteristic polynomial on $H^i_{\text{ét}}(X_{0,\bar k},\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ for every $\ell\ne p$. Both factor the same alternating product yielding the local zeta function: $$Z(X_0/\mathbb{F}_q,\,t) \;=\; \prod_{i=0}^{2\dim X_0}\det(1-t\cdot\mathrm{Frob}_q\,|\,H^i_*(X_0))^{(-1)^{i+1}}.$$

This is the statement that closes the Weil-conjectures package across all primes: every prime $\ell$ — including $\ell=p$, when interpreted via crystalline cohomology — gives the same Frobenius eigenvalues, hence the same zeta function. Deligne–Katz–Messing-style purity then forces those eigenvalues to be Weil $q$-numbers of weight $i$: algebraic integers $\alpha$ with $|\alpha|=q^{i/2}$ under every complex embedding.

5. F-isocrystals, Newton above Hodge

Tensoring with $K=\mathrm{Frac}\,W$ turns crystalline cohomology into something with finer structure than its étale cousin. Define an F-isocrystal over $k$ to be a finite-dimensional $K$-vector space $V$ with a $\sigma$-semilinear bijection $\varphi\colon V\to V$. Morphisms are $K$-linear maps commuting with $\varphi$. The category $\mathrm{F\text{-}Isoc}(k)$ is $\mathbb{Q}_p$-linear Tannakian, and over $k=\overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$ has been completely classified.

Dieudonné–Manin (1958, 1963). Over $k=\overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$, every F-isocrystal $(V,\varphi)$ is a direct sum of simple objects $V_\lambda$ indexed by $\lambda=s/r\in\mathbb{Q}$ (in lowest terms, with $r>0$), where $V_\lambda$ has dimension $r$ and $\varphi^r=p^s\cdot\mathrm{id}$. The number $\lambda$ is called the slope. The slopes appear with multiplicities adding up to $\dim V$.

The slopes are $p$-adic valuations of the eigenvalues of $\varphi$ (after passing to a finite extension where $\varphi$ becomes diagonalizable). Plot them as a polygon — the Newton polygon of $(V,\varphi)$ — by listing slopes in non-decreasing order $\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\cdots\le\lambda_n$ and drawing the lower convex hull of $(0,0),(1,\lambda_1),(2,\lambda_1+\lambda_2),\ldots$.

For $X_0$ smooth proper, the F-isocrystal $H^i_{\mathrm{cris}}(X_0/W)\otimes K$ has a Newton polygon. There is also a Hodge polygon, built from the Hodge numbers $h^{p,q}$ of any lift (or from the de Rham–Hodge spectral sequence in characteristic $p$): in degree $i$, the slope $p$ has multiplicity $h^{p,i-p}$. The two polygons share endpoints (total height $i$ times rank-conservation) but differ in shape:

Mazur's inequality (1973). The Newton polygon of Frobenius on $H^i_{\mathrm{cris}}(X_0/W)\otimes K$ lies on or above the Hodge polygon of degree $i$, and the two share the same endpoints. The case Newton$=$Hodge defines the ordinary stratum.

6. Period rings $B_{\mathrm{cris}}, B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ and Fontaine's comparison

The deepest payoff of crystalline cohomology is its appearance in $p$-adic Hodge theory — the framework Fontaine built in the 1980s to compare $p$-adic étale cohomology of varieties over $p$-adic fields with their other realisations. The classical analogue is the periods isomorphism over $\mathbb{C}$: $$H^*_{dR}(X/\mathbb{C}) \;\cong\; H^*_{\text{sing}}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q}) \otimes_\mathbb{Q} \mathbb{C},$$ which uses transcendental periods like $2\pi i$ as basis-change scalars. The $p$-adic analogue replaces $\mathbb{C}$ by Fontaine's period rings.

Fontaine's hierarchy. Three big topological $\mathbb{Q}_p$-algebras with $G_K=\Gal(\bar K/K)$-action, organized by which extra structure they carry: $$\mathbb{Q}_p \;\subset\; B_{\mathrm{HT}} \;\subset\; B_{\mathrm{cris}} \;\subset\; B_{\mathrm{dR}}$$
  • $B_{\mathrm{HT}}=\mathbb{C}_p[t,t^{-1}]$ where $t$ has degree $1$ — a graded ring; "Hodge–Tate".
  • $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$: a complete discrete valuation field with residue field $\mathbb{C}_p$, equipped with a decreasing filtration $F^\bullet$.
  • $B_{\mathrm{cris}}\subset B_{\mathrm{dR}}$: subring stable under a Frobenius $\varphi$, and inheriting the filtration.

The construction of $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$ uses divided powers — the same gadget as in §2. Concretely, $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$ is built from the perfection of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}/p$, taking PD-envelopes, and inverting a specific element. The technicalities are formidable; what one needs from them is the following:

Crystalline comparison (Fontaine–Messing conjecture; Faltings 1989, Tsuji 1999). Let $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ be a finite extension with residue field $k$, $W=W(k)$, and $X/K$ smooth proper with good reduction $X_0/k$. Then $$H^i_{\text{ét}}(X_{\bar K},\mathbb{Q}_p) \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_p} B_{\mathrm{cris}} \;\cong\; H^i_{\mathrm{cris}}(X_0/W) \otimes_W B_{\mathrm{cris}},$$ and the isomorphism is compatible with Frobenius, the filtration coming from de Rham of the lift, and the $G_K$-action on both sides.

The slogan: $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$ is the algebra of $p$-adic periods. Tensoring $p$-adic étale cohomology with $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$ "reveals" the crystalline structure that was hidden by the Galois action; conversely, taking $G_K$-invariants of the right-hand side recovers the étale realisation. One Galois representation $V=H^i_{\text{ét}}(X_{\bar K},\mathbb{Q}_p)$, three ways to extract structure: $D_{\mathrm{HT}}(V)$ recovers the Hodge–Tate decomposition; $D_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)$ recovers crystalline cohomology; $D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)$ recovers de Rham with its Hodge filtration. A Galois representation $V$ is called crystalline when $\dim_{K_0}D_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_p}V$ — equivalently, when it comes from the cohomology of a good-reduction variety.

This is the framework that lets one say "the $p$-adic Galois representation attached to a modular form is crystalline at $p$" — a statement that has no analogue in $\ell$-adic land, and that powers Wiles's modularity proof, Iwasawa-theoretic main conjectures for elliptic curves, and the local Langlands correspondence in the $p$-adic direction.

7. Connections

Crystalline cohomology sits at a crossroads. Below it is the foundational stack of de Rham, sheaf cohomology, and PD-algebra; above it sits the entire arithmetic-geometry program — modular forms, $p$-adic $L$-functions, the Langlands program in characteristic $p$. The 1990s saw the comparison conjecture proved (Faltings, Tsuji); the 2010s saw new period rings ($B_{\mathrm{st}}$, prismatic cohomology of Bhatt–Scholze) generalize the picture to bad-reduction and to derived stacks. The technology is still expanding.

  • Algebraic de Rham cohomology — de Rham complex — the characteristic-$0$ shadow that crystalline cohomology lifts to characteristic $p$ via the Berthelot–Ogus comparison.
  • Étale cohomology — Weil conjectures and Frobenius — Katz–Messing's theorem shows crystalline and $\ell$-adic cohomology share Frobenius eigenvalues and zeta function.
  • Hodge theory — the Hodge decomposition — Mazur's inequality says Newton lies above Hodge, and ordinary varieties achieve equality.
  • Iwasawa theory — $p$-adic $L$-functions — crystalline-comparable Galois representations are exactly the ones whose Iwasawa-theoretic $L$-functions admit Selmer-side companions.
  • Galois representations — crystallinity is the local-at-$p$ condition that distinguishes "geometric" $p$-adic representations from arbitrary ones.

Open frontier: a global crystalline / prismatic site that handles bad reduction, mixed characteristic, and derived schemes uniformly. Bhatt–Scholze's prismatic cohomology (2019) is a major step — it specializes to crystalline, étale, and de Rham according to the choice of base prism — but the full Tannakian picture (a single motivic Galois group recovering all four realizations) is still mostly conjectural beyond the abelian / Tate-motive case.
