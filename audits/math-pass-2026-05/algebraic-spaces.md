# Math correctness audit — `algebraic-spaces.html`

## Verified claims (sections)

- **§1 motivation.** $\mathbb{A}^1/\mathbb{Z}$ as the canonical "wants to be a quotient but isn't a scheme" — correct. The diagonal-fails-to-be-quasi-compact argument (preimage along $\mathbb{A}^1\times\mathbb{A}^1\to X\times X$ is $\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{A}^1$, an infinite disjoint union) is the standard Knutson obstruction.
- **§2 étale equivalence relation.** Definition (mono $j=(s,t)\colon R\hookrightarrow U\times U$, both projections étale, categorical reflexivity/symmetry/transitivity) is the textbook definition (Stacks 02WW). Étale projections force equivalence classes to be étale-locally discrete — correct intuition.
- **§3 Knutson definition.** Étale sheaf + diagonal representable by schemes + étale scheme atlas. The equivalence with "quotient $U/R$ by an étale equivalence relation" (presentation theorem) is correctly stated and is the standard Artin/Knutson result. The remark that "$R = U\times_X U$" is correct.
- **§3(a)–(c).** Scheme diagonals are locally closed immersions (EGA I, true); quasi-separated $\Leftrightarrow$ $\Delta$ quasi-compact; many authors implicitly restrict to qs spaces. All standard.
- **§4 morphism definitions.** Smooth = flat + lfp + geometrically smooth fibres ✓. Finite = proper + affine + lft (equiv. affine + integral) ✓. Étale-locality of étale/smooth/flat/lfp/lft/finite/closed-immersion/open-immersion is standard (Stacks 03YJ et seq.).
- **§4 separated subtlety.** $f$ separated $\Leftrightarrow \Delta_f$ closed immersion ✓. The note that separatedness of $\tilde f\colon U\to V$ alone doesn't suffice (need also $j\colon R\hookrightarrow U\times_V U$ closed) is correct and a real gotcha.
- **§5(i)–(ii).** Schemes embed fully faithfully ✓. Free finite-group quotient: $U/G$ is an algebraic space with $R = G\times U$ via $(g,u)\mapsto(u,g\cdot u)$ ✓. The quasi-projective-orbits sufficient condition for schemehood is essentially the standard SGA/Mumford criterion ("orbit contained in an affine open").
- **§6 hierarchy.** Correct characterizations: scheme ⇔ $\Delta$ locally closed immersion + Zariski atlas; algebraic space ⇔ $\Delta$ monomorphism / trivial geometric stabilisers; DM ⇔ $\Delta$ unramified / finite étale stabilisers; Artin ⇔ $\Delta$ representable + smooth atlas. Examples ($BG$ Artin for positive-dim $G$; $\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,n}$ DM for $2g-2+n>0$; $\mathrm{Bun}_G(C)$ Artin) all correct.

## Wrong / dubious claims (with file:line)

- **algebraic-spaces.html:272** — "any candidate would have to be a smooth $1$-dimensional $k$-variety dominated by $\mathbb{A}^1$, and a Zariski-dense fibre of $\mathbb{Z}$-orbits forces $X$ to fail to be quasi-separated." The chain of reasoning is hand-wavy: the candidate $X$ need not a priori be smooth or 1-dim until you fix dimension theory; the actual obstruction (made precise in step 4 of the scrubber) is purely the $\Delta$-quasi-compactness failure. Better to drop "smooth 1-dimensional $k$-variety" claim — the obstruction proven later doesn't use it.
- **algebraic-spaces.html:341** — "(Artin–Knutson)" attribution is non-standard. The equivalence "étale sheaf with rep. diagonal + étale atlas $\Leftrightarrow$ quotient by an étale equivalence relation" is usually credited just to Knutson (Algebraic Spaces, 1971, Ch. II); Artin's contribution was the broader representability theorem. Minor; "Knutson" alone would be more accurate.
- **algebraic-spaces.html:648** — "no scheme can have a free $\mathbb{Z}/2$ action exchanging two cycles whose blowups are linearly equivalent" — this paraphrase of the Hironaka obstruction is muddled. The actual obstruction is that on $H$ there exists a proper curve $C$ which is numerically trivial w.r.t. every line bundle, which is incompatible with $H$ being a (necessarily quasi-projective) scheme of this kind, **but** non-projective proper schemes do exist with such curves. The cleaner statement: the descended cycle classes give a relation among effective curves whose sum is zero in $N_1(H)$, which forces non-projectivity, and one then uses the additional structure (smooth proper 3-fold with this configuration) to rule out non-projective schemehood as well. As written, the argument conflates "scheme" with "projective scheme."
- **algebraic-spaces.html:671** — svgInner label "Ỹ = Bl_A(Y) ⊔ Bl_B(Y)" — the disjoint-union symbol is misleading; $\tilde Y$ is a single irreducible 3-fold obtained by blowing up the union $A\cup B$ (or by patching local blowups), not a disjoint union.
- **algebraic-spaces.html:685** — "$E\cdot C = 0$ for every line bundle $E$" — variable name $E$ is overloaded with the exceptional divisors $E_A, E_B$ in the same scrubber; should be $L\cdot C = 0$ for every line bundle $L$.

## Underspecified or unverifiable claims

- **algebraic-spaces.html:340** — "free actions of finite étale group schemes give étale equivalence relations, but so do many quotients without a global group action." True but no example/citation; a reader can't verify "many."
- **algebraic-spaces.html:585** — Properness for algebraic spaces: "universally closed" is checked through scheme atlases. Glossed; the actual definition uses the valuative criterion or universal closure of the underlying morphism of presentations. Not wrong, just shallow.
- **algebraic-spaces.html:701** — "$B(\mathbb Z/n) = [\mathrm{pt}/(\mathbb Z/n)]$" as a DM-stack example. Correct but assumes characteristic coprime to $n$ (otherwise $\mu_n \neq \mathbb Z/n$ as group schemes and care is needed). Unstated.
- **algebraic-spaces.html:795** — "Moishezon manifolds … proper algebraic spaces with maximal transcendence degree" — Moishezon manifolds are by classical definition compact complex manifolds with $\dim_{\mathbb C}\,a(X) = \dim X$ for the algebraic dimension $a(X)$. The "= proper algebraic spaces over $\mathbb C$" identification (Artin) is a real theorem but the parenthetical phrasing is too compressed for a reader to verify.

## Severity

**Minor.** The mathematical core (definitions, étale-equivalence-relation theorem, hierarchy, morphism properties, $\mathbb{A}^1/\mathbb{Z}$ obstruction) is correct. The two "moderate-leaning-minor" issues are the muddled Hironaka projectivity argument (§5 step 6) and the variable-name collision $E_A,E_B$ vs. "every line bundle $E$." The §1 hand-wave about "smooth 1-dim $k$-variety dominated by $\mathbb{A}^1$" should be cut. No claim is mathematically wrong in a way that would mislead a reader on the substance.
