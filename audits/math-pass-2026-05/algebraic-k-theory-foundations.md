## Wrong / dubious claims

- **`SK_1(\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}])\ne 0` is wrong** (line 370). The page asserts "the kernel $SK_1(R)$ vanishes for fields ... and Dedekind domains ..., but not in general — $SK_1(\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}])\ne 0$ already." This is incorrect. By the fundamental theorem of algebraic K-theory (Bass-Heller-Swan), for any regular ring $R$,
  $$K_1(R[t,t^{-1}]) \;\cong\; K_1(R)\oplus K_0(R).$$
  Applying this with $R=\mathbb{Z}$ (regular), one gets $K_1(\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}])\cong\{\pm 1\}\oplus\mathbb{Z}$, generated by $-1$ and the class of $t$. The unit group $\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}]^\times=\{\pm t^n:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}\cong\{\pm 1\}\oplus\mathbb{Z}$ is the same group, and the determinant map is a bijection, so $SK_1(\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}])=0$. **Suggested fix**: replace with a genuine nontrivial example. Standard textbook nontrivial-$SK_1$ examples include $SK_1(\mathbb{Z}[Q_8])\ne 0$ (Q_8 the quaternion group of order 8, Magurn et al.), or $SK_1(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{23}])$ via Milnor's calculations, or — closest to the algebraic-geometry flavor of the surrounding text — $SK_1$ of a singular ring like $SK_1(\mathbb{Z}[x,y]/(y^2-x^3))$ (the cuspidal curve).

## Underspecified or unverifiable claims

- **"Euler class of the chain complex of the universal cover"** (line 948) for the Wall obstruction. Strictly speaking this should be "Euler characteristic" (the alternating sum $\sum_i(-1)^i[C_i]$ in $\widetilde{K}_0(\mathbb{Z}[\pi_1])$), not "Euler class" (which usually means a top cohomology class of a vector bundle). Mild terminological imprecision; the math content is right.
- **"$\widetilde{K}_0(\mathbb{Z}[C_p])$ is related to the class group of $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_p]$ — but is NOT the same as it (it is the class group of the maximal order plus error terms)"** (widget line 1004). For $p$ prime, Rim's theorem (1959) gives an *isomorphism* $\widetilde{K}_0(\mathbb{Z}[C_p])\cong\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_p])$ — they are precisely the same group, since $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_p]$ already is the maximal order in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)$ for prime $p$. The widget's hedging applies to more general groups (non-prime cyclic, dihedral, etc.) where $\mathbb{Z}[G]$ may not be a maximal order and there are genuine "error terms" (the kernel of the map to the maximal-order class group). For the prime-cyclic case the widget overhedges.
- **Beilinson-Lichtenbaum decomposition $K_n(X)_{(\ell)}\cong\bigoplus_p H^{2p-n}_M(X,\mathbb{Z}_{(\ell)}(p))$** (line 954). This is the spectral-sequence-collapse interpretation; technically the motivic spectral sequence converging to K-theory may have extension issues integrally, and the precise BL conjecture identifies motivic with étale cohomology in a degree range. The displayed formula is the "morally correct" K_*-as-direct-sum-of-motivic statement, common in expositions but slightly informal. Not an error per se, just a simplification.
- **"$\prod_p\mathbb{Z}\times\{\pm 1\}$" for $K_1(\mathbb{Q})$** (widget readouts at lines 859 and 906). The decomposition is really a direct sum $\bigoplus_p\mathbb{Z}\oplus\{\pm 1\}$ since each $a\in\mathbb{Q}^\times$ has finitely many nonzero $v_p(a)$. Cosmetic notation choice, content is correct.
