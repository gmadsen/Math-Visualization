## Wrong / dubious claims (with file:line)

- **`mapping-class-groups.html:422` — Chain relation has parity swapped and a wrong exponent.** Standard statement (Farb–Margalit, *A Primer on Mapping Class Groups*, Prop. 4.12): for a chain $c_1,\dots,c_k$, the regular neighbourhood $N$ has **two** boundary components when $k$ is **odd** and **one** when $k$ is **even**, and the chain relations are
  $(T_{c_1}\cdots T_{c_k})^{k+1} = T_{d_1}T_{d_2}$ when $k$ is odd,
  $(T_{c_1}\cdots T_{c_k})^{2k+2} = T_d$ when $k$ is even.
  The page asserts the exponent $2k+2$ on **both** sides and assigns "single $T_d$" to even $k$ — exponent matches even, but the page's "$2k+2$ when $k$ odd $\Rightarrow T_{d_1}T_{d_2}$" half is incorrect (should be $k+1$).
- **`mapping-class-groups.html:912` (Casson widget, figure-8 surgery entry) — Internal inconsistency.** The note states $\Delta_{4_1}''(1) = -2$ and quotes $\lambda(S^3_{1/n}(K)) = (n/2)\Delta_K''(1)$, which gives $\lambda(S^3_{+1}(4_1)) = -1$, but the widget reports $\lambda = 0$. Either the value should be $-1$ (with the standard surgery formula and the figure-8 Δ'') or the formula/sign convention needs to be qualified. The figure-8 $+1$-surgery is also not "hyperbolic" — it is a small Seifert-fibered space (Brieskorn-like).
- **`mapping-class-groups.html:404` — Lickorish "1964" / "$3g-1$" attribution is the corrected count, fine, but the famous original number was $3g+1$ (Lickorish 1964) and the $3g-1$ improvement is from Lickorish's 1965 follow-up. Not wrong (the corrected count is what we use today), but the year "1964" alone obscures the two-paper history.
- **`mapping-class-groups.html:409` (genus-1 row of the Humphries table) — "# Humphries twists = 3" for $g=1$ is misleading.** Humphries' theorem is stated for $g\ge 2$; on $T^2$, $\mathrm{Mod}(T^2) = \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ is generated by the **two** Dehn twists $T_a, T_b$ about meridian and longitude (corresponding to $S, T$ generators / equivalently to $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ and $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\-1&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$). The table's "$3$" comes from blindly applying $2g+1$, which is not the correct Humphries minimum at $g=1$.
- **`mapping-class-groups.html:339` (genus calculator, T² entry) — Presentation `⟨a,b | (ab)^6=1, (aba)^4=1⟩`.** This is a known presentation but is missing the standard centrality / additional relation needed for SL₂(ℤ): the most common presentation is $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}) = \langle S, T \mid S^4=1,\,(ST)^6=1,\,S^2 \text{ central}\rangle$, equivalently $\langle a,b \mid a^4 = (ab)^6, a^2 \text{ central}\rangle$. As written without the $a^4=1$ (or $(aba)^4=(ab)^6$) tying relation it presents an extension, not SL₂(ℤ) itself. Worth double-checking against a source if kept.
- **`mapping-class-groups.html:521`+ (pseudo-Anosov dilatation widget readout)** — line `Volume contraction φ*: λ × 1/λ = 1 (area-preserving)`. True numerically but framed as "volume contraction" is misleading; pseudo-Anosov maps preserve a **transverse measure** (hence area for the singular Euclidean structure), they are not the multiplication "λ × 1/λ" of a volume form. Phrasing nit.

## Underspecified or unverifiable claims

- **`mapping-class-groups.html:301` — Birman exact sequence quoted unconditionally.** The sequence $1\to\pi_1(S',p)\to\mathrm{Mod}(S)\to\mathrm{Mod}(S')\to 1$ requires $\chi(S') < 0$ (otherwise $\pi_1 \to \mathrm{Mod}$ has non-trivial kernel: e.g., the centre of $\pi_1(T^2)$). Used in the $g\ge 2$ paragraph that follows, so the implicit hypothesis is fine, but the quoted sequence is presented without it.
- **`mapping-class-groups.html:412` (table row "g≥3 hyperelliptic vanishes")** — The hyperelliptic involution $\iota$ exists as a mapping class only for $g=2$ (where it is central, generating $Z(\mathrm{Mod}(\Sigma_2)) = \mathbb{Z}/2$); for $g\ge 3$ the generic surface has trivial automorphism group and there is no canonical hyperelliptic mapping class. The shorthand "$g$-chain hyperelliptic vanishes" is ambiguous — likely refers to the chain relation degenerating, but the wording reads as if the involution itself vanishes. Underspecified.
- **`mapping-class-groups.html:702` — "smooth and topological categories tear apart" in dim 4.** Correct in spirit but slightly overstated: there exist smooth structures on most simply-connected topological 4-manifolds (only the Kirby–Siebenmann obstruction can rule them out absolutely); the tear is in *uniqueness*, not always *existence*.
- **`mapping-class-groups.html:733` — "Freedman manifold $M_{E_8}$ ... carries no smooth structure at all."** ✓ via Donaldson (signature obstruction: a closed smooth simply-connected 4-manifold with definite intersection form has diagonal $Q$; $E_8$ is even definite and not diagonal). This is the standard line; reader may want a one-line "by Donaldson" to make the obstruction visible.
- **`mapping-class-groups.html:743` — "$\mathbb{CP}^2$ smooth structure unique up to diffeomorphism — open!"** Correct that smooth uniqueness for $\mathbb{CP}^2$ remains open (the smooth 4-d Poincaré is the related famous open problem). The "(open!)" is honest.
