# Math-correctness audit — brill-noether.html

Scope: every mathematical assertion. Pedagogy ignored.

## Verified claims

**Definitions (§1, §4).** $g^r_d=(L,V)$ with $\deg L=d$, $V\subset H^0(C,L)$ of dim $r+1$ ✓. Complete = $V=H^0$ ✓. $W^r_d(C)=\{L\in\Pic^d(C):h^0(L)\ge r+1\}$ as a determinantal subscheme of $\Pic^d$ via universal evaluation map; expected codim $(r+1)(g-d+r)$ via Macaulay/Porteous ✓. Special divisor $\Leftrightarrow h^1(D)>0\Leftrightarrow h^0(K-D)>0$ ✓.

**Riemann–Roch (§2).** $h^0(D)-h^0(K-D)=d-g+1$ ✓. Serre vanishing for $d>2g-2$ ✓. $h^0(K)=g$, $h^1(K)=1$ ✓. Geometric reading $h^1(D)=$ holomorphic differentials vanishing along $D$ = hyperplanes through $\varphi_K(D)$ in $\mathbb P^{g-1}$ ✓.

**Brill–Noether number (§3).** $\rho(g,r,d)=g-(r+1)(g-d+r)$ ✓. Derivation as $\dim\Pic^d - $ Porteous codim ✓. Genus-2: $\rho(2,1,2)=0$, every genus-2 curve hyperelliptic, $W^1_2$ a single point (canonical pencil $|K|$) ✓. Genus-3: $\rho(3,1,2)=-1$, hyperelliptic locus codim 1 in $\mathcal M_3$ ✓. Genus-4: $\rho(4,1,3)=0$, every genus-4 curve trigonal, generically two pencils (two rulings of the quadric containing the canonical model) ✓. Genus-5: $\rho(5,1,3)=-1$, trigonal locus codim 1 in $\mathcal M_5$ ✓.

**Existence theorem (§5).** Brill–Noether existence for *every* curve when $\rho\ge 0$, every component of $W^r_d$ has dim $\ge\rho$ — Kempf 1971, Kleiman–Laksov 1972 ✓. Sharp dimension $=\rho$ on a general curve (Griffiths–Harris 1980) ✓. Outline of cohomology proof via Poincaré bundle, pushforward, determinantal locus, Schubert/Schur class is correct in skeleton ✓. Poincaré-formula factor $\prod_{i=0}^{r} i!/(g-d+r+i)!$ and $\theta$-power $g-\rho=(r+1)(g-d+r)$ ✓ (matches ACGH §VII.4).

**Petri (§6).** Petri map $\mu_0\colon H^0(L)\otimes H^0(K\otimes L^{-1})\to H^0(K)$ as cup product ✓. $\dim T_L W^r_d = g-\operatorname{rk}\mu_0$ on the open stratum $W^r_d\setminus W^{r+1}_d$; injectivity $\Rightarrow\dim T_L=g-(r+1)(g-d+r)=\rho$ ✓. Gieseker 1982 first proof ✓. Geometric reading: Petri injectivity ⟺ no rank-2 quadric of the form $\sum s_it_i$ vanishes on the canonical image ✓ (Noether–Enriques–Petri).

**Moduli (§7).** When $\rho=-1$, $\mathcal M^r_{g,d}$ is a divisor in $\mathcal M_g$; Eisenbud–Harris (1987) computed the class in $\Pic(\overline{\mathcal M}_g)\otimes\mathbb Q$ via limit linear series ✓. Harris–Mumford use BN divisors to bound the canonical class and prove $\overline{\mathcal M}_g$ of general type for large $g$ ✓ (the cited threshold is $g\ge 24$, classical Harris–Mumford–Eisenbud result).

**Connections (§8).** Schubert / Chern-class formulation on $\Pic^d\cong J(C)$ ✓. Mercat conjecture (rank-$\ge 2$ BN) and counterexamples — open ✓. Cools–Draisma–Payne–Robeva and Jensen–Payne tropical proofs of BN/Gieseker–Petri via chip-firing ✓.

## Wrong / dubious claims

- **brill-noether.html:679 (table row $(g,g-1,2g-2)$).** Stated $\rho=g-1$ for the canonical series. **Wrong.** $\rho(g,g-1,2g-2)=g-g\cdot(g-(2g-2)+(g-1))=g-g\cdot 1=0$. The geometric reading "$\dim W^{g-1}_{2g-2}=0$ in general (just one $L$, namely $K$)" is correct and is in fact $=\rho=0$, not $\rho=g-1$. Numerical error in the $\rho$ column only.
- **brill-noether.html:1153 (table row $(6,2,5)$, "$\rho=-1$, BN divisor in $\mathcal M_6$").** Doubly wrong. (a) $\rho(6,2,5)=6-3\cdot(6-5+2)=6-9=-3$, not $-1$. (b) For $g=6$ no triple $(r,d)$ gives $\rho=-1$ (the equation $g-(r+1)(g-d+r)=-1$ has no integer solutions for $g=6$; e.g. $r=1\Rightarrow 2(7-d)=7$, $r=2\Rightarrow 3(8-d)=7$, $r=3\Rightarrow 4(9-d)=7$). Hence there is no Brill–Noether divisor in $\mathcal M_6$ at all. The row should be removed or replaced with a genuine BN-divisor case (e.g. $(g,r,d)=(11,2,8)$ or $(23,6,12)$ — the Harris–Mumford row that actually delivers $\overline{\mathcal M}_g$ of general type).
- **brill-noether.html:1135 (limit-series widget readout).** "the vanishing sequences on the two sides of each node must add to (≥) $(d-r,d-r+1,\ldots,d)$". Misstates the Eisenbud–Harris compatibility. Correct rule (ACGH/Eisenbud–Harris): if $a_0<\cdots<a_r$ and $b_0<\cdots<b_r$ are the vanishing sequences on the two sides of a node, then $a_i+b_{r-i}\ge d$ for **every** $i$ — a *single* threshold $d$, indexed in *opposite* directions on the two sides, not a sequence-of-thresholds. The current phrasing ("add to a sequence $(d-r,\ldots,d)$") is not a recognizable form of the condition.
- **brill-noether.html:1126 (limit-series widget label).** "$\rho(4,1,5)=4-2(4-5+1)=4$". Computation $4-2\cdot 0=4$ is arithmetically correct, but $\rho=4=g$ means $W^1_5(C)=\Pic^5(C)$ generically (every degree-5 line bundle has $h^0\ge 2$), so this isn't a "limit $g^1_5$" example — the locus is the entire Jacobian, not a determinantal subscheme. The illustration ("chain of 4 elliptic curves modeling a limit $g^1_5$") is then misleading: limit linear series are interesting precisely when $\rho$ is small/negative; on this example there is nothing special to limit.

## Underspecified or unverifiable claims

- **brill-noether.html:949 (Petri attribution).** "Eisenbud–Harris 1983". Their canonical limit-linear-series papers establishing Petri are *Limit linear series: Basic theory* (Invent. Math. 1986) and *The Kodaira dimension of the moduli space of curves of genus ≥ 23* (Invent. Math. 1987). 1983 is not the right reference for the Petri reproof; flag as a date slip.
- **brill-noether.html:678 (Castelnuovo's count for genus 4).** Calling the "two $g^1_3$'s on a generic genus-4 curve" *Castelnuovo's count* is non-standard attribution — this is classical (Petri / Noether) and follows from the canonical curve being a smooth $(2,3)$ complete intersection in $\mathbb P^3$, with the two rulings of the quadric giving the two pencils. Castelnuovo's name is associated with bounds on $h^0$ of curves in $\mathbb P^r$, not this count.
- **brill-noether.html:847 (hyperelliptic locus in $\mathcal M_g$).** Notation $\mathcal M^1_{g,2}$ for the hyperelliptic locus has codim $g-2$ in $\mathcal M_g$ (since $\dim\mathcal M_g^{\mathrm{hyp}}=2g-1$, $\dim\mathcal M_g=3g-3$). For $g\ge 4$ this is *not* a divisor; the page calls it "the simplest example" of "non-general curves where $W^r_d$ has dimension above $\rho$", which is fine pedagogically but the codimension should be flagged ($g=3$ is the only genus where it is codim 1).
- **brill-noether.html:945 (tangent space).** "$T_L W^r_d=$ dual of $\operatorname{coker}\mu_0(L)$". Standard statement is $T_L W^r_d^{\mathrm{sm}}=(\operatorname{Im}\mu_0)^\perp\subset H^0(K)^*=T_L\Pic^d$, identified with $(\operatorname{coker}\mu_0)^*$ via Serre duality. Dimension-wise correct and the duality identification is canonical, but the phrasing "$T_L=\operatorname{coker}^*$" elides the embedding into $T_L\Pic^d$ and is loose for a foundational definition.
- **Maximal rank conjecture (Larson)** is in the audit's focus list but **does not appear anywhere in the page body**. Cannot verify what isn't there; flag as a gap relative to the audit scope, not a mathematical error.
