# quaternions-octonions-and-division-algebras — pedagogical audit (2026-05)

**Section:** Algebra & homological
**Compared against:** algebra, lie-algebras

## Summary
A tight, well-paced page: six numbered concept sections, each with a real interactive widget, voice and notation are largely on-brand. The three notable issues are two broken cross-page anchors in the "Connections" list, a Unicode-vs-LaTeX split inside SVG/JS readouts that diverges from the peers' table-style notation, and a couple of jargon-before-definition spots in §2.

## Findings

### Notation drift
- **Unicode `ℝ ℂ ℍ 𝕆 𝕊` inside JS-built widget readouts** (lines 506-510 Cayley–Dickson rungs; 725-749 Frobenius case-tree node labels; 797 Hurwitz bar headers) while the surrounding prose and tables use KaTeX `$\mathbb{R}$ … $\mathbb{S}$`. Both peers handle this differently: `algebra.html` keeps every algebra symbol inside `$…$` even in widgets via row labels, and `lie-algebras.html`'s readouts use ASCII basis names (`e,h,f`). Cosmetic, low priority — but if a future widget reads `q.toString()` into prose the two registers will collide. Either accept the convention page-wide (Unicode inside SVG, KaTeX outside) and document it, or upgrade SVG `<text>` elements to render KaTeX (as `condensed-mathematics.html` does for a few labels).
- **`x*` vs `x^*`** — Cayley–Dickson formula at line 490 is the LaTeX `(a,b)^* = (a^*,-b)` (correct) but the widget readout at line 507 prints `self-conjugation x* = x` (ASCII). Same content, two glyphs; align by writing `x^* = x` in the readout (KaTeX-rendered would require a wrapper, but plain `x*` is fine if consistent — peers don't have an analogue to compare against).
- **`\mathrm{Im}` and `\mathrm{Sp}`, `\mathrm{SU}`, `\mathrm{SO}`, `\mathrm{Br}`** are all written with `\mathrm{…}` (lines 344, 366, 369, 861). `lie-algebras.html` writes `\mathrm{GL}_n`, `\mathrm{SL}_n`, `\mathrm{SO}_n` the same way (line 280-283); no drift. But the page **declares** `\Hom`, `\Spec`, `\Gal`, `\tr`, `\ad`, `\ind` macros via the loader (lines 22-29) and uses none of them — `\tr` would tighten line 708's `\tr(x)=0`. Either prune the unused macros or use them.
- **`\overline q`** at lines 263-265 vs **`\bar{q}`** convention some references use — peers favour `\overline` for multi-letter overlines (`\overline\varphi` etc.) and `\bar` for single chars. The single-char `\overline q` here is fine but `\bar q` is one keystroke shorter and matches `complex-analysis.html`. Cosmetic.

### Undefined jargon
- **§2 line 366:** "compact **Lie group**, isomorphic as a **Lie group** to $\mathrm{SU}(2)$" and "the **Lie algebra** is $\mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H}\cong\mathfrak{su}(2)\cong\mathfrak{so}(3)$ — three dimensions of pure imaginary, with **bracket = quaternion commutator**" all appear before any in-page definition or callback to `lie-algebras.html#axioms`. The §2 callback aside *is* present (line 481) and links to `lie-algebras.html#axioms`, which is good — but the prose introduces "Lie algebra", "$\mathfrak{su}(2)$", "$\mathfrak{so}(3)$", "bracket" and "commutator" all in one sentence with no gloss. Compare to `lie-algebras.html` §1 which spends a paragraph on "$[x,x]=0$ … Jacobi" before using the term. Suggest one parenthetical: "the **Lie algebra** (the tangent space at the identity, with $[u,v]=uv-vu$)".
- **§2 line 475 note:** "the **spinor's signature**" appears with no definition or link. A reader who hasn't seen spin geometry will not parse this. Either gloss it ("a vector that needs a $4\pi$ rotation to return to itself") or drop the term — the prior sentence already says it.
- **§5 line 707 sketch:** "$x^2\le 0$" is used on line 708 without defining the partial order on a non-real algebra. It means "$x^2$ is a non-positive real" but the inequality is suggestive of a missing premise. One-clause gloss: "$x^2\in\mathbb{R}_{\le 0}$". The widget readout at line 726 writes `x² ∈ ℝ_{<0}` and gets it right (strict), but the prose says `≤` — minor inconsistency between widget and prose.
- **§7 (Connections) line 854:** "**$11$-dimensional supergravity**", "**$E_8$ lattice**", "**non-commutative geometry**" all appear in one sentence as terminal name-drops with no in-page anchor. Conventional for a Connections section, but `algebra.html` and `lie-algebras.html` both keep their connections lists shorter and link out to the relevant topic page. Consider trimming or adding a `pathway.html` callback.

### Tone mismatches
- **§5 sketch (line 708)** is a textbook-voiced one-paragraph proof outline: "Sketch. Let $D$ be such an algebra. Pick $x\in D\setminus\mathbb{R}$. Its minimal polynomial is real and irreducible, hence …" The voice is dry compared to §1's "Hamilton carved … Brougham Bridge" or §2's "the spinor's signature", and compared to `lie-algebras.html`'s register ("Skew-symmetry is the antisymmetric part of the commutator; the Jacobi identity is the trace of associativity that survives"). The widget below it (`frob-tree`) does interactive work — consider trimming the prose sketch to two sentences and letting the case-tree carry the proof structure.
- **§7 Connections wall (line 854):** "The 1, 2, 4, 8 phenomenon reaches well beyond algebra. The same dimensions parametrise the parallelisable spheres … Hopf fibrations … exceptional Lie group $G_2 = \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb O)$. Open frontiers include …" — five name-drops in two sentences with no narration breath. Compare to `lie-algebras.html` §7 Connections which does the same job with bulleted item-per-link clarity. The bullet list directly below (lines 857-861) already does this well; the lead paragraph could be cut by two sentences without loss.
- **British/US spelling:** "realisation" (263), "visualiser" (372, 385), "randomise" (273), "parametrise" (854), "parallelisable" (854) — peer pages are mixed too (`algebra.html` is mostly US; `lie-algebras.html` is mixed). No corpus-wide rule, but inside one page the suffixes drift between -ise and -ize. Choose one. Cosmetic.

### Missing worked examples
- **§3 (Cayley–Dickson tower)** has the ladder widget and a static table but no concrete worked doubling. The Cayley–Dickson formula `(a,b)(c,d) = (ac-d^*b, da+bc^*)` is given at line 490; readers would benefit from "let's double $\mathbb{C}$ to recover $\mathbb{H}$" with $a,b,c,d \in \mathbb{C}$ and the resulting four real coordinates. The widget at line 495 only inspects the *result* of each rung; nothing lets the reader compute the doubling.
- **§6 (Hurwitz)** introduces Bott–Milnor / Kervaire (line 781) and the four/eight/nine-square identities (lines 782-784) without any toy. The four-square identity is huge (line 783) and goes by with no narration; a small verifier ("plug in $a=1,b=2,c=0,d=0,e=0,f=1,g=2,h=0$, click verify, see both sides equal") would land it. Compare `algebra.html` §6 finite fields, where the Frobenius-orbit widget makes the abstract count concrete.
