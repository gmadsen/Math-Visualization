# spectral-graph-theory — pedagogical audit (2026-05)

**Section:** Combinatorics & graph theory
**Compared against:** expanders, extremal-combinatorics

## Summary
A strong, on-house-style page: 8 numbered sections, each with a working interactive widget, narrated in the conversational-but-precise tone the canonical template aims for. The two highest-priority issues are a semantic clash with `expanders.html` over what `\lambda_2` denotes (Laplacian's second-smallest vs. normalised adjacency's second-largest — same symbol, opposite "good direction"), and a `\frac{1}{1}` typo in the Courant–Fischer expression in §3. The rest is minor polish.

## Findings

### Notation drift
- **High priority — semantic, not cosmetic.** `\lambda_2` denotes the *second-smallest combinatorial-Laplacian* eigenvalue throughout `spectral-graph-theory.html` (e.g. §3 line 666: "$\lambda_2$ is the algebraic connectivity"; §4 line 826: "$\lambda_2/2 \le h(G) \le \sqrt{2 d \lambda_2}$"). In `expanders.html` §2 line 392–394 it denotes the *second-largest normalised-adjacency* eigenvalue: "$1 = \lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \cdots$ … spectral gap $1-\lambda_2 \ge \delta$". So a "small $\lambda_2$" means *bad expansion* on `expanders.html` but *bad expansion* on `spectral-graph-theory.html` requires *small $\lambda_2$ in the opposite direction* (the normalised-Laplacian $\nu_2$, which spectral-graph-theory introduces only in §5). Recommend a one-paragraph reconciliation note in spectral-graph-theory's §3 or §6 ("the symbol `\lambda_2` is overloaded across the literature; on this page it always means … on the sibling expanders page it means …"), or a footnote linking out at the first cross-page callback.
- **Medium — reuse of `\mathcal{L}`.** `spectral-graph-theory.html` introduces `\mathcal{L}` for the *symmetric normalised Laplacian* `D^{-1/2} L D^{-1/2}` in §5 line 986. `expanders.html` line 526–527 also writes `\mathcal{L}` for the same operator (`\mathcal{L} = I - \widehat A`). These agree in math but the spelling of *normalisation* drifts (`expanders.html` writes "normalized", "normalised" varies). Cosmetic only.
- **Cosmetic — `\widehat A` vs `D^{-1/2} A D^{-1/2}`.** `expanders.html` carries the symbol `\widehat A = A/d` for the regular-graph normalisation (line 392). `spectral-graph-theory.html` never uses `\widehat A`; it expands `D^{-1/2} A D^{-1/2}` inline every time (e.g. lines 986, 1308). Both work, but a reader bouncing between the two pages will not realise these are the same gadget. Recommend a one-liner cross-reference in spectral-graph-theory §5: "in the $d$-regular case this is $\widehat A = A/d$, the form expanders.html uses".
- **Cosmetic — `\mathbb{1}` indicator.** §5 line 984 writes `\mathbb{1}[j\sim i]` (blackboard-bold one) for the indicator. The convention elsewhere on the page and on `expanders.html` is the bold `\mathbf{1}` for the all-ones vector and indicator vectors `\mathbf{1}_S`. The `\mathbb{1}` here is locally introduced and never reused. Recommend `\mathbf{1}[j\sim i]` for parity.

### Undefined jargon
- "Dirichlet energy" appears in §2 line 551 ("constraining this Dirichlet energy") and §3 line 670 ("smooth means small Dirichlet energy"). The phrase is *named* without a one-line definition; the reader is expected to identify it with the energy form `f^T L f` quoted just above. The expanders / extremal pages do not invoke this terminology, so a reader landing here from one of them needs a parenthetical: "*Dirichlet energy* — the form `(1/2)\sum (f_i - f_j)^2` introduced above".
- "Courant–Fischer characterisation" §3 line 668 is named, then immediately followed by the formula. A short clause "(the variational characterisation of the second eigenvalue of a self-adjoint matrix)" would unblock readers without a functional-analysis prereq, especially since the cross-page link in §5 to `functional-analysis.html#spectral` shows the editor is aware they're leaning on it.
- "Rayleigh" appears as a bare proper-name reference in §2 line 551 ("Courant–Fischer, Rayleigh, Cheeger") with no definition or callback anywhere. Either drop it or annotate "Rayleigh quotient `f^T L f / f^T f`".
- "Hecke operators on weight-$2$ forms" / "automorphic representations" §6 line 1152: dropped without callback in a paragraph that the reader of a *graphs* page is unlikely to follow. The expanders page treats this material similarly briefly but at least anchors the depth claim in "Deligne's proof of the Ramanujan–Petersson conjecture" (line 918) and surrounds it with one paragraph of motivation. Recommend either a callback to `./modular-forms.html#hecke-action` (which expanders.html already cites) or a note "see expanders.html §6 for the full LPS story".
- "spectral measure of $T_d$" / "universal cover" — not invoked on spectral-graph-theory but the Alon–Boppana statement in §6 line 1149 is presented as a result without proof sketch; expanders §6 line 910 explains "the universal cover is $T_d$, so the spectrum of $G_n$ converges to that of $T_d$, whose top is $2\sqrt{d-1}$". Spectral-graph-theory's version reads as a cited fact; a one-liner would close the gap.

### Tone mismatches
- §1 line 312 hero sub-tagline ("graphs as *discrete differential geometry*") and the Coda §8 closing ("What spectral graph theory really teaches is that *finite* objects can be analysed by tools designed for the continuum…") match the canonical template's voice well — second-person, conversational, anchored in a thesis. No drift.
- §6 line 1152 has one isolated paragraph that drifts toward textbook-citation tone: "The story is one of the most beautiful applications of automorphic representations to combinatorics." Bare assertion of beauty without the cash-out the rest of the page provides. Either expand to a sentence saying *what* makes it beautiful (e.g. "a graph eigenvalue equals an integer of an `L`-function up to normalisation"), or trim.
- §5 line 994 subhead "A walk on a graph is a heat equation" is the right register — ditto §8 Coda's two-column dictionary. The page knows when to use a slogan-headline and does it well.
- No over-casual / meme-tone offences. No formulas-without-narration walls; every displayed math is wrapped in a claim and a follow-up.

### Missing worked examples
- All 8 numbered sections (§1 adjacency, §2 kernel, §3 Fiedler, §4 Cheeger, §5 random walks, §6 bipartite/expanders, §7 spectral clustering, §8 Coda) ship with at least one widget you can poke. §8 (the Coda) is intentionally a synthesis table, and that's fine — it is non-numbered ("Coda:" rather than "8."). No sections are pure-definition without a toy. Coverage is on par with `extremal-combinatorics.html` (6 widgets across 7 numbered sections, with §7 also a synthesis "Connections" section).
- Minor: §6 packs *three* big results (bipartite criterion, expander definition, Alon–Boppana, Ramanujan) into a single section with one widget (the bipartite spectrum visualiser). The Ramanujan / Alon–Boppana half has no toy — it's all prose. Consider either splitting into two sections or letting the §6 widget overlay the Alon–Boppana band the way `expanders.html` §6 line 1022 does (`shaded yellow band [-2\sqrt{d-1}, 2\sqrt{d-1}]`). The expanders page already nails this, and copying the gesture would let spectral-graph-theory's §6 widget speak to *both* halves of its prose.

### KaTeX macros / formatting
- **Bug — `\frac{1}{1}` at §3 line 669.** The Courant–Fischer expression reads `\min_{...} \frac{1}{1}\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}(f(i)-f(j))^2`. `\frac{1}{1}` renders as `1` and is almost certainly a fossil of `\frac{1}{2}` (which would match the energy form quoted at line 547 and again at line 551). Either drop the fraction (since `f^T L f` already swallows the `1/2`) or restore the `2`. This is a content bug, not a formatting one — the right-hand minimisation as written is *twice* `\lambda_2`.
- **Malformed close tag at line 551.** `<div class="note">…Dirichlet energy.</p></div>` — there is no opening `<p>` inside the div, so the `</p>` is spurious. The browser will tolerate it; the JSON round-trip will preserve it; but it's an obvious oddity that no other `.note` block on the page exhibits. Compare line 841's clean `<div class="note">…</div>` close.
- **Macro inventory matches template.** Page declares the same six-macro block (`\Spec`, `\Gal`, `\Hom`, `\tr`, `\ad`, `\ind`) as `category-theory.html` lines 22–29. None of them is actually used on this page — pure copy-paste from the template, which is the correct house behaviour. No locally-introduced macros, no homemade delimiters.
- **Cosmetic — section-symbol parity.** Inline references to other sections write `§2`, `§3`, etc. (e.g. line 549, 672, 827, 1140). The template (`category-theory.html`) and both peers use the same convention. Good.

minor polish (one content typo `\frac{1}{1}`, one semantic notation clash with `expanders.html` worth a reconciling note; otherwise on-house-style)
