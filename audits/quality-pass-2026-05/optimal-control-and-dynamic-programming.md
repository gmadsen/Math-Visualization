# optimal-control-and-dynamic-programming — pedagogical audit (2026-05)

**Section:** Control theory & optimization
**Compared against:** convex-optimization, mathematical-finance

## Summary
A strong, well-paced page: every numbered section has a working widget, the Pontryagin / HJB duality is the spine and the prose returns to it deliberately, and the closing Connections list is genuinely useful. A handful of sign-convention, jargon-ordering, and CRRA-domain wrinkles deserve a polish pass before promotion, but nothing structural.

## Findings
### Notation drift
- **Costate sign convention is in tension between this page and `convex-optimization.html`.** §3 uses `H = λᵀ f − L` with `u* = argmax H` and transversality `λ(T) = −∂_x Φ`, then in §4 says "Pontryagin reappears with `λ(t) = −∂_x V(x*(t),t)`." Convex-optimization's KKT section (`#lagrangian-duality`) writes the Lagrangian with `+λᵢgᵢ`, `λᵢ ≥ 0`, and stationarity `0 ∈ ∂f + Σλᵢ∂gᵢ` — the opposite sign on the multiplier. The page acknowledges the convention split parenthetically ("many texts write H = λᵀf + L and minimise; we use the cleaner 'maximise H' form"), but downstream cross-page traffic will collide. Recommend at least a one-line callback note when the cross-link from convex-optimization.html#connections lands at §3 here, since it advertises "the same Lagrangian / Hamiltonian structure runs in continuous time, with the co-state λ(t) playing the role of a multiplier" — but the sign flips.
- **`λ` reused for two unrelated things on the same page.** §3 uses `λ` for the costate vector; §6 introduces "Banach fixed-point" arguments with `γ` for the discount but never reuses `λ`, which is fine; however, §6's Bellman operator is called `T`, the same symbol as the horizon `T` from §1–§5. The prose says "Define the Bellman operator T: V↦TV" two paragraphs after `T` last appeared as "horizon T". A rename to `\mathcal{B}` or `\mathsf{T}` would prevent confusion (mathematical-finance.html keeps operators script: `\mathcal{L}^u`).
- **`u` as both control and conjugate-variable target.** Cosmetic: §1 establishes `u` = control. §6's "discount with γ ∈ [0,1)" then writes `min_u { c(x,u) + γV(f(x,u)) }` which is consistent. No drift inside the page, but the reader who chases the convex-optimization callback into §6 will encounter `u` as primal variable in convex-opt's KKT section — a low-stakes overload worth flagging only because both pages live in the same section.
- **`σ` overloaded in §7.** `σ(x,u,t)` is the diffusion coefficient, then in the Merton paragraph `σ` is the scalar volatility of the risky asset. Standard finance abuse, also done in mathematical-finance.html, so this is consistent with the section peer rather than drift — flagged for awareness only.
- **`\operatorname*{arg\,min}` / `\operatorname*{arg\,max}`**: target uses these consistently (§1, §3, §4). Convex-optimization uses `\arg\min` in §5 prose. Cosmetic; both render — but settling on `\operatorname*{arg\,min}` (the target's choice) gives the limits-below display behavior expected in display math. No change needed.

### Undefined jargon
- **"absolutely continuous co-state" (§3, in the Pontryagin statement note).** The page never defines absolute continuity, and there is no callback to real-analysis. For a graduate-notebook audience this is borderline acceptable, but the conventions of category-theory.html prefer either a one-clause gloss ("differentiable a.e. with integrable derivative") or a callback to `./real-analysis.html`. Mathematical-finance.html cleanly callbacks to `./stochastic-calculus.html#sde` for `dB_t`.
- **"weak lower semicontinuity in the calculus of variations" (§1, third bullet).** Used as if known: "convexity of f-in-u is the optimal-control analog of weak lower semicontinuity in the calculus of variations." No definition, no callback to variational-methods.html. Convex-optimization.html when it reaches for analogous machinery (§8 connections list) at least name-drops the destination link.
- **"Filippov's theorem" (§1).** Stated by name with no citation or callback for the curious. A one-liner gloss ("essentially the Banach–Alaoglu / weak compactness argument applied to controls") or even just a parenthetical year would match the page's habit elsewhere of dating Pontryagin (1956), Crandall–Lions (1983), Howard (1960), Merton (1969).
- **"transversality condition" (§3, inside the Pontryagin note).** First use is inside the boxed statement: "with the *transversality condition* `λ*(T) = −∂_x Φ(x*(T))`." The italics signal "term being defined", but the proof scrubber widget uses the phrase again without unpacking *why* it's called transversality (orthogonality of variations to admissible terminal directions). One sentence would close the loop.
- **"viscosity solution" (§4, "Two pathologies and one rescue").** Defined in-place as "a Lipschitz V that satisfies one-sided differential inequalities at every test point." That's the right level of detail for the page's voice, but "test point" is itself jargon (test functions in PDE theory). Either swap "test point" → "smooth test function touching V from above or below," or accept the imprecision with a callback to the upcoming partial-differential-equations.html section. Currently no callback fires.
- **"adapted" (§7).** "The control u_t is now *adapted* (depends only on the history of W up to time t)." The parenthetical does the work — this one is fine. Flagged here only as a positive contrast to the items above.
- **"open-loop schedule" vs "feedback law" (§4, "From the value to the optimal feedback").** The terms are introduced together but neither is defined; the contrast is implicit. A reader new to control wouldn't be able to tell from this paragraph alone what an "open-loop schedule" *is* mechanically. One half-sentence — "(a control prescribed as a function of `t` alone, computed before the trajectory is run)" — would close it.
- **"Polyak averaging"** does not appear in the target (no false flag); just noting that mathematical-finance.html and convex-optimization.html define their probability/stochastic terms before use, and §7 here mostly does so too.

### Tone mismatches
- **§1 closing slogan is excellent and matches the canonical voice** — "without controllability, the question is wrong; without convexity, the answer might not exist." This is exactly the conversational-but-precise register category-theory.html sets, and matches the tone of mathematical-finance.html's "thumb rule" callout.
- **§5 "It's the answer your textbook autopilot is computing"** — good instinct, lands the LQR claim with concrete imagery. Convex-optimization.html does the same in §4 with "diet, transportation" examples. No issue.
- **§7 Merton paragraph closes with "Mean-variance investing recovers Markowitz; the γ that calibrates risk aversion makes it the bridge between behaviour and dynamics."** The first clause is misleading — Merton's CRRA result is *not* the same as Markowitz mean-variance optimisation; they coincide only in the log-utility limit / single-period special case. Mathematical-finance.html §4 is more careful: "Higher Sharpe ratios mean larger positions; higher risk aversion (γ↑) or higher volatility (σ↑) shrink the position toward zero." Recommend rewording the target line to avoid the false equivalence (this is borderline a *semantic* finding, not just tone).
- **§7 CRRA domain claim has a subtle wrong note.** Target says "(CRRA preferences, γ∈(0,1)∪(1,∞))" — true for the formula `c^{1-γ}/(1-γ)`, but the closed-form `u* = (μ−r)/(γσ²)` requires `γ > 0` only and the page's own widget slider runs `0.5 ≤ γ ≤ 6` (skipping `γ=1` which is the log-utility limit, fine). The text should briefly note `γ=1` is the log-utility limit handled by `u(c)=log c` rather than by L'Hôpital — the omission reads as a typo rather than a deliberate choice. Mathematical-finance.html `#merton-portfolio` writes "γ>0, γ≠1" with the same gap; resolving it once across both pages is the cleanest fix.
- **§6 closing flourish "*This* is Bellman's 'principle of optimality' in action: never re-solve a subproblem you've already solved."** Strong. No change.
- **§4 third "pathology" bullet (viscosity solutions) starts mid-thought** with "Crandall & Lions (1983) introduced..." — this is fine prose but it's labelled as a pathology in the heading "Two pathologies and one rescue" while in fact being the *rescue*. Reordering the heading to "Two pathologies, one rescue" or labelling the third bullet `**Rescue:**` to mirror the strongs in the first two would make the rhythm explicit.
- **No formula-walls-without-narration** — every display block is sandwiched between explanatory prose. Good.

### Missing worked examples
- **§2 (Euler–Lagrange / Bolza).** The Bolza ↔ Mayer widget toggles between two cost displays for the same trajectory; that demonstrates the *equivalence* but not the *Euler–Lagrange equation itself*. There is no concrete E–L computation worked out (e.g., the brachistochrone, or just `L = ½(ẋ² − x²)` → `ẍ + x = 0`). For comparison: §3 has the variational derivation in one frame, §5 fully derives the Riccati ODE. §2 is the lightest section by computational substance — adding even a one-line brachistochrone or harmonic-oscillator E–L derivation would lift it.
- **§3 (Pontryagin).** No fully worked PMP problem on a concrete plant. The proof scrubber walks the *derivation* but not an *application*. Convex-optimization.html §3 runs the Slater proof scrubber *and* the KKT geometry widget on a pentagonal LP (an actual problem). A bang-bang time-optimal example for the double integrator (linking back to §1's reachable-set widget) would be the natural worked computation, and would also reinforce the "Pontryagin already at work" comment in §1's note.
- **§7 (Stochastic DP).** The Merton widget exercises the *formula* `u* = (μ−r)/(γσ²)` but never derives it. The text says "HJB plus the ansatz `V(X)=KX^{1-γ}/(1-γ)` collapses to closed form" with no intermediate steps shown. Mathematical-finance.html §5 also gestures at this without working it out, but has a separate `#hjb` section with a Bellman recursion widget on a binomial tree. The target page conflates Merton with the only stochastic-control example, so adding the algebra (one display block: substitute ansatz, take ∂/∂u inside the inf, solve linearly for u*) would close the gap.
- **All other sections** (1, 4, 5, 6, 8) have at least one worked computation or interactive that exercises the section's main object. Good.
