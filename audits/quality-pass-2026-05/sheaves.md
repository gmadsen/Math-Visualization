# sheaves — pedagogical audit (2026-05)

**Section:** Algebraic geometry
**Compared against:** schemes, sheaf-cohomology

## Summary
The page is rich, well-structured, and pedagogically ambitious — every numbered section has prose worthy of a graduate text and most have at least one interactive widget. The two genuine concerns are (a) a noticeable sheaf-letter notation drift (`F` vs the peers' `\mathcal{F}`) that becomes jarring when a reader bounces from sheaves.html to sheaf-cohomology.html, and (b) one authorial-draft slip in the §9 worked example that shipped a "let's redo:" thought-out-loud sentence into the published prose. Three sections (§2, §4, §8) lack interactive widgets despite being substantial.

## Findings

### Notation drift
- **Sheaf letter (semantic, high priority).** sheaves.html consistently writes a sheaf as a plain italic `$F$` (lines 280, 313, 374, 822, 1183, 1192, 1197, 1205, 1207, 1287, …) with `\mathcal{F}` reserved mostly for $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules from §8 onward. By contrast, sheaf-cohomology.html uses `\mathcal{F}` uniformly from line 285 onward (`H^i(X, \mathcal{F})`, `\mathcal{F}'`, `\mathcal{F}''`, etc.), and schemes.html doesn't deal in sheaves of arbitrary type but does write its only sheaf — the structure sheaf — as `\mathcal{O}_X`. A reader who finishes §3 of sheaves with $0 \to F' \to F \to F'' \to 0$ in their head, then opens sheaf-cohomology.html, hits `0 \to \mathcal{F}' \to \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{F}''` (line 546) and silently re-types. Recommend normalizing sheaves.html to `\mathcal{F}` (which it already does for $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules in §8/§9, so this is internal-consistency drift too).
- **`\Spec` spacing.** sheaves.html uses `\Spec\,R` with thin-space (lines 275, 290, 437, 832, 837, 1452, 1454, 1509, 1515, 1532), as does sheaf-cohomology.html (lines 515, 519, 524, 684, 801, 816). schemes.html sometimes uses `\Spec(R)` (line 221), sometimes `\Spec{}R` (lines 1416, 1532, 1534), sometimes `\Spec\mathbb{Z}`. Sheaves+sheaf-cohomology agree; the schemes outlier is not sheaves' problem. No action.
- **`R\text{-mod}` casing.** sheaves.html line 290 writes `$R\text{-mod} \simeq \mathsf{QCoh}(\Spec\,R)$` (lowercase "mod") but line 314 writes `$R\text{-Mod}$` (capital), and line 1518 uses `R\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}` in the §9 theorem statement. Three different spellings on one page. Pick one — `R\text{-Mod}` matches the `\mathsf{Sh}, \mathsf{PSh}, \mathsf{QCoh}, \mathsf{Coh}, \mathsf{Ab}` pattern used everywhere else.
- **`\mathcal{H}\!om` macro.** sheaves.html and sheaf-cohomology.html both reach for `\mathcal{H}\!om` (sheaves L1197, 1198, 1199, 1443, 1462, 1480; sheaf-cohomology L740). sheaf-cohomology line 740 wraps it in a redundant `\mathrm{}`: `\mathrm{\mathcal{H}\!om}(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{O}_X)` — sheaves.html has the cleaner spelling. Cosmetic; no change to sheaves.html needed.

### Undefined jargon
- **§1 line 283**, "Theorems A and B" of Cartan-Serre and "Stein manifolds" both invoked in the historical-motivation paragraph with no parenthetical gloss or callback. The page does later link to `several-complex-variables.html#stein` from a Used-in aside, but the forward reader has no anchor. Recommend a half-sentence ("Theorems A and B — that coherent sheaves on a Stein manifold have vanishing higher cohomology and are generated by global sections").
- **§1 line 283**, "$\mathcal{O}_X$-modules" appears in the second-to-last sentence of the motivation but is not defined until §8. Acceptable as a forward reference but worth a parenthetical "(defined in §8)".
- **§1 line 290**, "$R\text{-mod} \simeq \mathsf{QCoh}(\Spec\,R)$" — the symbol $\mathsf{QCoh}$ appears in the very first paragraph of the page; "quasi-coherent" is not defined until §9. Add "(definition in §9)" to the sub or move the slogan to the §9 intro.
- **§2 line 343**, "the étale, fppf, and fpqc topologies" — three acronyms in a list with no expansion. Even a parenthetical like "(faithfully flat finitely presented; faithfully flat quasi-compact)" would land for the prerequisite-checking reader.
- **§5 line 832**, the parenthetical "(this can include generizations)" for the skyscraper stalk is dropped without prior definition of "generization" — a Zariski-topology term peers don't use until specialized contexts.
- **§6 line 1217**, `\mathrm{Pic}(X)` is used in the long exact sequence display before being introduced as an object of study; it is properly defined in §8 line 1477. A forward link or a one-clause gloss "(the Picard group, classified in §8)" would close the loop. sheaf-cohomology.html has §8 "The Picard group as $H^1$" so this is sheaves' job to set up.
- **§8 line 1466**, the "qcqs" abbreviation appears in a table cell without expansion; line 1473 then expands it in prose. Swap the order — define before using in the table.
- **§9 §10**, "Hilbert's syzygy theorem", "Serre's theorem", "Riemann–Roch theorem" are name-dropped in the coda without inline gloss; this is more "preview" than "jargon" but a reader would benefit from one-clause hints.

### Tone mismatches
- **§9 line 1527 — semantic, high priority.** The worked example for $\widetilde{\mathbb{Z}/6}(D(2))$ shipped with thinking-out-loud authorial draft text: `"$\widetilde{M}(D(2)) = (\mathbb{Z}/6)_2 = \mathbb{Z}/3$, since inverting $2$ in $\mathbb{Z}/6$ kills the ideal generated by $3$? — let's redo: $\mathbb{Z}/6 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/3$ by CRT…"`. The first half, with the question mark and "let's redo:", is internal monologue; only the CRT-decomposition explanation should remain. This is unique on the page and stands out badly against the polished §6/§7 prose.
- **§6 widget caption** ("Stalk-wise vs. globally — a stalkwise surjection that is not globally surjective"). The widget itself is a static two-panel illustration with no controls — it's a figure dressed as a `<div class="widget">`. Compare to schemes.html `#w-specz` (input field driving live evaluation) or sheaf-cohomology.html `#w-circle` (sliders moving cocycle classes). Either trim the chrome to a plain `<figure>`/note or wire up an interaction (toggle to reveal the lift, slider for branch cut, etc.).
- **§5 line 850 espace-étalé prose** is excellent but the immediately-following widget hint reads "slide $x$ along $X$ · see the stalk $F_x$ lifted, with each section picking one germ per point" — a bit abstract for a discoverability hint compared to the peers' "type an integer · hover a prime" (schemes) or "Drag the twist to see the $H^1$ generator" (sheaf-cohomology). Concretize: "slide $x$ from 0 to 10 · watch the yellow / pink section dots agree on $(3.8, 5.2)$ and split elsewhere."
- **§4 line 768-770 categorical-preliminaries h3** is dense prose with no example: "Limits in $\mathsf{Sh}(X)$ are computed pointwise … embed each stalk $F_x$ into an injective $\mathbb{Z}$-module $I_x$, and form the sheaf $\prod_x i_{x*}I_x$." This is house-style at its dryest; the peers (sheaf-cohomology §1, schemes §1) break this kind of paragraph with a "Why care?" sentence or a slogan. Add one.

### Missing worked examples
- **§2 (Presheaves) — no widget.** Substantial section with seven prose subsections (Examples, Restriction and functoriality, Morphisms of presheaves, The site perspective, The functor of points) and no poke-able toy. A small "click an open subset → see its image under restriction" interactive (or even just a clickable Hasse-diagram of opens with arrows for restriction maps) would match house style.
- **§4 (Key examples) — no widget.** Has a static "Summary table" of $C^0_X, C^\infty_X, \mathcal{O}_X, B, \underline{S}^{\mathrm{pre}}, \underline{S}, i_{x*}A, f_*F$ but nothing the reader can poke. The skyscraper sheaf in particular cries out for a slider widget ("move $x$ along $X$, watch $(i_{x*}A)(U)$ flip between $A$ and $\{\ast\}$").
- **§8 ($\mathcal{O}_X$-modules) — no widget.** Has a checklist table for operations $\oplus, \otimes, \mathcal{H}\!om, \Lambda^k, \mathrm{Sym}^k, f^*, f_*$ and no interactive toy. A "rank table for vector bundles" or a "tensor-product fiber-dimension explorer" would match the style of §9's `#w-qcoh`.
- **§3 has two widgets** (gluing diagnostic + equalizer) and **§9 has one** (sections of $\widetilde{M}$ over $D(f)$); these are well-judged. §5 has two (espace étalé + germ agreement) and §7 has one (sheafify the constant presheaf). The asymmetric distribution puts §2/§4/§8 conspicuously light.

### KaTeX macros / formatting
- All macros used (`\Spec, \Hom`) are in the canonical macros block (lines 23-29). Helper-block at line 188-251 is verbatim (`$, $$, SVG, ensureArrow, drawArrow, drawNode`) plus two extras (`gcd`, `factorize`) annotated as "used by §9 widget" — fine.
- **No locally-invented macros.** The `\mathcal{H}\!om`, `\widetilde{M}`, `\underline{S}^{\mathrm{pre}}`, `\underline{S}` constructions are standard KaTeX.
- **§6 line 1207 spacing nit.** The display `$$0 \to \Gamma F' \to \Gamma F \to \Gamma F'' \to H^1 F' \to H^1 F \to H^1 F'' \to H^2 F' \to \cdots$$` writes `H^1 F'` as juxtaposition rather than `H^1(X, F')` or `H^1(F')` — slightly nonstandard. The reference page sheaf-cohomology.html line 547 writes `H^0(X, \mathcal{F}')` etc. with explicit parens. Cosmetic but worth aligning.
- **§3 line 387** uses the cosimplicial-style display `$\xrightarrow{\ \ \ }$` (literally three spaces inside the over-arrow brace) to suggest "etc." — looks fine in KaTeX but is unusual; sheaf-cohomology.html uses `\xrightarrow{\;\cdot 2\pi i\;}` with thin-space `\;`. No semantic change needed; mention only because if a future reader normalizes spacing they should know the convention.
- **Helper-block / widget-chrome hygiene.** Every widget on the page uses `.widget / .hd / .ttl / .hint / .readout / .row` chrome correctly. SVGs all carry `viewBox` and `<title>` (lines 413, 429, 865, 876, 1187, 1345 verified). No ad-hoc classes. The §9 `#w-qcoh` widget's readout calls `renderMathInElement` directly (line 1676) to re-render after innerHTML mutation — same pattern schemes.html uses (line 384 `hideValue`). No drift.

## Severity
minor polish (with one genuine semantic patch — fix the §9 `let's redo:` stray draft text — and one judgment call on whether to add widgets to §2/§4/§8 or leave them as the page's "definition-heavy stretches").
