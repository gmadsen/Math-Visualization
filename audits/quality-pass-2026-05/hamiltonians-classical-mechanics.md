# hamiltonians-classical-mechanics — pedagogical audit (2026-05)

**Section:** Mathematical physics
**Compared against:** schrodinger-equation, special-relativity

## Summary
A confident, well-scaffolded page: each of the six sections has a concrete, interactive widget, the prose moves from geometry to mechanics in the same conversational-but-precise voice that `category-theory.html` sets, and the worked-examples section is a genuine pedagogical asset that the two reference peers lack. Drift is mostly cosmetic; one notation choice (the symbol `$\omega$` reused for symplectic form, frequency, and KAM frequency vector) is the only semantic snag worth flagging.

## Findings
### Notation drift
- **`$\omega$` overloading (semantic, high priority).** §1 introduces `$\omega = -d\theta$` as the symplectic 2-form. §6 then writes `$H = \tfrac{p^2}{2m} + \tfrac{1}{2}m\omega^2 q^2$` for the harmonic-oscillator angular frequency, and §5 uses `$\omega_i(I) = \partial H/\partial I_i$` for action-angle frequencies (and again `$\omega_1, \omega_2$` for KAM). The KAM widget readout even says `"ω₂/ω₁ = 1.610"`. Within a single page, `$\omega$` therefore denotes a 2-form, a scalar frequency, and a frequency vector. The references avoid this: `schrodinger-equation.html` uses `$\omega$` only for oscillator frequency; `special-relativity.html` doesn't use it at all. Recommend disambiguating the §6 oscillator and §5 KAM frequencies with `$\omega_0$` (or just `$\nu$`) and a one-sentence forward warning at the introduction of the symplectic form.
- **`$T^*Q$` vs `$T^* Q$` spacing (cosmetic).** Target writes `$T^*Q$` and `$T^*M$` consistently — fine. No drift against references (neither uses cotangent bundles), but worth a heads-up that the `\Hom`-style `\operatorname` macros in the page header are unused here.
- **`$\mathbb{R}$` / `$\mathbb{Z}$`.** Target uses `\mathbb{R}` (e.g. `H\colon T^*Q\to\mathbb{R}`) and `\mathbb{Z}^n` (KAM Diophantine condition). Matches `schrodinger-equation` (`L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)`) and `special-relativity` (`\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})`). No drift.
- **`$L_i$` for angular momentum.** §4 defines $L_i = \epsilon_{ijk} q^j p^k$ in the Noether table; §6 then uses `$L = q\times p$` (no index) and `$L$` as a scalar magnitude in the Kepler widget readout (`L=0.65`). The slider is labelled `$L$` — fine in 2D, but the §4 definition was vector/3D. A one-line note ("in the 2D Kepler diagram below, $L$ denotes the scalar $z$-component") would close the gap.

### Undefined jargon
- **"Lagrangian submanifold" (§3).** `"The graph … is a Lagrangian submanifold of $(T^*Q\times T^*Q,\,\omega\ominus\omega)$"` — the term and the `$\ominus$` notation are introduced in passing with no definition or callback. Reference peers either define analogous structural terms inline (Schrödinger §1: `"Self-adjointness of $H$ on its natural domain (Sobolev space $H^2$ for nice $V$)"`) or callback-link them (`functional-analysis.html#hilbert`). Recommend either a one-line gloss ("a half-dimensional submanifold on which $\omega$ vanishes") or a callback to `symplectic-manifolds.html`.
- **"Birkhoff zones" (§5).** `"Resonant tori … shatter into thin Birkhoff zones with chaotic layers"` — first and only mention. The reader has no purchase. A parenthetical ("thin annular regions of bounded chaotic motion between surviving tori") would help.
- **"Birkhoff series" / "homoclinic point" (§6).** `"The failure of the Birkhoff series to converge near a homoclinic point opens the door to chaos"` — both terms appear without definition or callback. The peer pages reliably gloss specialist terms inline (special-relativity §3: `"proper orthochronous transformations $\mathrm{SO}^+(1,3)$, i.e. those with $\det\Lambda = +1$ (proper, no parity flip)"`).
- **"WKB limit" / "eikonal limit" (§3 note).** Casual mention in the HJ→Schrödinger aside. The aside is `<aside class="note">` so the bar is lower, but a reader of this page may not have seen WKB. Matching the references' habit of inlining a definition would be ideal.
- **"Cantor-like set of positive measure" (§5).** Acceptable for a graduate audience but a borderline case; the reference pages tend to gloss measure-theoretic vocabulary on first use.

### Tone mismatches
- **Voice generally matches.** Hero subtitle ("Phase space is a symplectic manifold and physics is its geometry") and confident slogans ("Phase space is a symplectic manifold by birthright", "Each torus carries a quasi-periodic linear flow") are squarely in the conversational-but-precise register that `category-theory.html` sets. Both references are at the same temperature.
- **Second-person addressing is sparser than in `schrodinger-equation.html`.** Schrödinger §1 says `"once you have it, Stone's theorem hands you a one-parameter unitary group"`; special-relativity §1 says `"if you ride a train at speed $u$ and shine a flashlight forward"`. The target rarely steps into second person — §3 says only `"can we choose $S(q, t)$ so that the transformed Hamiltonian vanishes? If so, the new coordinates are constants of motion and we've integrated the dynamics"` (good first-person plural). One or two more "you"-framed walkthroughs (e.g. in §5 around action-angle change of variables) would tighten the match.
- **§6 "What's left out" is excellent.** The `<h3>What's left out</h3>` paragraph on the three-body problem is the kind of editorial honesty the references model well. No drift.
- **No over-casual / meme tone, no formula-walls without narration.** Energy-conservation derivation in §2 (`$\dot H = X_H(H) = dH(X_H) = \omega(X_H, X_H) = 0$`) is the densest formula chain on the page and it's bookended by `"Two structural facts fall out of the defining identity, almost for free"` and the quoted `"because $\omega$ is antisymmetric — geometry, not a separate axiom"`. Healthy.

### Missing worked examples
- **All six numbered sections have at least one widget.** §1 phase-space cell (Liouville), §2 Hamiltonian flow (4 H choices), §3 canonical-transform area-preservation (4 maps), §4 Noether conserved-quantity tracker (3 systems × 3 quantities), §5 KAM tori with perturbation, §6 Kepler orbits. This is denser than `special-relativity.html` (one widget per concept, no worked-examples section) and matches `schrodinger-equation.html` (which also runs ~6 widgets).
- **§6 does the heavy lifting that the peers omit.** A dedicated "Worked examples" section with four canonical systems (oscillator, Kepler, geodesic flow, billiards) plus a "What's left out" coda is a real pedagogical strength. The reference peers do not have an analogous section.
- **§3 widget tests one direction only.** The `Canonical transformations preserve area` widget shows that all three canonical maps yield `det J = 1` and the non-canonical one doesn't — a clean confirmation, but it never visualises *generating-function*-derived transformations or the HJ equation. The HJ equation in particular has no toy. A one-paragraph narrated example (e.g. HJ for the free particle: `S(q,t) = q^2/2t` separates) would round out the section without needing a new widget.
- **§4 Noether table is good but the conserved-quantity widget can't actually exhibit angular momentum on the 1D oscillator.** Selecting `osc` + `L` produces the readout `"Angular momentum L only defined for the 2D central potential"` — correct guard, but the only system that *can* exhibit `L` is the central potential, which is also the only one whose dynamics aren't visualised in the widget. A small phase-portrait alongside the time series (as `§2`'s widget already shows) would make the conservation visible rather than just numeric.

### KaTeX macros / formatting
- **No locally-defined macros beyond the page header.** Header declares `\Spec, \Gal, \Hom, \tr, \ad, \ind` (the standard set, copied verbatim from references). None are used in the body of this topic — minor surface area but harmless. Both reference peers carry the same unused macros.
- **No invented delimiters.** All math uses the canonical `$…$`, `$$…$$`, `\(…\)`, `\[…\]` set; no `\begin{equation}` or stray `\[\]` outside the loader's whitelist.
- **`\ominus` in §3 is non-standard but supported by KaTeX.** `(T^*Q\times T^*Q,\,\omega\ominus\omega)` uses `\ominus` to mean the difference symplectic form `(\omega, -\omega)`. The symbol renders, but the meaning is field-specific jargon — see the "undefined jargon" bullet on Lagrangian submanifolds.
- **`<select>` options contain raw KaTeX.** §2 (`hf-H`) and §3 (`ct-map`) `<select>` elements have option labels like `"$H = (p^2 + q^2)/2$ (harmonic oscillator)"`. The page already loads `js/katex-select.js` (line 178) so the LaTeX-in-options will render in the custom popover — good. No fix needed; flagging only because it's the gotcha called out in `AGENTS.md`.
- **Helper-block spot-check.** Top-of-body `<script>` block (lines 187-239) defines `$`, `$$`, `SVG`, `ensureArrow`, `drawArrow`, `drawNode` byte-for-byte the same as `category-theory.html` and `special-relativity.html`. (Note: `schrodinger-equation.html` is identical too; `special-relativity.html` line 234 actually has a small bug — `cx,cy:y` instead of `cx:x,cy:y` in `drawNode` — but the *target* is correct.) No deviation in the target.
- **Widget chrome is canonical.** Every widget uses `.widget / .hd / .ttl / .hint / .row / .readout / .note / .ok` exactly as the spec requires. No ad-hoc classes. SVGs include `viewBox` and `aria-label`.
- **Unicode-in-readouts (cosmetic).** Some readouts mix LaTeX (`$\omega_2/\omega_1 = 1.610$` in the KAM hint) with raw Unicode (`ω₂/ω₁ = ${ratio.toFixed(3)}` in the JS-emitted readout text on line 892, and `q² + q²` Unicode subscripts in §1's button label). This is consistent with the references' own pragmatism (Schrödinger writes `t = ${t.toFixed(1)}    width σ(t) = …`) — call it idiomatic, not drift.
