# modularity-and-flt — pedagogical audit (2026-05)

**Section:** Modular forms & L-functions
**Compared against:** bsd, galois-representations

## Summary
Pedagogically strong: the §1–§3 FLT chain (Frey → modularity → Ribet) reads like the page's author actually loves the proof, with five rich, distinct widgets and a clear contradiction narrative. §4 (deformation rings) and §5 (R = T) compress hard a lot of machinery into ~120 lines each — the Selmer/Hecke/Taylor–Wiles vocabulary lands fast and largely without examples. A handful of small-but-concrete drifts on macros and notation are listed below.

## Findings
### Notation drift
- Residual representation: this page writes `\overline\rho_{E,p}` consistently (lines 274, 365, 686, 692, 794, 812, 954, 1094). galois-representations.html `#mod` writes `\rho_{f,\lambda}` and never bars its modular Galois rep, but the close cousin `\bar{\mathbb{Q}}` (lines 278, 287, 315, 614) consistently uses `\bar{...}` rather than `\overline{...}`. Recommend pick one form: `\overline\rho` is fine, but match the bar convention used for `\bar{\mathbb{Q}}` cited from galois-reps in the §3 callback (low priority — both render the same; cosmetic).
- The `\GL_2` macro is invoked at line 669 in the auto-injected backlink (`Modularity: the proven $\GL_2$ case`) but `\GL` is not defined in this page's macro block (lines 37–44). bsd.html and galois-representations.html also lack `\GL`. The whole rest of this page uses `\mathrm{GL}_2(\cdot)` (lines 274, 536, 954, 956, 957, 960). The backlink will render literal `\GL_2` (high priority — semantic, visible in the "Used in" panel of §2). Either add `'\\GL':'\\mathrm{GL}'` to the macro block or rewrite the backlink source on the originating page.
- Frobenius trace symbol drift: galois-representations.html uses `\tr\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_p)` (lines 548, 552, 597) via the `\tr` macro. modularity-and-flt.html only refers to "Frobenius trace" in prose (line 533, 537) and `a_p(E)`-style numerics; never invokes `\tr`. No drift in formulas, just an opportunity (low priority — when §4–§5 quote `tr`-as-data they could use `\tr` instead of writing it out).
- Eigenvalue/coefficient subscript: `a_p(E)` (modularity §2) vs `a_p` (bsd §2 and galois-reps §5). All three pages overload `a_p` for both Frobenius trace and Fourier coefficient depending on context — this page handles the dual usage cleanly with the parenthetical disambiguator `a_p(E)` vs `a_p(f_E)` (no drift, called out as a positive).

### Undefined jargon
- "tamely ramified" appears at line 274 ("tamely ramified with a very simple shape at the bad primes") with no inline gloss. galois-representations §9 (`#ram`) defines inertia and the Artin conductor exponent but never lands the words "tame" / "wild" — the only place those appear in the corpus pre-FLT is in §1's bullet ("encodes wild/tame ramification" at line 287) without a definition. Cross-page callback exists, but the term is still unglossed at first use here. Quote: "tamely ramified with a very simple shape at the bad primes." Suggest a one-clause inline definition or a glossary popover.
- "newform" appears at line 531 in the modularity theorem statement and again at 686, 690, 692, 696 — the page assumes the reader knows newform vs eigenform vs cusp form. modular-forms.html owns the definition; this page's prereq link in the hero (line 255) covers it, but a 6-word inline tag like "(eigenform of new level)" at first occurrence would be safer. Low priority.
- "Steinberg at a Steinberg prime" (line 963) is name-dropped without explanation in §4's `<div class="note">`. No glossary callback. The term has no upstream definition in galois-representations.html (which only mentions "Steinberg representation" zero times). Quote: "Steinberg at a Steinberg prime, etc." Either gloss or strike — currently it functions as decoration.
- "ordinary at $p$" (lines 963, 974, 991) — first use is in the Selmer-cutdown note with no callback. galois-representations.html does not define "ordinary" either. Low–medium priority; consider a 5-word inline ("ordinary = unit Frobenius eigenvalue").
- "Steinberg multiplicative reduction" subtype is implicit but the simpler "multiplicative reduction" is well-supported by the §1 Tate's-algorithm note (line 272) — fine.
- "Eichler–Shimura and Deligne" (line 1033) named without callback. galois-representations §7 explains Deligne's construction; a See-also link in the §5 callback list would close the loop (currently the §5 callback only points to `hecke-operators.html#algebra`).

### Tone mismatches
- §1–§3 hit the house voice well: confident second-person hint cells ("the calculator below makes the bookkeeping explicit"), narrative connectives ("The contradiction that closes the tile is Wiles'…"). bsd.html's hero has a comparable rhythm ("Count points… and multiply… born on EDSAC printouts"). No drift here — flagged as a positive.
- §4 § "Cutting down by ramification data" (lines 963–965) is a single dense paragraph with three undefined technical conditions in a list ("unramified outside $\Sigma$, ordinary at $p$, Steinberg at a Steinberg prime, etc.") followed by a Selmer-group formula. Compared to galois-representations §9 which builds inertia → conductor over two paragraphs with a worked formula `f_p = 1` for multiplicative reduction, this lands as textbook compression rather than the page's earlier conversational mode. Medium priority.
- §5 lead-in (lines 1031–1041): the sentence "Surjectivity is easy (the modular Galois representations visibly land in $R_\Sigma$). The miracle is:" is great house voice. The paragraph that follows describing Taylor–Wiles patching (line 1041) is one 80-word sentence packed with "auxiliary sets," "augmented rings," "regular sequence of the same length," "compatible limit." Compare bsd §3's two-clause sentences and §4's "Why this is hard" callout. Medium priority — split into 2–3 sentences with at least one concrete number (e.g. "in Wiles' original proof, $|Q_n| \sim n$").
- The "Coda — beyond semistable, toward Langlands" `<h3>` at line 936 is well-paced and appropriately conversational — flagged positive.

### Missing worked examples
- §4 (Deformation rings): the only widget is `Widget 4 · Deformation ring dimension counter` (lines 967–977), which is a three-toggle "toy count" with the readout admitting "(toy count)" and `R_Σ` reduced to `Z_p[[x_1,…,x_n]]`. No actual worked example of an `R^\mathrm{univ}` is computed for any specific `\overline\rho` — e.g., the residual rep of `11a1` mod 3, or a Dihedral/CM case. Compared to bsd §1's `Mordell–Weil explorer` (six concrete curves with explicit `(A, B, rank, gen)`) and §2's point-count product, §4's widget is structurally a placeholder. Medium-high priority for a graduate notebook capstone — pick one residual rep and trace the deformation conditions through.
- §5 (R = T): `Widget 5 · Taylor–Wiles patching` (lines 1043–1050) is a slider that grows two stacks of generic boxes labelled `x_1, x_2,…` and `T_{\ell_1}, T_{\ell_2},…` and flips an `R ↠ T` text to `R = T` when `n ≥ 6`. There is no concrete Hecke algebra computed, no specific `\Sigma`, and the "patching level" 0–6 is dimensionless. Compared to bsd §3's `Rank-vs-L curve zoo` widget (six LMFDB curves with explicit `a_p`, `Ltay` leading coefficient, generators) this is again a schematic placeholder. Medium-high priority — even one row of `\mathbb{T}_\Sigma` for `11a1` would ground the section.
- §3 (Ribet) is well-served by *three* widgets (contradiction chain, level-lowering simulator, ribet stepper). §1 is well-served by Frey explorer + conductor calculator. §2 is well-served by modularity correspondence + trace mismatch meter. §4 and §5 are the asymmetry.

### KaTeX macros / formatting
- The page's macro block (lines 37–44) defines `\Spec, \Gal, \Hom, \tr, \ad, \ind` — the same canonical set used in category-theory.html. Does NOT define `\GL` (used at line 669, see notation drift) or `\Sha` (would be used in BSD-style refs but this page does not need it).
- bsd.html has a quirk worth noting in case it spreads: it declares `macros:{}` twice (once with `\Sha`, once with the canonical six) — the second silently overrides the first, so `\Sha` is actually undefined on bsd.html unless the `throwOnError` swallows it. Not this page's bug, but watch for a copy-paste mishap.
- Deformation widget readout (lines 988–994): the script stores `'Residual $\\overline\\rho$ ramified at $\\{2, p\\}$;'` then strips `\$` and `\\` with `.replace(/\\\$/g,'').replace(/\\\\/g,'')`, producing the plain string "Residual overlinerho ramified at {2, p}" inside `<div class="readout">`. The intent is clearly to avoid raw `$…$` in a `.readout` (correct, since KaTeX is not auto-rendering inside dynamically-injected `textContent`), but the result is the literal word "overlinerho" in front of the user. Low–medium priority; either drop the dollar-signs from the source string and use the actual unicode `ρ̄`, or use `innerHTML` + a `renderMathInElement` call after assignment.
- All other delimiter usage is conformant to AGENTS.md (`$…$`, `$$…$$`, `\(…\)`, `\[…\]` configured at lines 30–34); no ad-hoc delimiters introduced.
- Helper block at lines 187–235 is a verbatim copy of category-theory.html / bsd.html / galois-representations.html (`$`, `$$`, `SVG`, `ensureArrow`, `drawArrow`, `drawNode`) — clean.
- Widget chrome conformant: `.widget`, `.hd`, `.ttl`, `.hint`, `.row`, `.readout`, `.note`, `.ok`, `.bad` used throughout. No ad-hoc classes spotted. Custom rule at line 126 (`table.plain td.match{color:var(--green)}`) is a reasonable scoped extension, not a violation.
