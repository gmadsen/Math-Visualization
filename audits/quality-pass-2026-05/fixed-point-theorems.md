### Undefined jargon
- **§3 line 341 invokes "Riesz's lemma"** parenthetically as the reason the closed unit ball is non-compact in infinite dimensions, with no inline gloss and no callback to `functional-analysis.html` (where the lemma's content is implicitly used in §6 / §14 but not named). One-clause gloss ("the constructive non-compactness witness") or a `See also: functional-analysis.html#bigfour` would close the loop. Low priority — the surrounding sentence makes the role clear.
- **§5 line 409 introduces "best-response set $B_i(\sigma_{-i})$" and "mixed-strategy simplices"** without anchoring; these are the engine of the Nash example, not just decoration. A reader who has not seen game theory will recognize "Nash equilibrium" but not what makes the correspondence a non-singleton at ties. One sentence ("$B_i(\sigma_{-i}) = \arg\max_{\sigma_i} u_i(\sigma_i, \sigma_{-i})$, the set of player $i$'s utility-maximizing strategies given the opponents' profile $\sigma_{-i}$") would help.
- **§6 line 457 drops "viscosity solutions of Hamilton–Jacobi equations"** as a name with no follow-up. Compare to `functional-analysis.html`'s pattern of one-clause glosses for downstream applications. Cosmetic.
- **§7 line 563 names "upper-hemicontinuous correspondences" and "Browder's selection theorem"** in a single sentence without definition, then "Eilenberg–Montgomery or Browder" reappears at §Connections line 855 as a frontier note. Both are name-only. The §6 pattern of "Equivalence with Ekeland" gives one sentence to each named neighbor; this paragraph could match.
- **§7 line 549 says "fixed-point theory for set-valued maps"** before §5 (Kakutani) is reached in reading order? Actually §7 follows §5/§6, so set-valued maps are already on the table — no jargon-before-definition issue. Positive note.
- **§7 line 555 uses "barycentric coordinates"** for the first time without defining them; line 559 then uses them in the proof "$x=\sum_i x_i v_i$ in barycentric coordinates". The phrase is glossed implicitly by the formula but a one-clause "(weights summing to 1 at the simplex vertices)" would help readers who haven't seen them.
- **§Connections line 855 names "Conley index", "mountain-pass", "general-equilibrium economies", "Eilenberg–Montgomery"** in a single frontier sentence — appropriate for a §Connections sweep, matches `functional-analysis.html` §17 and `dynamical-systems.html` §13's frontier-note pattern. Positive.
