# measure-theory — pedagogical audit (2026-05)

**Section:** Analysis
**Compared against:** real-analysis, harmonic-analysis-fourier

## Summary
The page is in strong shape — 14 numbered sections, 11 widgets, 12 quiz placeholders, and a tightly written house-style narrative voice that exactly matches the canonical template (Riemann-vs-Lebesgue opener and the spike/sin-over-n DCT counterexamples are some of the best motivational widgets in the corpus). The one genuine load-bearing gap is §2 (`#sigma-algebras`) — the foundational definitions section — which has no widget at all; everything downstream stays poke-able. A handful of cosmetic notation drifts and one mild jargon-before-definition spot round out the findings.

## Findings

### Notation drift
- `\,d\mu` vs `dx`/`d\mu` (no thin space) is inconsistent within the page. Lines 790, 796, 804, 944, 951, 958, 1116, 1133, 1356, 1486 use `\,d\mu`; line 274 has `\tfrac{\sin x}{x}\,dx` (good); but the §6 `dx` in the integral list at line 783 and the iterated-integral options on line 1383–1384 (`$\int\!\int 1\,dx\,dy$`) sometimes elide the thin-space convention. Both peers are similar — `harmonic-analysis-fourier.html` is consistent with `\,dx` (lines 270, 272, 274, 429, 448), `real-analysis.html#multivariable-integration` line 1051 also uses `\,dy` / `\,dx`. Cosmetic; the dominant convention across all three pages is `\,d…`.
- `\mathcal{L}` is overloaded inside `measure-theory.html` itself: §1 line 276 uses `$\mathcal{L}$` for "the desired class of integrable functions" (informal, motivational), and §8 line 1106 then re-uses `$\mathcal{L}^p(\mu)$` for the seminormed space (formal, pre-quotient). A first-time reader meeting "the class $\mathcal{L}$" in §1 may carry the symbol forward and be confused when it reappears with a $p$-superscript meaning something narrower. **Semantic drift, low priority** — fix by either renaming the §1 informal class (`\mathcal{F}`, "good" class, etc.) or by adding a forward-pointer in §8 that says "§1's $\mathcal{L}$ becomes $\bigcup_p \mathcal{L}^p$ here."
- `\mathbf{1}_{E}` (bold one for indicator) is used in measure-theory (lines 272, 624, 967, 968, 1268, 1647) and harmonic-analysis-fourier (lines 583, 593, 596). `real-analysis.html` does not use indicators in body text. Consistent — no drift. The widget readouts (lines 405, 1646, 1657, 1666, 1673, 1678) display `𝟙_[a,b]` as a Unicode glyph rather than `\mathbf{1}_{[a,b]}`, which is fine inside `<text>`/JS layers but makes the in-prose vs in-readout symbol mismatch (a la category-theory.md's `\mathrm{id}_A` vs `id_A` finding) — cosmetic.
- `m`, `m^*`, `\mathcal{M}` for Lebesgue measure / outer measure / Lebesgue σ-algebra are used in §3–§4 (lines 469, 564, 567, 611). Consistent; matches `real-analysis.html#lebesgue-differentiation` (line 2675) which uses the same notational palette. Good.
- `\hat f` in §14's Fourier-paragraph (line 1849) matches `harmonic-analysis-fourier.html` exactly. No drift.
- `\sigma$-algebra` rendering: lowercase `\sigma` rather than a macro is used everywhere (line 421, 432, etc.) including in TOC (line 247) — consistent with how harmonic-analysis-fourier writes `\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^n)`. No issue.

### Undefined jargon
- §1, line 274 mentions **"absolutely integrable"** in passing ("Even such a 'nice' function as $\tfrac{\sin x}{x}$ fails to be *absolutely* integrable") before §6 line 803 defines `f \in L^1` via $\int|f| < \infty$. First-time readers see the term italicized for emphasis with no prior definition. Inline gloss recommended: "(integrable in the sense $\int|f| < \infty$, defined in §6)".
- §1 line 281 introduces **"Radon measure"** in the desiderata note without definition; it then appears again at §4 line 564 ("the unique translation-invariant Radon measure on $\mathbb{R}$") still without inline gloss. The Riesz section (§12) implicitly characterises Radon measures via the regularity bullet list at lines 1585–1588 but the term "Radon" never gets a `<strong>` or definition anchor. Adding a parenthetical in §4 ("Radon = locally finite + inner-regular on opens, the topic of §12") closes the loop.
- §2, line 432 says **"Borel $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{B}(X)$"** is generated by the open sets, but this presumes a topological structure on $X$ that the section premise doesn't yet supply. The cross-page callback at lines 438–446 points to point-set-topology, which is the right move; just consider one prose sentence ("…on a topological space, this yields…") to flag the implicit hypothesis switch.
- §11, line 1483 introduces **"mutually singular"** with $\nu\perp\mu$ as a definition, then line 1489 invokes the **"Cantor–Lebesgue measure on $C\subset[0,1]$"** as an example with no callback. The Cantor *set* is built earlier in §4 (line 590) but the Cantor *measure* (the singular measure with the Cantor function as distribution) is not constructed anywhere on the page. A one-sentence definition or a forward-pointer to where it lives (`real-analysis.html#absolute-continuity`, which builds the Cantor function explicitly) would help.
- §12 line 1593 invokes **"Urysohn's lemma"** parenthetically with no callback. Cosmetic — Urysohn is a point-set-topology staple — but the page has callbacks to point-set-topology elsewhere; one more in §12 would be consistent.
- §14, line 1853 in the Harmonic analysis paragraph: **"BMO"**, **"Littlewood–Paley theory"**, **"Calderón–Zygmund"**, **"Hardy spaces"** all dropped in a single sentence without expansion. This is an outro/connections section so the brevity is justified, but BMO and Littlewood–Paley are never expanded as acronyms anywhere on the page.

### Tone mismatches
- Tone is consistent with the house style (peer pages and `category-theory.html`) throughout. Two micro-spots that lean drier than the rest:
  - §10's **"Operational protocol"** paragraph at line 1373 ("Before swapping order of integration on a signed $f$, compute $\int\int|f|$ in *either* order…") is the strongest piece of practical "how to use this" prose on the page — `harmonic-analysis-fourier.html` has nothing equivalent and the page would benefit from one or two more such pragmatic notes (e.g. in §11, "How to recognize when Radon-Nikodym applies in practice", or in §8 "When do you actually use Hölder vs Minkowski"). This is a positive observation: more of this voice please, not a complaint.
  - §11 line 1491's **"Proof idea (Hilbert-space route)"** paragraph is excellent and uses the same conversational mode as `harmonic-analysis-fourier.html#schwartz`'s proof sketch. By contrast, §8's **"Hölder, Minkowski"** subsection (lines 1113–1122) is pure-textbook ("Proof: the Young inequality $ab\le a^p/p + b^q/q$ (follows from the convexity of $\exp$)…") with no "why these and not other inequalities" framing. A short why-care sentence would lift it.
  - §13 line 1717's "Applying Carathéodory's criterion to $\mathcal{H}^s$ produces a $\sigma$-algebra…" is bare-bones for a section-opening definition. Both peers (`harmonic-analysis-fourier.html#fourier-series` line 271 and `real-analysis.html#real-numbers` line 275) open with a motivating "what does this property buy us" sentence before the formalism — §13 jumps straight into the inf.

### Missing worked examples
- **§2 "$\sigma$-algebras and measures" has no widget.** Verified: lines 420–462 contain definitions, the cross-page callback aside, the backlinks aside, and a quiz placeholder — but no `<div class="widget">`. This is the highest-priority finding. The section sets up the entire page's vocabulary (σ-algebra, generated σ-algebra, Borel, countable additivity) and is the *only* section without an interactive demo. A small widget — e.g. "click sets to add to your collection, watch the closure under complements/countable unions colour them in"; or "drag a generator family $\mathcal{E}$, watch $\sigma(\mathcal{E})$ light up the closure" — would massively strengthen the foundation. Peer comparison: `harmonic-analysis-fourier.html` has a widget for every numbered section (1–8); `real-analysis.html#real-numbers` (line 292's `w-sqrt2` widget) demonstrates the abstract notion "completeness" with rationals approaching $\sqrt 2$ — exactly the kind of "abstract definition + physical handle" the §2 prose calls for.
- §8 ($L^p$): single widget (the unit-ball gallery, line 1148). The Hölder-inequality proof and the Riesz–Fischer proof sketch both name techniques that would benefit from a small toy (e.g. a "Cauchy sequence converges via subsequence" stepper, or a "two-norm balance" widget showing $\int|fg|$ vs $\|f\|_p\|g\|_q$ as $p$ varies). Low priority — the unit-ball widget is a very good single representative.
- §10 (Fubini): has the iteration-strip widget (line 1375) but the **continuous Fubini-failure example** at line 1369–1371 ($f(x,y) = (x^2-y^2)/(x^2+y^2)^2$ giving $\pi/4$ vs $-\pi/4$) is asserted, not demonstrated. A small "compute both iterated integrals numerically, watch them disagree" widget would elevate this from claim to demonstration. Peer: `real-analysis.html#absolute-continuity` line 2273's `w-cantor` widget shows a slider that *forces* the reader to verify the AC failure interactively — same pedagogical move would fit §10 perfectly.
- §11 has the density widget but the **Lebesgue decomposition** ($\nu = \nu_{ac} + \nu_s$) is named at line 1489 and never visualised. A widget that lets the reader add a Dirac mass (or a Cantor singular component) onto the density and watches the absolutely-continuous + singular split would close the loop.

### KaTeX macros / formatting
- All KaTeX macros used are either standard or in the shared `\Spec / \Gal / \Hom / \tr / \ad / \ind` macros block (lines 38–43) — no undeclared local macros. Identical block to both peer pages.
- `\operatorname` is **not used at all** in `measure-theory.html`. Operators that *would* benefit: `\liminf`, `\limsup` (KaTeX has these built-in — used correctly at lines 951, 953), `\sup`, `\inf` (built-in, used correctly), `\dim_H` (line 1719 — uses `\dim_H` raw; this works because `\dim` is built-in but `_H` then attaches as a subscript without `\operatorname` framing), `\mathrm{diam}` (line 1714 — should be `\operatorname{diam}` for proper spacing on either side; cosmetic). Compare: `harmonic-analysis-fourier.html` line 690 uses `\mathrm{id}` (canonical decoration), line 701 `\mathrm{p.v.}`, `\mathrm{sgn}`. The `\mathrm` vs `\operatorname` split here matches `category-theory.md`'s observation: `\mathrm` for fixed semantic decorations (`id`, `op`, `p.v.`, `sgn`), `\operatorname` for genuine operators with arguments (`Hom`, `Spec`, and ideally `diam`, `Lan`, etc.).
- `\,` thin-space discipline before `d\mu`/`dx` is mostly consistent (lines 790, 796, 804, 944, 951, 958, 1106, 1133, 1356, 1486, 1567 all use `\,d…`) but the §1 prose at line 268 writes `$\int_a^b f$` (no `\,` because no measure is named), and §6's range-slice display at line 798 has `2^{-n} \le f(x) < (k+1)\,2^{-n}` — the `\,` between $(k+1)$ and $2^{-n}$ is fine spacing. No drift, just noting the page is careful.
- `\colon` for typed-arrow colons (function signatures `f\colon X \to Y`) is used consistently (lines 268, 423, 467, 564, 618, etc.). Matches both peers.
- `\sqcup` for disjoint union (lines 435, 607, 611, 1268, 1481, 1483) is used consistently and correctly. Good.
- Helper-block hygiene: top-of-body `<script>` at lines 187–235 is byte-identical to `category-theory.html`'s helper block (`$`, `$$`, `SVG`, `ensureArrow`, `drawArrow`, `drawNode`). No deviations. Widget chrome (`.widget`, `.hd`, `.ttl`, `.hint`, `.row`, `.readout`, `.note`, `.ok`, `.bad`) is used consistently across all 11 widgets. No ad-hoc classes.
- One micro-formatting nit: §1 line 281 `(iv) integration is well-defined on $[0,\infty]\cup\mathbb{R}$ and on abstract measure spaces` — the `[0,\infty]\cup\mathbb{R}` reads oddly (the union is just $\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}$ or $[-\infty,\infty]$). Probably a typo for `$[0,\infty]$ and on $\mathbb{R}$` or `$\overline{\mathbb{R}}$`. Worth a sentence-level review of (iv).
