Half-integral weight forms

A controlled mirror of classical modular forms, accessed through theta. Half-integral weight needs a metaplectic double cover; the Shimura lift packages it back as integral weight; Kohnen's plus space picks out a canonical preimage; and Waldspurger turns Fourier coefficients into central $L$-values — culminating in Tunnell's polynomial-time test for the congruent number problem.

1. The metaplectic group and the modular cocycle

Modular forms of integer weight $k$ transform by

$f(\gamma\tau) = (c\tau+d)^k\,f(\tau), \qquad \gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}).$

What if you want $k$ to be a half-integer like $\tfrac12$? You need a square root of $c\tau+d$. But the square root has two branches, and as $\gamma$ varies the branch choice does not glue together consistently — the cocycle relation

$\sqrt{c_{12}\tau+d_{12}} \;\stackrel{?}{=}\; \sqrt{c_1(\gamma_2\tau)+d_1}\;\sqrt{c_2\tau+d_2}$

fails by a sign. The fix: pass to a double cover $\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})}$ — the metaplectic group — whose elements are pairs $(\gamma,\,\varepsilon)$ with $\varepsilon\in\{+1,-1\}$ a chosen sign of the square root. The composition law builds in the missing sign; the cocycle becomes genuine on the cover.

The theta multiplier. Rather than wrestle with abstract square roots, Shimura defined a concrete cocycle by ratio: $$j(\gamma,\tau) \;=\; \frac{\theta(\gamma\tau)}{\theta(\tau)}, \qquad \theta(\tau)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}} q^{n^2},\;\; q=e^{2\pi i\tau}.$$ This $j(\gamma,\tau)$ is well-defined as a function of $\tau$ for $\gamma\in\Gamma_0(4)$, and behaves like a square root of $c\tau+d$ up to an explicit eighth root of unity. A half-integer-weight form of weight $k+\tfrac12$ on $\Gamma_0(4N)$ is then a holomorphic function $f\colon \mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{C}$ satisfying $$f(\gamma\tau) \;=\; \chi(\gamma)\, j(\gamma,\tau)^{2k+1}\, f(\tau)$$ for all $\gamma$ in the level subgroup, plus growth conditions at cusps.

Why level $\Gamma_0(4)$?

The theta function $\theta(\tau)$ does not transform nicely under all of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ — it transforms cleanly only under matrices whose lower-left entry $c$ is divisible by $4$. (Equivalently: under $\Gamma_0(4)$.) Below level $4$, branch ambiguities in $\sqrt{c\tau+d}$ propagate sign or fourth-root inconsistencies that no honest function can absorb. So $\Gamma_0(4)$ is the canonical home of weight-$\tfrac12$ modular forms.

2. Theta functions as the prototype

The Jacobi theta series

$\theta(\tau) \;=\; \sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}} q^{n^2}, \qquad q = e^{2\pi i \tau},$

is the canonical weight-$\tfrac12$ modular form on $\Gamma_0(4)$. It transforms by the theta multiplier $j(\gamma,\tau)$ defined above; in fact $j(\gamma,\tau)$ is $\theta(\gamma\tau)/\theta(\tau)$.

The pedagogical magic of $\theta$ is that taking powers $\theta^k$ gives weight-$k/2$ modular forms whose Fourier coefficients are the sums-of-squares counts:

$\theta(\tau)^k \;=\; \sum_{n\ge 0} r_k(n)\, q^n, \qquad r_k(n) = \#\{(a_1,\ldots,a_k)\in\mathbb{Z}^k : a_1^2+\cdots+a_k^2 = n\}.$

Classical formulas, from modular-form spaces of low dimension

Because the spaces $M_{k/2}(\Gamma_0(4))$ are finite-dimensional and explicit, $\theta^k$ can be matched against an Eisenstein basis to extract closed-form $r_k(n)$ formulas:

  • $k=2$ (Jacobi-Gauss): $r_2(n) = 4(d_1(n) - d_3(n))$, where $d_j(n)$ counts divisors of $n$ congruent to $j\pmod 4$.
  • $k=4$ (Jacobi): $r_4(n) = 8\sigma(n) - 32\sigma(n/4)$, where $\sigma(n) = \sum_{d\mid n} d$ and $\sigma(n/4) = 0$ if $4\nmid n$.
  • $k=8$: $\theta^8$ is a weight-$4$ form on $\Gamma_0(4)$; matched against $E_4$ and twists, one finds $r_8(n) = 16\sum_{d\mid n}(-1)^{n-d}\,d^3$.

3. The Shimura lift

So far the half-integral world looks like an unwieldy curiosity: irregular factor of $\sqrt{c\tau+d}$, level $\Gamma_0(4N)$ forced on you, character squared everywhere. Shimura (1973) made it tractable by constructing a lift from this strange world back to the familiar integer-weight world.

Shimura's theorem (1973). Let $f$ be a Hecke eigenform of weight $k+\tfrac12$ on $\Gamma_0(4N)$ with character $\chi$. There exists an integer-weight Hecke eigenform $F$ of weight $2k$ on $\Gamma_0(2N)$ with character $\chi^2$ such that the eigenvalues match: at every prime $p\nmid 4N$, the Hecke eigenvalue $a_F(p)$ of $F$ at $p$ is determined by the Hecke eigenvalue $a_f(p^2)$ of $f$ at $p^2$ via $$\boxed{\;\;a_F(p) \;=\; a_f(p^2) \;+\; \chi(p)\,p^{k-1}.\;\;}$$

Two surprises packaged into one formula:

  • The weight doubles. Half-integer $k+\tfrac12$ goes to integer $2k$ — exactly twice the "fractional part-free" weight. The level halves: $\Gamma_0(4N) \to \Gamma_0(2N)$.
  • The Hecke side squares. On half-integral forms, Hecke operators are most naturally indexed by $T_{p^2}$ (acting at $p^2$, not $p$). Squaring on Hecke indices matches doubling on weights.

The Shimura lift is a functor, not a coincidence: it commutes with all Hecke operators, takes eigenforms to eigenforms, and the integer-weight image $F$ has all the modular-form structure (Petersson product, Atkin-Lehner involutions, $L$-function) that you already know. The half-integral side becomes a controlled mirror.

4. Kohnen's plus space

Shimura's lift packages half-integral data as integer data. But the lift is not bijective on the nose — many half-integral eigenforms share a Shimura image. Kohnen (1980-82) found a canonical section: a distinguished subspace on which Shimura becomes an honest isomorphism.

Kohnen's plus space. Define $$S^+_{k+1/2}(\Gamma_0(4)) \;=\; \bigl\{ f \in S_{k+1/2}(\Gamma_0(4)) \,:\, a_f(n) = 0 \text{ unless } (-1)^k n \equiv 0\text{ or }1 \pmod 4 \bigr\}.$$ This is the subspace whose nonzero Fourier coefficients are concentrated on $n$ for which $(-1)^k n$ is a discriminant of a quadratic field (or a perfect square / zero).

The condition looks ad hoc, but it is exactly the condition needed to align with discriminants of binary quadratic forms — the inputs to Waldspurger's formula in the next section.

Kohnen-Zagier (1981). The Shimura correspondence restricts to a Hecke-equivariant isomorphism $$S^+_{k+1/2}(\Gamma_0(4)) \;\xrightarrow{\;\;\sim\;\;}\; S_{2k}(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})).$$ Every level-1 newform of weight $2k$ has a unique (up to scalar) preimage in the plus space.

So the dictionary is:

weight $2k$ on $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$↔weight $k+\tfrac12$ on $\Gamma_0(4)$, plus condition
$\Delta$, weight 12↔a unique cusp form in $S^+_{13/2}(\Gamma_0(4))$
$E_4$ in $M_4$, weight 4 (Eisenstein, not cuspidal)↔the Cohen-Eisenstein series $\mathcal{H}_{5/2} \in M^+_{5/2}(\Gamma_0(4))$ (Kohnen-Zagier itself is cuspidal $S^+ \cong S_{2k}$; the Eisenstein partners extend the correspondence to the full $M^+$ via Cohen's series)
weight 16, 18, 20: 1-dim each↔weight 17/2, 19/2, 21/2 plus space: 1-dim each

The coefficient filter is the geometric content. Discriminants of imaginary (or real) quadratic fields $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})$ satisfy exactly the congruence $D \equiv 0$ or $1\pmod 4$. The plus-space condition says: only Fourier coefficients indexed by genuine quadratic discriminants matter. This sets up Waldspurger.

5. Waldspurger's formula

The Shimura lift gives an integer-weight twin $F$ for every half-integral eigenform $f$. Waldspurger (1981) added the breakthrough: the Fourier coefficients of $f$ are not just abstract data — they encode central $L$-values of $F$.

Waldspurger's formula (in Kohnen-Zagier form). Let $f \in S^+_{k+1/2}(\Gamma_0(4))$ be a Hecke eigenform corresponding under Shimura to $F \in S_{2k}(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}))$. For $D$ a fundamental discriminant with $(-1)^k D > 0$, $$\bigl| a_f(|D|) \bigr|^2 \;=\; C_{f,F} \cdot |D|^{k-1/2} \cdot L\bigl(F \otimes \chi_D,\, k\bigr),$$ where $\chi_D$ is the quadratic character of conductor $|D|$, $L(F\otimes\chi_D, s)$ is the twisted $L$-function, and $C_{f,F}$ is an explicit constant involving Petersson norms. The central value is at $s = k$ — the centre of the functional equation for the weight-$2k$ form $F$.

Read this both directions:

  • Forward (analytic to combinatorial). $L$-values are hard to compute analytically — the Dirichlet series $\sum a_F(n)\chi_D(n)/n^s$ does not converge at $s=k$, the centre. Waldspurger says: take a Fourier coefficient (a finite combinatorial count) and square it. That gives the central $L$-value modulo an explicit normalization.
  • Backward (combinatorial to analytic). The integers $a_f(|D|)$ for varying $D$ are constrained by a non-trivial equality with $L$-values — the coefficients themselves can be negative (the sign depends on a choice of normalization), but their squares $|a_f(|D|)|^2$ are non-negative, matching the conjectural non-negativity of the central $L$-values; they obey distribution laws inherited from the analytic theory, and their non-vanishing is governed by the central $L$-values.

Why squared?

The Fourier coefficient $a_f(|D|)$ depends on a choice of normalization (a sign), but the central value $L(F\otimes\chi_D, k)$ is intrinsic — and conjecturally non-negative. The square $|a_f(|D|)|^2$ is the unsigned, normalization-free invariant.

6. Application: Tunnell's theorem and the congruent number problem

The congruent number problem asks: for which positive integers $n$ does there exist a right triangle with rational sides whose area equals $n$? The first few congruent numbers are $5, 6, 7, 13, 14, 15, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24, \ldots$. The non-congruent ones are $1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 16, 17, 18, 19, \ldots$. There is no obvious pattern. The problem dates to the 10th century (Arab manuscripts, then Fibonacci); the elliptic-curve formulation $E_n: y^2 = x^3 - n^2 x$ is essentially classical, and Mordell's 1922 finite-generation theorem makes the rank a meaningful invariant.

The translation: $n$ is congruent iff the elliptic curve

$E_n \;:\; y^2 \;=\; x^3 - n^2 x$

has positive Mordell-Weil rank over $\mathbb{Q}$. (Each rational point with $x\ne 0, \pm n$ produces a rational right triangle of area $n$.)

BSD predicts: $E_n$ has positive rank iff $L(E_n, 1) = 0$. So congruent number theory becomes a question about central $L$-values. Waldspurger's formula then turns it into a question about Fourier coefficients of an explicit half-integral weight form.

Tunnell's theorem (1983). Let $n$ be an odd squarefree positive integer. Define $$A(n) = \#\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{Z}^3 : 2x^2+y^2+32z^2 = n\}, \qquad B(n) = \#\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{Z}^3 : 2x^2+y^2+8z^2 = n\}.$$ Then $L(E_n, 1) \ne 0$ iff $B(n) \ne 2A(n)$.

By BSD, this would imply: $n$ is non-congruent iff $B(n) \ne 2A(n)$. There is an analogous statement for even squarefree $n$ (with different ternary forms). One direction (vanishing $\Rightarrow$ nonzero $A(n)/B(n)$ identity, i.e. positive rank $\Rightarrow$ Tunnell sums match) is unconditional via Waldspurger; the converse rests on BSD for $E_n$.

Tunnell's test runs in polynomial time in $\log n$: the ternary forms $A(n)$ and $B(n)$ are finite sums whose terms can be enumerated quickly. Modulo BSD, this is the cleanest known algorithm for the millennium-old congruent number problem — and the cleanest example of Waldspurger's formula at work. The half-integral world is not a curiosity; it is the engine.

Connections

Half-integral weight forms are still actively researched: Goldfeld's effective lower bounds for class numbers go through them; non-vanishing of central twisted $L$-values (Bump-Friedberg-Hoffstein, Murty-Murty) is partially controlled by Waldspurger; and the higher-rank generalization (Shimura's lift to $\mathrm{Sp}_{2k}$) and the geometric Langlands counterpart are open questions. The plus space's role inside the larger automorphic-forms-on-the-metaplectic-cover story remains a frontier.

  • Theta functions — sums of squares from $\theta^k$ — the elementary entry point that this page recasts as the inaugural specimen of half-integral weight.
  • Modular forms — definition — the integer-weight sibling that Shimura's lift maps into.
  • Hecke operators — eigenforms and Euler factors — the Hecke compatibility is what makes the Shimura correspondence meaningful.
  • L-functions — special values and arithmetic — Waldspurger's formula realizes central $L$-values as squared Fourier coefficients.
  • Birch & Swinnerton-Dyer — rank equality — Tunnell's theorem is conditional on BSD for $E_n: y^2 = x^3 - n^2 x$.
  • Automorphic forms (adelic) — Whittaker newforms — the modern adelic framework that subsumes the classical metaplectic picture.
