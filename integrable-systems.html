Integrable systems

Exactly solvable nonlinearity: Liouville integrability and action-angle tori, KdV solitons, Lax pairs and isospectral flow, the inverse scattering transform, the Toda lattice, and the soliton zoo.

1. Liouville integrability and action-angle variables

Most Hamiltonian systems are chaotic. A rare and beautiful exception is an integrable system: one with $n$ degrees of freedom and $n$ independent conserved quantities $I_1,\dots,I_n$ that are in involution, $\{I_i,I_j\}=0$. The Liouville–Arnold theorem says the common level sets are then $n$-dimensional tori, and there are action-angle coordinates $(I,\theta)$ in which the dynamics is utterly simple: $\dot I=0$, $\dot\theta=\omega(I)$ — straight-line winding on a torus.

The pendulum ($n=1$) already shows the picture: its energy $H=\tfrac12 p^2-\cos\theta$ is the single integral, and the phase plane is foliated by its level curves — closed libration orbits inside, unbounded rotation orbits outside, separated by the separatrix. Each closed curve is a 1-torus carrying periodic motion.

2. The KdV equation and solitons

Shallow water waves obey the Korteweg–de Vries equation

$$u_t+6uu_x+u_{xxx}=0.$$

The term $6uu_x$ steepens a wave (as in a breaking shock); the term $u_{xxx}$ disperses it. KdV is the exact balance, and it supports solitons $u=2\kappa^2\operatorname{sech}^2\!\big(\kappa(x-4\kappa^2t-x_0)\big)$ — localized humps whose speed $4\kappa^2$ grows with amplitude $2\kappa^2$. In 1965 Zabusky and Kruskal found numerically that a tall soliton overtaking a short one passes straight through, both surviving intact up to a phase shift — particle-like behaviour that named the soliton and launched the field.

3. Lax pairs and infinitely many conservation laws

Why is KdV solvable? Lax’s insight: rewrite it as an isospectral deformation of a linear operator,

$$\dot L=[P,L]\quad\Longrightarrow\quad L(t)=U(t)\,L(0)\,U(t)^{-1},$$

a similarity transformation that preserves the spectrum of $L$. Every eigenvalue is thus a conserved quantity. For KdV the operator is precisely the Schrödinger operator $L=-\partial_x^2-u(x,t)$, with $P=-4\partial_x^3-6u\partial_x-3u_x$; expanding $\dot L=[P,L]$ regenerates the KdV equation and, through the spectral data, an infinite tower of conservation laws. 4. The inverse scattering transform

The inverse scattering transform (IST) is a nonlinear Fourier transform. Read the initial profile $u(x,0)$ as the potential of the Schrödinger operator $-\partial_x^2-u$ and compute its scattering data: a reflection coefficient $r(k)$ on the continuous spectrum plus discrete eigenvalues $-\kappa_n^2$ (bound states). The magic is that under KdV this data evolves linearly and explicitly; reconstructing $u(x,t)$ is then a (linear) Gelfand–Levitan–Marchenko integral equation.

The dictionary is exact: each bound state is one soliton of amplitude $2\kappa_n^2$, and a reflectionless potential ($r\equiv0$) is a pure $N$-soliton solution. The Pöschl–Teller wells $-N(N+1)\operatorname{sech}^2x$ are exactly reflectionless, with $N$ bound states at $E=-1,-4,\dots,-N^2$.

5. The Toda lattice and finite-dimensional integrability

Integrability is not only a PDE phenomenon. The Toda lattice — point masses on a line coupled by exponential springs $V(r)=e^{-r}+r-1$ — is the discrete cousin of KdV and carries its own compression solitons. In Flaschka’s change of variables it becomes a Lax flow $\dot L=[B,L]$ with $L$ a symmetric tridiagonal (Jacobi) matrix, so its eigenvalues are conserved and the finite lattice is Liouville integrable.

The payoff reaches far: sampling this isospectral tridiagonal flow at integer times reproduces the QR algorithm for eigenvalues, and the same structures appear in random matrix theory. Integrable dynamics and numerical linear algebra are, surprisingly, the same story.

6. The soliton zoo and integrable hierarchies

KdV has many integrable siblings, all solved by the Lax/IST machinery: the focusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation (bright envelope solitons in optics and deep water), sine-Gordon (topological kinks and oscillating breathers), and modified KdV. Each shares the defining features — a Lax pair, infinitely many conservation laws, and clean multi-soliton scattering.

Moreover each equation is just one rung of an infinite hierarchy of commuting flows, generated by a bi-Hamiltonian (Magri) structure: two compatible Poisson brackets whose recursion operator manufactures the higher symmetries and conserved quantities. Integrability, once found, comes in towers.

Connections

Integrable systems are a thin, exactly-solvable thread running through analysis, geometry, and algebra. The frontier is to recognize integrability when it hides — in random matrices, in the KPZ universality class, in supersymmetric gauge theory and the Bethe ansatz — and to understand the near-integrable regime where KAM theory governs the slow onset of chaos.
