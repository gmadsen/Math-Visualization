Quantum field theory

The capstone of the relativistic-physics arc: promote $\phi(x)$ and $\psi(x)$ to operator-valued distributions, organize amplitudes by Feynman diagrams, run couplings with the renormalization group, and gauge the symmetries — the framework that produces every Standard-Model prediction.

1. From classical fields to operator-valued distributions

Klein-Gordon's $j^0$ failed as a probability density (negative on superpositions of positive- and negative-energy modes; see the KG current widget). Dirac's first-order equation fixed the sign of $j^0$ but introduced a filled negative-energy sea as the heuristic for antiparticles. Quantum field theory dissolves both problems by promoting $\phi(x)$ and $\psi(x)$ from c-number functions to operator-valued distributions on a Fock space, with the negative-energy branch reinterpreted as positive-energy antiparticles from the start.

For a free real scalar, impose the equal-time canonical commutation relation

$$[\phi(t,\vec x),\,\pi(t,\vec y)] \;=\; i\hbar\,\delta^3(\vec x - \vec y),\qquad \pi = \dot\phi,$$

and decompose into momentum modes:

$$\phi(x) \;=\; \int\!\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3 2E_p}\Bigl[a_p\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + a_p^\dagger\,e^{+ip\cdot x}\Bigr],\qquad E_p = \sqrt{|\vec p|^2 + m^2}.$$

The coefficients satisfy $[a_p, a_q^\dagger] = (2\pi)^3 2E_p\,\delta^3(\vec p - \vec q)$ — the algebra of an infinite collection of harmonic oscillators, one per momentum mode. Define the vacuum $|0\rangle$ by $a_p|0\rangle = 0$ for all $p$; the $n$-particle states $a_{p_1}^\dagger\cdots a_{p_n}^\dagger|0\rangle$ span Fock space. The Hamiltonian is $H = \int (d^3p/(2\pi)^3 2E_p)\,E_p\,a_p^\dagger a_p$ after normal-ordering ($:O:$ — re-arranging all $a^\dagger$ to the left of all $a$, dropping the formally-infinite zero-point c-number that results), manifestly bounded below.

For a Dirac fermion, the same construction with anticommutators $\{b_p,b_q^\dagger\} = \delta(\vec p-\vec q)$ produces creation operators $b_p^\dagger$ for electrons and a separate set $d_p^\dagger$ for positrons — antiparticles are positive-energy excitations of a different creation operator, not holes in a sea. The sea was a useful pre-QFT crutch; QFT replaces it with the operator algebra and the antiparticle prediction stands.

2. Fock space and multi-particle states

The Hilbert space of a free field is the Fock space

$$\mathcal{F} \;=\; \bigoplus_{n\ge 0}\mathcal{H}^{(n)},\qquad \mathcal{H}^{(0)} = \mathbb{C}\cdot|0\rangle,\quad \mathcal{H}^{(1)} = \mathcal{H}_1,$$

and for each $n\ge 2$, $\mathcal{H}^{(n)} = \mathrm{Sym}^n\mathcal{H}_1$ (bosons) or $\mathcal{H}^{(n)} = \Lambda^n\mathcal{H}_1$ (fermions). The (anti)symmetrization is automatic — it falls out of the (anti)commutation algebra of the creation operators:

$$a_p^\dagger a_q^\dagger|0\rangle = +a_q^\dagger a_p^\dagger|0\rangle\ \text{(boson)},\qquad b_p^\dagger b_q^\dagger|0\rangle = -b_q^\dagger b_p^\dagger|0\rangle\ \text{(fermion)}.$$

Setting $p=q$ in the fermion case gives $b_p^\dagger b_p^\dagger|0\rangle = 0$ — the Pauli exclusion principle as a structural identity, not an axiom. The spin-statistics theorem (Pauli 1940) shows that integer spin must use commutators and half-integer spin must use anticommutators: any other choice is incompatible with Lorentz invariance and microcausality $[\mathcal{O}(x),\mathcal{O}(y)]=0$ for spacelike $(x-y)^2 > 0$.

The number operator $\hat N = \int (d^3p/(2\pi)^3 2E_p)\,a_p^\dagger a_p$ satisfies $\hat N|n_{p_1},\ldots,n_{p_k}\rangle = (n_{p_1}+\cdots+n_{p_k})|\cdots\rangle$ — Fock states are simultaneous eigenstates of $\hat N$ and $H$. This is the field-theoretic upgrade of the harmonic-oscillator ladder: each momentum slot has its own ladder, and the global state is a tensor of single-mode kets.

3. The Feynman path integral

An equivalent — and for many purposes more powerful — formulation of QFT is Feynman's path integral. The transition amplitude between field configurations $\phi_i$ at time $t_i$ and $\phi_f$ at time $t_f$ is

$$\langle\phi_f, t_f \mid \phi_i, t_i\rangle \;=\; \int_{\phi(t_i)=\phi_i}^{\phi(t_f)=\phi_f}\!\mathcal{D}\phi\;\;\exp\!\Bigl(\,\frac{i}{\hbar}S[\phi]\,\Bigr),\qquad S[\phi] = \int d^4x\,\mathcal{L}(\phi,\partial\phi).$$

The integral is over every field history with the given boundary conditions — classical solutions, near-misses, wildly non-classical zigzags. Each contributes the phase $e^{iS/\hbar}$.

The classical limit is stationary phase: as $\hbar\to 0$, the phase $S/\hbar$ oscillates rapidly except near configurations where $\delta S/\delta\phi = 0$ — exactly the Euler-Lagrange equation. Off-classical contributions cancel by destructive interference; the dominant configurations are stationary, not zero-action. Quantum corrections are the leading deviation: expand $S[\phi_{\mathrm{cl}} + \eta]$ to quadratic order in the fluctuation $\eta$, and the resulting Gaussian integral gives the one-loop result.

Wick rotation $t \to -i\tau$ maps $iS \to -S_E$ with $S_E > 0$; the path integral becomes

$$Z_E \;=\; \int\mathcal{D}\phi\;e^{-S_E[\phi]/\hbar}$$

— a Boltzmann sum, identical in form to a statistical-mechanics partition function. Euclidean QFT in $d$ dimensions and classical statistical mechanics in $d$ dimensions are the same formalism: phase transitions in spin systems map to RG flows of the dual QFT, lattice regularizations compute thermodynamic quantities, and Monte Carlo methods cross over freely. The Lorentzian theory is recovered by analytic continuation back to real time.

4. Perturbation theory and Feynman diagrams

Convention. Throughout this page we work in mostly-minus signature $\eta_{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}(+1,-1,-1,-1)$, matching the Dirac equation page; an on-shell timelike 4-momentum carries $p^2 = E^2 - |\vec p|^2 = m^2 > 0$, which is what makes the Feynman propagator $i/(p^2 - m^2 + i\epsilon)$ have its pole at the physical mass. Special relativity and Klein-Gordon use the opposite mostly-plus convention; all amplitudes are convention-independent, but watch the sign when comparing $p^2-m^2$ across pages.

For an interacting theory $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_0 + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{int}}$, the path integral exponential factors as $e^{iS_0/\hbar}\,e^{iS_{\mathrm{int}}/\hbar}$ and one expands the second factor in powers of the coupling. Wick's theorem reduces time-ordered products of free fields to sums over pairwise contractions, and each surviving term is a Feynman diagram.

The dictionary, in the canonical QED vocabulary:

  • Vertices come from $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{int}}$. The QED vertex is $-ie\gamma^\mu$ — one fermion line in, one fermion line out, one photon line.
  • Internal lines are propagators: $i/(p^2-m^2+i\epsilon)$ for a scalar, $i(\not{p}+m)/(p^2-m^2+i\epsilon)$ for a Dirac fermion, $-i\eta^{\mu\nu}/(p^2+i\epsilon)$ for a Feynman-gauge photon. Internal lines are off-shell ($p^2\ne m^2$) — they are virtual particles, not observable on their own.
  • External lines are on-shell wave functions $u(p),v(p),\epsilon^\mu(p)$ from the LSZ reduction formula (Lehmann-Symanzik-Zimmermann: the amputation rule that converts time-ordered correlation functions into physical scattering amplitudes by stripping external propagators and projecting onto on-shell states), with $p^2 = m^2$.
  • Closed loops introduce a momentum integral $\int d^4k/(2\pi)^4$ — this is where the famous UV divergences live.

The textbook calculation $e^+e^-\to\mu^+\mu^-$ at tree level gives $\sigma = 4\pi\alpha^2/(3s)$ in the limit $\sqrt s\gg m_\mu$. At $\sqrt s = 100\,\mathrm{GeV}$ the cross-section is $\sim 8.7\times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{nb} \approx 8.7\,\mathrm{pb}$ — measured to subpercent precision at LEP, and the universal benchmark for $e^+e^-$ collider analyses.

5. Renormalization and running couplings

One-loop diagrams in QED contain divergent integrals like $\int d^4k\,(k^2-m^2)^{-2}$. The cure is the renormalization program: regulate, absorb, and rewrite.

  1. Regulate. Replace $\int d^4k$ by a finite version: dimensional regularization $d = 4-\epsilon$, Pauli-Villars subtraction with heavy regulator masses, or a hard cutoff $\Lambda$.
  2. Absorb. Write the bare Lagrangian's couplings as $g_0 = g + \delta g$, $m_0^2 = m^2 + \delta m^2$, $\psi_0 = \sqrt{Z_\psi}\,\psi$, and choose the counterterms $\delta g, \delta m^2, Z_\psi - 1$ to cancel the divergent pieces of every loop integral. This is mechanical at one loop and works to all orders in renormalizable theories.
  3. Rewrite. The physical observable is a finite function of the renormalized coupling $g(\mu)$ at a sliding scale $\mu$.

The running of $g(\mu)$ with $\mu$ is captured by the renormalization-group equation

$$\mu\frac{\partial g(\mu)}{\partial\mu} \;=\; \beta(g),$$

where the sign of $\beta$ matters. In QED, $\beta_{\mathrm{QED}}(\alpha) = +(2/3\pi)\alpha^2$ at one loop, so $\alpha(\mu)$ grows with energy — measured at LEP as $\alpha(M_Z)\approx 1/128$ vs $\alpha(0)\approx 1/137$. In QCD, $\beta_{\mathrm{QCD}}(\alpha_s) = -(7/2\pi)\alpha_s^2$ (Gross-Wilczek-Politzer 1973), so $\alpha_s(\mu)$ shrinks — quarks are nearly free at high energy (asymptotic freedom) and confined at low energy.

Wilson's effective-field-theory reframing reads renormalization as integrating out high-momentum modes: a theory at scale $\Lambda$ is mapped to an effective theory at scale $\mu < \Lambda$ with redefined couplings. Renormalizability is the special case where only finitely many couplings survive in the IR; non-renormalizable theories are still meaningful effective theories below their cutoff (the Fermi theory of weak interactions, gravitons, low-energy chiral perturbation theory).

6. Quantum gauge fields and the Standard Model

Promoting a continuous symmetry from global to local is the central organizational idea of the Standard Model. If $\psi(x) \to e^{i\alpha^a(x)T^a}\psi(x)$ is to leave the Lagrangian invariant, the kinetic term must use a covariant derivative

$$D_\mu \;=\; \partial_\mu - igA_\mu^a T^a,\qquad A_\mu^a(x) \to A_\mu^a + \tfrac{1}{g}\partial_\mu\alpha^a + f^{abc}\alpha^b A_\mu^c,$$

where $T^a$ generate the gauge Lie algebra (with structure constants $f^{abc}$) and $A_\mu^a$ is the gauge field. The kinetic term for $A_\mu^a$ is the Yang-Mills action $-\tfrac14 F^a_{\mu\nu}F^{a\mu\nu}$ with $F^a_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A^a_\nu - \partial_\nu A^a_\mu + gf^{abc}A^b_\mu A^c_\nu$.

The Standard Model gauge group is

$$G_{\mathrm{SM}} \;=\; \mathrm{SU}(3)_C \times \mathrm{SU}(2)_L \times \mathrm{U}(1)_Y$$

— $\dim = 8 + 3 + 1 = 12$ gauge bosons in total: 8 gluons $G^a_\mu$ (color SU(3)), 3 weak bosons $W^1, W^2, W^3$, and the hypercharge $B$. Quantizing requires gauge fixing (the Faddeev-Popov procedure introduces ghost fields in non-Abelian gauges) and the BRST formalism (Becchi-Rouet-Stora-Tyutin: a nilpotent graded operator $Q$ with $Q^2=0$ that encodes the residual gauge freedom after gauge-fixing; physical states are the cohomology $\mathrm{ker}\,Q / \mathrm{im}\,Q$, ghosts and anti-ghosts cancel unphysical longitudinal modes) that reorganizes the residual graded symmetry.

The Higgs mechanism breaks $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y \to \mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$ via a vev $\langle\phi\rangle\ne 0$. The vev's electric charge $Q\langle\phi\rangle = 0$, so one combination of $W^3$ and $B$ — the photon $A_\mu = \sin\theta_W\,W^3_\mu + \cos\theta_W\,B_\mu$ — does not couple to it and stays exactly massless. The orthogonal combinations $Z_\mu, W^\pm_\mu$ acquire masses $M_Z = M_W/\cos\theta_W \approx 91\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $M_W \approx 80\,\mathrm{GeV}$. Renormalizability survives the spontaneous breaking ('t Hooft 1971), and every Standard-Model precision test — the LEP electroweak fits, the muon $g-2$, the Higgs discovery at the LHC — closes the loop on this mechanism.

Connections

Quantum field theory is the framework, not the endpoint. The Standard Model successfully predicts every collider observable to date, but open frontiers remain: quantum gravity (general-relativity quantization fails as a renormalizable QFT — gravitons have dimension-2 couplings, signaling effective-field-theory only); strong-CP (why is the QCD $\theta$-angle observably zero?); neutrino masses (Majorana vs Dirac, double-beta decay); the hierarchy problem ($M_H \ll M_{\mathrm{Pl}}$ requires fine-tuning); and the dark sector (no SM particle accounts for cosmological dark matter or dark energy). The conjectured Langlands-style geometric reformulations and string-theoretic UV completions continue to drive mathematical interaction, while precision-frontier measurements at the LHC, lepton colliders, and neutrino experiments tighten the noose.

  • Dirac equation — the immediate precursor; QFT promotes Dirac's $\psi$ to an operator-valued field, making antiparticles structural rather than sea-holes and giving each Dirac field a dedicated antiparticle creation operator.
  • Klein-Gordon equation — the simpler precursor; QFT cures KG's negative-probability problem by reinterpreting $j^\mu$ as a charge current and the negative-energy branch as positive-energy antiparticles.
  • Gauge theory — the classical (mathematical) version: principal bundles, connections, curvature. QFT quantizes the connection $A_\mu^a$ and reads gauge-fixing as a constraint on the path integral, with Faddeev-Popov ghosts and the BRST formalism.
  • Lie algebras — the gauge-group generators $T^a$ live in $\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)$; structure constants $f^{abc}$ control non-Abelian self-interactions of $W^a$ and gluons.
  • Representation theory — quark and lepton multiplets are irreducible representations of $G_{\mathrm{SM}}$; the labelling (color triplet, weak doublet, hypercharge) is a representation-theoretic statement, and the Higgs vev singles out the unbroken $\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$ representation that the photon couples to.
  • String theory — the natural successor capstone: replaces point-particle QFT by a 2-dimensional worldsheet QFT whose target-space limit reproduces gravity plus matter, and whose dualities (T-duality, mirror symmetry, AdS/CFT) feed back into pure mathematics.
