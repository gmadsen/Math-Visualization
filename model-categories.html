Model categories

Quillen's axiomatic homotopy theory: weak equivalences, cofibrations, fibrations and the lifting calculus that turn an abstract category into a computable homotopy category — the standard bridge from chain complexes to $\infty$-categories.

1. Quillen's model category axioms

A model structure on a category $\mathcal{M}$ is the choice of three classes of morphisms — weak equivalences $W$, fibrations $F$, cofibrations $C$ — together with the data of two functorial factorizations. Quillen's five axioms (1967) require:

  • M1 (limits/colimits): $\mathcal{M}$ has all small limits and colimits.
  • M2 (2-of-3): in any composable pair $X\xrightarrow{f}Y\xrightarrow{g}Z$, if any two of $f,g,gf$ lie in $W$, so does the third.
  • M3 (retracts): $W$, $F$, $C$ are each closed under retracts in the arrow category $\mathcal{M}^\to$.
  • M4 (factorization): every $f\colon X\to Y$ admits two functorial factorizations $$f\;=\;X\xrightarrow{\text{cof}}\;\widetilde X\;\xrightarrow{\text{triv. fib}}Y \qquad\text{and}\qquad f\;=\;X\xrightarrow{\text{triv. cof}}\;\widehat X\;\xrightarrow{\text{fib}}Y.$$ A trivial (co)fibration is one that is also a weak equivalence.
  • M5 (lifting): in every commutative square $$\begin{array}{ccc}A&\to&X\\{\scriptstyle i}\!\downarrow & & \downarrow\!{\scriptstyle p}\\ B&\to&Y\end{array}$$ with $i\in C$ and $p\in F$, a diagonal lift $B\to X$ exists provided either $i$ or $p$ is in $W$.

The choice of three classes is heavily over-determined: the classes $C$ and $F$ each determine the other through the lifting calculus (cofibrations are the maps having the left lifting property against trivial fibrations, fibrations the right against trivial cofibrations), and $W$ supplies the homotopy-theoretic content. Two of the three classes $\{W, C, F\}$ already pin the structure down.

The payoff is the homotopy category $\Ho(\mathcal{M}) = \mathcal{M}[W^{-1}]$. A priori a localization of a category at a class of morphisms can have proper-class-sized Hom-sets — the model-category axioms guarantee this never happens. Concretely, $\Hom_{\Ho(\mathcal{M})}(X,Y) = \pi(QX, RY)$, homotopy classes of maps between a cofibrant replacement of $X$ and a fibrant replacement of $Y$, where homotopy is defined via cylinder/path objects from the factorizations.

2. Standard model structures: spaces, simplicial sets, chain complexes

The axioms are abstract; their content emerges from the canonical examples. Three model structures together fix most of the standard vocabulary of homotopy theory.

Topological spaces (Quillen)

On the category $\mathbf{Top}$ of compactly generated topological spaces:

  • $W$ = weak homotopy equivalences: maps inducing isomorphisms on all $\pi_n$ for all basepoints.
  • $F$ = Serre fibrations: maps with the homotopy lifting property against the inclusions $D^n\hookrightarrow D^n\times[0,1]$.
  • $C$ = retracts of relative cell complexes: built from $\emptyset$ by iterated cell attachment $S^{n-1}\hookrightarrow D^n$.

This is the model structure presented by CW complexes — every space has a CW-approximation, which is its cofibrant replacement.

Simplicial sets (Kan–Quillen)

On the category $\mathbf{sSet} = \mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}, \mathrm{Set})$:

  • $W$ = maps $f$ such that $|f|$ is a weak homotopy equivalence in $\mathbf{Top}$.
  • $F$ = Kan fibrations: maps with the right lifting property against horn inclusions $\Lambda^n_k \hookrightarrow \Delta^n$ for all $0\le k\le n$.
  • $C$ = monomorphisms (levelwise injections).

Every simplicial set is cofibrant ($C$ = mono is permissive); the fibrant objects are exactly the Kan complexes.

Chain complexes (projective)

On the category $\mathrm{Ch}_{\ge 0}(R)$ of non-negatively graded chain complexes of $R$-modules:

  • $W$ = quasi-isomorphisms (iso on all $H_n$).
  • $F$ = chain maps that are surjective in every positive degree.
  • $C$ = chain maps that are monomorphisms in every degree with degreewise projective cokernel.

Every complex is fibrant (the terminal object $0$ is a degreewise surjection target), and cofibrant replacement of an $R$-module $M[0]$ is a projective resolution.

3. Cofibrant and fibrant replacement

The factorization axiom M4 supplies, for any object $X\in\mathcal{M}$, a canonical pair of replacements:

$$\emptyset\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{cof}\;}\;QX\;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\;X,\qquad X\;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\;RX\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{fib}\;}\;*.$$

$QX$ is cofibrant (the unique map $\emptyset\to QX$ is a cofibration), and the trivial fibration $QX\to X$ exhibits $X$ as a homotopy quotient of a more accessible cofibrant object. Dually $RX$ is fibrant, and $X\to RX$ is a trivial cofibration.

Why bother? Functors don't generally preserve weak equivalences. Their derived versions do — provided we pre-replace inputs by cofibrant (or fibrant) objects:

  • For a left Quillen functor $L$ (defined formally in §4: a left adjoint preserving cofibrations and trivial cofibrations): compute $\mathbb{L}L(X) := L(QX)$. The Ken Brown lemma says $L$ preserves weak equivalences between cofibrant objects, so $\mathbb{L}L$ is well-defined on $\Ho(\mathcal{M})$.
  • Dually, for a right Quillen functor $R$: $\mathbb{R}R(X) := R(RX)$.

The classical concretizations:

Model categoryCofibrant replacement of...Recovers
$\mathbf{Top}$ (Quillen)a space $X$a CW-approximation of $X$
$\mathbf{sSet}$ (Kan–Quillen)any simplicial set(itself — every $K$ is cofibrant)
$\mathrm{Ch}_{\ge 0}(R)$ (projective)an $R$-module $M$ in degree 0a projective resolution $P_\bullet\to M$
$\mathrm{Ch}_{\ge 0}(R)$ (injective)$M$ in degree 0(itself; fibrant replacement gives an injective resolution)

So projective resolutions, CW-approximations, and Kan completions are all instances of one machine: M4's factorization producing a cofibrant model of an arbitrary object.

4. Quillen functors and Quillen equivalences

An adjunction $L \dashv R\colon \mathcal{M}\rightleftarrows\mathcal{N}$ between model categories is a Quillen pair when $L$ preserves cofibrations and trivial cofibrations — equivalently, when $R$ preserves fibrations and trivial fibrations. The two conditions are equivalent because lifts in $\mathcal{N}$ correspond to lifts in $\mathcal{M}$ via the adjunction.

From a Quillen pair we extract total derived functors:

$$\mathbb{L}L\colon \Ho(\mathcal{M})\to\Ho(\mathcal{N}),\quad \mathbb{L}L(X) = L(QX);\qquad \mathbb{R}R\colon \Ho(\mathcal{N})\to\Ho(\mathcal{M}),\quad \mathbb{R}R(Y) = R(RY).$$

These descend through the localization because of Ken Brown's lemma: a left Quillen functor preserves weak equivalences between cofibrant objects (and dually for right). The unit and counit of $L\dashv R$ pass to a derived adjunction $\mathbb{L}L\dashv\mathbb{R}R$ on the homotopy categories.

A Quillen pair is a Quillen equivalence if the derived adjunction is an equivalence of categories — equivalently, if for every cofibrant $X\in\mathcal{M}$ and fibrant $Y\in\mathcal{N}$, a map $LX\to Y$ is a weak equivalence iff its adjoint $X\to RY$ is.

The motivating example is the geometric realization adjunction:

$$|\cdot|\;\colon\;\mathbf{sSet}\;\rightleftarrows\;\mathbf{Top}\;\colon\;\mathrm{Sing}.$$

This is a Quillen equivalence with respect to the Kan–Quillen and Quillen model structures. The unit $K\to\mathrm{Sing}\,|K|$ is a weak equivalence in $\mathbf{sSet}$ (every $K$ is cofibrant; $\mathrm{Sing}|K|$ is a Kan complex hence fibrant); the counit $|\mathrm{Sing}\,X|\to X$ is a weak homotopy equivalence (CW-approximation by simplices), but in general not a homeomorphism. Two presentations of one homotopy theory.

Other prototypes:

  • Tensor–Hom on $\mathrm{Ch}(R)$: $-\otimes_R N \dashv \Hom_R(N,-)$ derives to $\mathrm{Tor}^R(-,N)$ on the left, $\mathrm{Ext}^*_R(N,-)$ on the right.
  • Constant–colimit: $\Delta\dashv\mathrm{colim}\colon \mathcal{M}\rightleftarrows\mathcal{M}^I$ derives to $\mathrm{hocolim}_I = \mathbb{L}\mathrm{colim}$ via cofibrantly replacing the diagram.

5. Monoidal model categories and the pushout-product axiom

A model category $\mathcal{M}$ equipped with a closed symmetric monoidal product $\otimes$ is a monoidal model category when the model structure and the monoidal structure are compatible in a specific sense. The compatibility is captured by the pushout-product axiom.

For two cofibrations $i\colon A\to B$ and $j\colon C\to D$, form the universal pushout square

$$\begin{array}{ccc} A\otimes C & \xrightarrow{1\otimes j} & A\otimes D\\ {\scriptstyle i\otimes 1}\downarrow & & \downarrow\\ B\otimes C & \to & A\otimes D\sqcup_{A\otimes C}B\otimes C \end{array}$$

and the canonical pushout-product map

$$i\Box j\;\colon\; A\otimes D\sqcup_{A\otimes C}B\otimes C\;\longrightarrow\;B\otimes D.$$

The axiom: $i\Box j$ is always a cofibration, and trivial whenever either $i$ or $j$ is. Plus a unit axiom ensuring the monoidal unit $\mathbb 1$ behaves correctly under cofibrant replacement.

The pushout-product axiom is what makes $\otimes$ derive correctly to $\otimes^{\mathbb L}$ on the homotopy category. The standard examples all satisfy it:

  • $\mathbf{Top}$ (cartesian product, compactly generated): pushout-product of two relative cell complexes is again a relative cell complex.
  • $\mathbf{sSet}$ (cartesian product): the pushout-product of two monomorphisms is a monomorphism (the proof reduces to checking that the inclusion of a sub-bisimplicial set into the product is a cof).
  • $\mathrm{Ch}(R)$ for $R$ commutative (tensor): for cofibrations of degreewise-projective complexes, the pushout-product is again a cofibration of degreewise-projective complexes.

The deeper consequence — Schwede–Shipley's monoid axiom — lifts the model structure to monoid objects: $E_\infty$-rings, structured ring spectra, simplicial monoids, and dgas all arise as monoids in their respective monoidal model categories.

6. From model categories to $\infty$-categories

Why do model categories matter now? Because they are the standard tool for presenting $(\infty,1)$-categories. Modern foundations (Lurie's Higher Topos Theory) define $\infty$-categories intrinsically as quasi-categories, but constructing examples almost always passes through a model-category presentation.

Given a simplicial model category $\mathcal{M}$ (one enriched in $\mathbf{sSet}$ in a way compatible with the model structure), the underlying $(\infty,1)$-category is

$$\mathcal{M}_\infty\;:=\;N_\Delta(\mathcal{M}^{cf}),$$

the simplicial nerve of the full simplicial subcategory of cofibrant–fibrant objects. Two facts make this work:

  • If both $X, Y\in\mathcal{M}$ are cofibrant–fibrant, then the simplicial Hom $\mathrm{Map}_{\mathcal{M}}(X,Y)$ is a Kan complex — the higher coherence data of homotopies between maps $X\to Y$.
  • The simplicial nerve $N_\Delta$ converts a category enriched in Kan complexes into a quasi-category. The result has objects = cofibrant–fibrant objects of $\mathcal{M}$, $1$-simplices = maps, $2$-simplices = homotopies between composites, etc.

Three structural facts:

  1. Quillen equivalences induce $(\infty,1)$-equivalences: a Quillen equivalence $\mathcal{M}\simeq_Q\mathcal{N}$ produces an equivalence of quasi-categories $\mathcal{M}_\infty\simeq\mathcal{N}_\infty$.
  2. Dugger's theorem: every presentable $(\infty,1)$-category arises as $\mathcal{M}_\infty$ for some combinatorial simplicial model category — meaning the underlying category is locally presentable and the model structure is cofibrantly generated by a set of generating (trivial) cofibrations, with a compatible simplicial enrichment satisfying the SM7 / pushout-product axiom for the simplicial action. So model categories are not just one source of examples but an exhaustive presentation theorem.
  3. Homotopy 1-categories agree: $h(\mathcal{M}_\infty) = \Ho(\mathcal{M})$. The classical homotopy category of model-category fame is the $1$-truncation of the modern $\infty$-categorical incarnation.

The bridge runs both ways. Old computations done with model-category machinery (Bousfield localization, transferred model structures, Quillen equivalences between model structures on the same underlying category) translate to $(\infty,1)$-categorical statements (localization of $\infty$-categories, accessible reflective sub-$\infty$-categories, equivalences of presentations). Conversely, intrinsic $\infty$-categorical theorems (the adjoint functor theorem in $\infty$-form, the straightening–unstraightening equivalence) recover and clarify model-category folklore.

Connections

Model categories occupy a structural middle ground: too combinatorial to be the final word on $\infty$-category theory, but flexible enough to construct almost every $\infty$-category arising in practice. Open frontiers include the search for model structures that present specific stable $(\infty,1)$-categories (motivic, equivariant, derived algebraic geometry), and the hunt for monoidal model structures supporting subtle ring-spectrum or operadic enrichments.

  • Derived categories — $D(\mathcal{A}) = K(\mathcal{A})[\mathrm{qis}^{-1}]$ — the prototype $W^{-1}$-localization, recovered as $\Ho$ of the projective model on $\mathrm{Ch}(\mathcal{A})$.
  • Projective modules — cofibrant objects in the projective model on $\mathrm{Ch}(R)$; resolutions are cofibrant replacements.
  • Kan complexes — the fibrant objects in the Kan–Quillen model on $\mathbf{sSet}$.
  • Quasi-categories ($\infty$-categories) — the destination of the simplicial-nerve construction $\mathcal{M}^{cf}\rightsquigarrow\mathcal{M}_\infty$.
  • Quillen's Q-construction — uses the same exact-category / cofibration machinery; Waldhausen's $S_\bullet$-construction generalizes it to categories with cofibrations and weak equivalences.
